1. Hypothesis Summary  
The hypothesis posits that EGCG-rich green tea catechins selectively target cancer stem cells (CSCs) by compromising their mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation. It suggests that the selective toxicity is achieved by exploiting unique properties of CSC mitochondrial membranes and enzyme profiles. In parallel, EGCG is proposed to modulate IL-8 and PI3K/AKT signaling pathways—both of which are essential for CSC survival—which together cause severe energy depletion and metabolic stress, trigger intrinsic apoptotic pathways, and ultimately impede CSC self-renewal. The hypothesis is structured to be validated using an integrated approach that includes high‐resolution respirometry, single-cell metabolomics, mitochondrial proteomics, and targeted genomic profiling to compare mitochondrial dynamics in CSCs against those in normal cells or non-CSC tumor cells.
