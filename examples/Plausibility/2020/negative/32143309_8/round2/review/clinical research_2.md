Hypothesis Summary:
The hypothesis proposes that a nano-formulated epigallocatechin gallate (EGCG) therapy can reprogram tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs) within the tumor microenvironment. Specifically, it argues that the nano-formulation enhances EGCG’s bioavailability and targeting, enabling it to modulate the cellular redox state and chelate pro-oxidative metal ions. This modulation is expected to reduce HIF-1α stabilization and lower immunosuppressive cytokine levels, thereby shifting TAMs from a pro-tumorigenic M2 phenotype to an anti-tumorigenic M1 state. Additionally, the hypothesis asserts that the inherent anti-oncogenic properties of EGCG—such as inhibition of the PI3K/AKT pathway and the promotion of apoptosis—further support tumor suppression. In combination, these effects are believed to generate a robust anti-tumor immune response.
