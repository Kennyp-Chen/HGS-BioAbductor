# literature evidence

## "Natural and herbal compounds targeting breast cancer, a review based on cancer stem cells" (PMID: 32952942)
"Epigallocatechin gallate (EPGCG) of green tea and especially some of its analogs (analogs 4, 6) potentially activate the AMP-activated protein kinase (AMPK) in breast cancer cells. AMPK controls activities such as cell cycle progress, energy situation, protein synthesis, and cell growth and vitality. AMPK inhibits proliferation and up-regulation of an inhibitor of cyclin-dependent kinase (CDK) p21cip1. Also, it inhibits the down-regulation of the mammalian target of rapamycin (mTOR). Eventually, all of these events give rise to the suppression of the CSC population.

...An in vivo study showed that epigallocatechin-3-gallate applies its anti-CSC activity via several mechanisms including inducing apoptosis through activation of caspase 3, inhibiting the expression of CD44 and MMP2, suppressing the angiogenesis through inhibition of VEGF and enhanced oxidative stress."

## "The Great Escape: The Power of Cancer Stem Cells to Evade Programmed Cell Death" (PMID: 33477367)
"Notably, epigallocatechin-3-gallate (EGCG), the main component of green tea, reduced lung CSC spheroid size, decreased stemness markers, inhibited proliferation, and stimulated apoptosis. The mechanism is mainly ascribed to the alteration of the Wnt/beta-catenin pathway. ... and it activated apoptosis in xenograft models. ... EGCG also suppressed the self-renewal ability of neck and head CSCs, reducing the expression of stemness markers and controlling spheroid formation. Furthermore, EGCG enhanced cisplatin-induced chemosensitivity by inhibiting ABC transporter genes and reducing tumor growth, and it activated apoptosis in xenograft models. ... The authors have ascribed the main mechanism of the anti-CSC activity of EGCG to the suppression of the Notch pathway."

While these quotations show induction of apoptosis and impairment of survival/metabolic pathways, there is *lack* of explicit evidence linking "severe metabolic stress" (from *dual* mitochondrial and survival signaling impairment via EGCG) as the *trigger* for the "intrinsic apoptotic cascade" (e.g., cytochrome c release/caspase-9 activation) in CSCs.

# Conclusion

While the literature solidly supports that EGCG:
- Can induce apoptosis in cancer stem cells,
- Impairs survival/metabolic signaling (AMPK, mTOR, Notch, Wnt/beta-catenin) and is associated with oxidative stress in CSCs,
- Is linked to mitochondria-associated signaling,

There is **no clear and direct evidence supporting the full, specified relationship**: that *dual impairment of mitochondrial and survival signaling by EGCG, causing severe metabolic stress, triggers the intrinsic apoptotic cascade (mitochondrial pathway) in cancer stem cells*. Available evidence semantically implies partial links, but not the whole sequence as required by the query.

Answer: False
