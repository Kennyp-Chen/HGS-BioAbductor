1. Hypothesis Summary:
The hypothesis posits that in the context of NAFLD (non‐alcoholic fatty liver disease)–associated liver cancer development, an increase in the activity of FTO (an m6A RNA demethylase) in hepatocytes leads to enhanced m6A demethylation. This enzymatic change is proposed to dysregulate the expression of genes involved in metabolism and inflammation, thereby creating a pro-tumorigenic microenvironment that supports the initiation and progression of hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC).

The theme of RNA methylation, particularly m6A modification, has garnered significant attention in cancer research. However, linking FTO specifically as an m6A demethylase to the dysregulation of metabolic and inflammatory gene expression in NAFLD-associated liver carcinogenesis is a relatively focused and emerging concept. While m6A modifications and FTO’s role in other cancers have been studied, applying this mechanism directly to NAFLD-related HCC offers an innovative perspective, distinguishing it from more generalized studies. The hypothesis could be considered moderately original, provided that it establishes clear connections and causality between FTO activity, gene expression changes, and the inflammatory-metabolic environment in NAFLD contexts.

The proposed mechanism is plausible, as both m6A dynamics and inflammation are known to influence cancer biology.
