1. Observation extraction:
 • Observation 1 (Abstract 1): In a liver fibrosis (LF) mouse model, genome‐wide alterations in m6A methylation were found, with thousands of genes showing hyper‐ or hypomethylation. This study implicated not just m6A changes in general but also specific methyltransferases (e.g., WTAP) and demethylases (ALKBH5) in the fibrotic process.
 • Observation 2 (Abstract 2): In hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), abnormal m6A methylation of long noncoding RNAs is correlated with altered expression of oncogenes/tumor suppressors, affecting behaviors such as proliferation, migration, invasion, stemness, and drug resistance.
 • Observation 3 (Abstract 3): m6A RNA methylation—and its regulators (writers, erasers, and readers)—play an important role in liver cancer development. Although the exact molecular mechanisms remain unclear, aberrant m6A regulation is closely linked with liver tumorigenesis.

3. Causal analysis (summary):
  Would we see some of the observations if the hypothesis was true: Yes, if increased FTO-mediated m6A demethylation in hepatocytes occurs in NAFLD-associated liver carcinogenesis, one might expect dysregulated gene expression and a pro-tumorigenic microenvironment. However, the observed roles of m6A modifications in liver fibrosis and HCC are already linked to multiple m6A regulators. The existing studies demonstrate that m6A dysregulation is important but do not single out FTO’s role as a unique causal factor. Consequently, while the hypothesis is consistent with the idea that altered m6A demethylation is involved, it does not offer a novel mechanism beyond what has already been suggested by the regulation through other enzymes.

4. Disproof analysis:
  Does some observations disprove the hypothesis: None of the abstracts directly contradict the notion that altered m6A modifications can drive liver pathology. However, none of them specifically support or implicate increased FTO activity in NAFLD-associated carcinogenesis. There is no direct contradiction, but the evidence favors a broader view of m6A regulation rather than FTO-specific causality.

5. Conclusion:
  hypothesis: neutral

The available observations show that m6A methylation alterations play an important role in liver fibrosis and hepatocellular carcinoma, but they already point to multiple regulators (writers, erasers, readers) influencing these processes. The hypothesis—that increased FTO-mediated m6A demethylation in hepatocytes in NAFLD leads to dysregulated gene expression promoting HCC—is not directly supported as a novel causal explanation. It remains consistent with known roles of m6A regulation, but alternative, equally plausible enzymes and mechanisms have already been established, making it a neutral addition rather than a missing piece or a definitively novel causal explanation.
