Observation 3 (Metabolic/inflammatory reprogramming via α-dicarbonyl compounds and RAGE in NAFLD/HCC):

Nonetheless, this observation has been explained through well-established metabolic and inflammatory mechanisms independent of FTO, so the hypothesis does not offer a clearly novel causal explanation—again, not a missing piece.

3. Causal analysis (summary):
Would we see some of the observations if the hypothesis was true: Yes, an increase in hepatocyte-specific FTO and consequent targeted m6A demethylation could contribute to enhanced lipid accumulation and inflammation that promote tumorigenesis in NAFLD. Thus, while the hypothesis could be consistent with parts of the observations, it does not fill an uncovered niche—it overlaps with mechanisms already described.

The hypothesis that hepatocyte-specific upregulation of FTO drives targeted m6A demethylation of metabolic and inflammatory transcripts in NAFLD-associated liver carcinogenesis is consistent with the notion of FTO’s role in liver cancer.
