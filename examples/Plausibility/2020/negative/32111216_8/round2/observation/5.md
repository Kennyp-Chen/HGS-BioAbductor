3. Causal analysis (summary):
 Would we see some of the observations if the hypothesis was true: The hypothesis posits that increased FTO activity demethylates m6A marks on mRNAs encoding key lipid metabolic enzymes, stabilizing these transcripts and increasing enzyme production—thereby heightening the accumulation of lipotoxic metabolites. This imbalance might then augment ROS and carbonyl stress and potentially potentiate inflammatory pathways, including those already attributed to the RNF5/IGF2BP1 axis. Although the hypothesis is compatible with several observed phenomena in NAFLD and steatotic HCC, each observation already has well-established causal explanations (excess FA supply, mitochondrial dysfunction, and ER stress). As a result, the added role of FTO does not strongly represent a missing mechanism but rather an additional layer that is not clearly distinct from known pathways.

4. Disproof analysis:
 Does some observation disprove the hypothesis: None of the observations outright contradict the proposed involvement of FTO. Every observation—mitochondrial oxidative stress, RNF5-mediated stabilization, and ER stress—is expected in NAFLD progression, regardless of FTO’s status. Therefore, while the hypothesis is compatible with the data, it does not uniquely resolve any unexplained gap that current models leave open.

5. Conclusion:
 Given that the metabolic, oxidative, and inflammatory observations in NAFLD and steatotic HCC are already well explained by established mechanisms (e.g., PPARγ activation, mitochondrial dysfunction, and ER stress), the hypothesized role of FTO as a central regulator adding to these cascades is not clearly a novel missing piece.
