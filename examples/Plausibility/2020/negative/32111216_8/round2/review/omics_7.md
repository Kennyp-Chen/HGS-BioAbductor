1. Hypothesis Summary

The hypothesis posits that chronic HBV and HCV infections in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) lead to dysregulation of the m6A RNA modification machinery. Key components include:
• An increase in the methyltransferase METTL3 and abnormal FTO activity.
• Resultant alterations of m6A modifications on three kinds of transcripts: host tumor suppressor mRNAs, viral transcripts, and mRNAs encoding oncogenic/metabolic regulators (e.g., HIF-1α and BMI1). These changes drive aberrant RNA stabilization, decay, or translational reprogramming, mediated by m6A reader proteins (e.g., YTHDF2).
• A dual pathogenic mechanism: destabilization of tumor suppressors coupled with metabolic reprogramming that propels oncogenesis, and a compromised antiviral immune response that enables viral persistence and fosters an immune-tolerant tumor microenvironment.
• The use of advanced molecular profiling (like RNA immunoprecipitation sequencing and single-cell RNA-seq) and innovative epitranscriptomic editing tools (such as CRISPR-dCas systems targeting m6A sites) as a strategy to restore normal mRNA homeostasis and immune function, potentially offering a novel therapeutic avenue for HBV/HCV-associated HCC.
