1. Hypothesis Summary  
The hypothesis posits that in liver carcinogenesis associated with non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD), there is a hepatocyte-specific upregulation of the m6A demethylase FTO. FTO is thought to work in synergy with other m6A regulators to trigger targeted demethylation of mRNAs related to metabolic and inflammatory processes. This demethylation is proposed to enhance the stability and translation of these transcripts, ultimately promoting lipid accumulation and chronic inflammation. The downstream effect of these molecular changes is the remodeling of the liver microenvironment into one that favors tumorigenesis. The research strategy includes using advanced single-cell and spatial transcriptomic techniques coupled with CRISPR-mediated modulation of FTO to chart this cascade and validate FTO as a potential molecular target in NAFLD-driven hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC).
