Keywords extracted were "exosomal microRNAs," "ctDNA methylation," and "hepatocellular carcinoma". The returned abstracts and accessible full texts largely address:

- The improved sensitivity and specificity of integrating multiple biomarkers/algorithms for HCC surveillance, especially for AFP-negative subtypes.
- Whether composite signatures of exosomal miRNAs and ctDNA methylation (combined) specifically predict aggressive or AFP-negative HCC.

After careful review of pertinent review and research literature, including those devoted to the most recent advances in liquid biopsy and biomarker integration in HCC, I looked for explicit statements or semantically equivalent claims supporting the predictive value of a composite signature comprising exactly exosomal miRNAs and ctDNA methylation alterations for aggressive or AFP-negative HCC.

# literature evidence
## "Refining Cancer Management Using Integrated Liquid Biopsy" (PMID: 32089746)
"Furthermore, a similar result showed that integrating the exosomal RNAs (exoRNAs) ... with alpha-fetoprotein accurately distinguished early hepatocellular carcinoma from liver cirrhosis."
(*Note*: The study provides evidence for integrating exosomal miRNAs and AFP, but not ctDNA methylation.)

## "Updating the Clinical Application of Blood Biomarkers and Their Algorithms in the Diagnosis and Surveillance of Hepatocellular Carcinoma: A Critical Review" (PMID: 36901717)
"Since about 20% of HCCs do not produce alpha-FP due to its biological diversity, combining alpha-FP with novel biomarkers can enhance HCC detection sensitivity. ... there is no single ideal marker in HCC. When combined with other clinical parameters, the detection of some biomarkers has higher sensitivity and specificity in comparison with a single biomarker."
(*Note*: This establishes the rationale for composite biomarker use in HCC, including for AFP-negative disease, but does not specifically cite a composite of exosomal miRNAs and ctDNA methylation.)

## "Circulating Biomarkers for Therapeutic Monitoring of Anti-cancer Agents" (PMID: 35285488)
"Single-nucleotide variants, insertions, and deletions are the most common alterations used to differentiate ctDNA from cfDNA from other sources, although novel approaches including methylation signatures and bespoke panels personalized to an individual's tumor mutations have been used for this purpose."
(*Note*: No evidence for a composite signature combining exosomal miRNAs and ctDNA methylation for aggressive/AFP-negative HCC.)

# Conclusion
There is evidence of integrated biomarker approaches (including exosomal miRNAs and AFP) increasing diagnostic accuracy in HCC, and rationale for composite markers in challenging HCC subtypes. However, no literature provides direct or semantically implied evidence for a composite signature *of exosomal miRNAs and ctDNA methylation alterations* specifically predicting aggressive hepatocellular carcinoma, including AFP-negative variants.

Answer: False
