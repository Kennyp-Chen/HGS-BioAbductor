 Would we see some of the observations if the hypothesis was true: Yes, one might observe elevated AFP-L3 levels in an inflammatory milieu. Therefore, while the hypothesis introduces a specific cytokine–glycosyltransferase axis, it does not offer a uniquely novel explanation for these observations.

4. Disproof analysis:
 Does some observations disprove the hypothesis: None of the observations explicitly contradict the hypothesis. However, they do show that multiple alternative and well-established mechanisms (transcriptional regulation, direct cytokine-induced DNA damage, and standard post-translational modifications) account for the malignant characteristics and marker profiles in HCC. This diminishes the necessity of invoking the specific glycosyltransferase axis as the primary explanation.

5. Conclusion:
The hypothesis proposes that inflammatory cytokines drive glycosyltransferase upregulation leading to enhanced production of the AFP-L3 isoform, contributing to early HCC aggressiveness. Although this is an interesting idea, the available observations are already well explained by established transcriptional networks, inflammation-induced damage, and known post-translational modifications. Thus, while the hypothesis is not directly disproved, other explanations are more likely.
