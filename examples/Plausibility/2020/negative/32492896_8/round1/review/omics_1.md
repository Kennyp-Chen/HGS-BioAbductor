1. Hypothesis Summary

The hypothesis posits that in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), hepatic stellate cells (HSCs) produce hepatocyte growth factor (HGF), which in turn activates the MET pathway. This activation is proposed to drive the production of the AFP-L3 isoform—a serological marker associated with tumor aggressiveness. By inhibiting HSC-derived HGF, the hypothesis suggests that MET pathway signaling would be dampened, reducing AFP-L3 levels and tumor aggressiveness. Ultimately, this approach is proposed as a novel therapeutic strategy to improve clinical outcomes in HCC patients.

  – Linking the inhibition of this paracrine signaling to a reduction in AFP-L3 production, a biomarker more conventionally viewed as reflective rather than mechanistically linked to tumor biology.
