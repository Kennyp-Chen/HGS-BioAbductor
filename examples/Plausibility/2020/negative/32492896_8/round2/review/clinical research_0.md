1. Hypothesis Summary

Your hypothesis proposes that in chronic liver inflammation, high levels of inflammatory cytokines (particularly IL-6 and TNF-α) lead to increased expression of specific glycosyltransferases in hepatocytes. This enzyme upregulation then produces aberrantly glycosylated alpha-fetoprotein (AFP), predominantly converting it into the AFP-L3 isoform. Traditionally considered just a biomarker, AFP-L3 is here posited to actively contribute to hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) pathogenesis by engaging in oncogenic signaling and modulating transcription factor networks. These changes are thought to drive aggressive tumor behavior, including increased vascular invasion and poor cell differentiation. Finally, the hypothesis asserts that targeting the cytokine–glycosyltransferase axis may not only improve early diagnostic strategies but also offer a therapeutic pathway to hinder HCC progression, with validation planned using modern tools like CRISPR gene editing, single-cell transcriptomics, and high-resolution glycomics.
