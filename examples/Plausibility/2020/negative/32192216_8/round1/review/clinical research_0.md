1. Hypothesis Summary  
─────────────────────────────  
The hypothesis proposes that when both hepatic PPARα and PPARβ/δ are activated at the same time, they work synergistically to increase the production and secretion of fibroblast growth factor 21 (FGF21) and a specific phosphatidylcholine species, PC (18:0/18:1). These secreted factors are then proposed to “reprogram” muscle lipid metabolism via a mechanism mediated by PPARα. Ultimately, this dual receptor activation and the subsequent biochemical cascade are suggested as a new therapeutic target for systemic metabolic disorders such as nonalcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) and insulin resistance.

2. Originality Assessment  
─────────────────────────────  
• Novel Components:  
  – The idea of simultaneous activation of two hepatic PPAR isoforms (PPARα and PPARβ/δ) is not commonly emphasized.  
  – The coupling of PPAR activation to both FGF21 and a specific lipid moiety (PC (18:0/18:1)) as mediators is a unique twist that integrates hormonal (FGF21) and lipid signals.  

• Innovative Perspective:  
  – Linking liver-derived signals (FGF21 and specific phosphatidylcholine) to the reprogramming of muscle lipid metabolism introduces a novel endocrine axis that could bridge hepatic and muscle function in metabolic regulation.  
  – Although FGF21 is well-studied in metabolic contexts, its synergy with a specific phospholipid in affecting muscle metabolism is an innovative perspective that distinguishes this hypothesis from many existing studies.
