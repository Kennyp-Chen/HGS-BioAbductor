1. Hypothesis Summary  
The hypothesis posits that by modulating the diurnal activity of hepatic PPARβ/δ, one can restore the optimal production and secretion of a specific phosphatidylcholine species, PC (18:0/18:1). This liver-derived lipid signal is then proposed to enhance fatty acid oxidation in muscle via activation of muscle PPARα. The overall anticipated effect is an improvement in systemic lipid metabolism and insulin sensitivity, which may counteract metabolic disorders such as NAFLD.

• The concept of a liver–muscle endocrine crosstalk via PC (18:0/18:1) is not a mainstream approach in current literature and offers a fresh perspective on inter-organ metabolic regulation.  
• Using distinct PPAR subtypes in different tissues to coordinate metabolic homeostasis adds novelty, as most studies tend to focus on a single tissue or receptor type.  
In sum, the hypothesis introduces an innovative mechanism that distinguishes it from more traditional views on metabolic regulation and inter-organ communication.
