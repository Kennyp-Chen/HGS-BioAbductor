1. Hypothesis Summary

The hypothesis posits that administering specific dietary antioxidants—namely N-acetylcysteine (NAC) and vitamin E—at clinically optimized doses can mitigate hepatic oxidative and endoplasmic reticulum (ER) stress in patients with NAFLD (or similar liver metabolic disorders). Using state-of-the-art omics and imaging techniques, the hypothesis suggests that reducing oxidative stress leads to a rebalancing of peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor (PPAR) isoform activity: enhancing PPARα-driven catabolic/lipid oxidation pathways while suppressing PPARγ-driven lipogenic processes. This metabolic shift is further proposed to stimulate the secretion of key hepatokines (such as FGF21) and endocrine mediators (including adiponectin), thereby promoting peripheral lipid oxidation and improving overall systemic insulin sensitivity. Furthermore, the hypothesis integrates advanced molecular profiling and computational modeling to objectively quantify dose-response relationships and signaling dynamics, providing a mechanistic basis for targeting NAFLD progression.

• The incorporation of cutting-edge omics and imaging techniques, along with computational modeling, represents an innovative attempt to quantify and mechanistically detail the intervention strategy.  
• Overall, while the antioxidant intervention itself is a well-trodden path, the specific focus on modulating PPARα and PPARγ balance—leading to a cascade affecting hepatokine release and systemic insulin sensitivity—adds a novel layer of mechanistic detail that distinguishes it from existing studies.
