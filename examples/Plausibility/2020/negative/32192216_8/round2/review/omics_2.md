1. Hypothesis Summary  
──────────────────────────────  
The hypothesis posits that administering specific dietary antioxidants—namely N-acetylcysteine (NAC) and vitamin E—at clinically optimized doses can reduce hepatic oxidative and endoplasmic reticulum (ER) stress. In turn, this metabolic reprogramming is expected to stimulate the secretion of hepatokines (with a focus on fibroblast growth factor 21, FGF21) and endocrine mediators (including adiponectin), ultimately promoting peripheral lipid oxidation and improving systemic insulin sensitivity. The study further suggests employing molecular profiling and computational modeling to rigorously detail the dose-response relationships and signaling dynamics associated with this intervention, targeting a mechanistic intervention strategy against nonalcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) progression.
