1. Hypothesis Summary

The hypothesis posits that supplementing the diet with polyphenol-rich extracts reduces hepatic oxidative stress, which then specifically enhances hepatic PPARβ/δ activity. This receptor activation upregulates lipogenic genes that drive the synthesis of a defined phosphatidylcholine, PC (18:0/18:1). The lipid is proposed to be secreted into the bloodstream and transported to skeletal muscle, where it acts—either solo or together with other endogenous lipid mediators—to activate PPARα. In muscle, this activation promotes fatty acid uptake and oxidation, ultimately reducing circulating triglycerides and ameliorating both dyslipidemia and hepatic steatosis. The hypothesis further outlines the use of modern stable isotope lipidomics and wearable biosensors to monitor metabolic fluxes in real time, and suggests employing CRISPR-Cas9-mediated gene-editing in the liver and muscle to perturb PPARβ/δ and PPARα functions respectively, thereby validating each sequential event in the proposed cascade.
