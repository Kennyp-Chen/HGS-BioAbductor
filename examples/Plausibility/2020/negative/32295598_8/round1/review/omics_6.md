1. Hypothesis Summary

The hypothesis posits that altering m6A modifications on long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) changes their secondary structure and binding properties. This structural change triggers epigenetic reprogramming of metabolic pathways, which in turn drives the progression of hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC). Based on this cascade, the hypothesis further suggests that targeting the m6A modification machinery might restore proper lncRNA function and metabolic balance, offering a novel therapeutic strategy in HCC.

• Interconnection with Metabolic Reprogramming: While metabolic reprogramming is a well-known hallmark in cancer, proposing that epigenetic reprogramming via lncRNA alterations impacts metabolic pathways in HCC is an innovative integration of epigenetics, RNA biology, and cancer metabolism.

• Therapeutic Angle: The suggestion to target the m6A machinery as a means to restore normal lncRNA activity and metabolic balance in HCC introduces a fresh perspective in the realm of precision oncology. However, similar approaches have been explored to some extent, so the originality lies in the specific focus on lncRNA structural dynamics and metabolism.
