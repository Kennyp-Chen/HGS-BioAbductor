1. Hypothesis Summary:
The hypothesis posits that in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), abnormal m6A modifications on tumor‐suppressive long noncoding RNAs (lncRNAs) change their secondary structure. This structural alteration, in turn, impairs the lncRNAs’ ability to interact with epigenetic regulators, which normally help maintain cellular homeostasis. As a consequence, such a disruption promotes metabolic reprogramming and cell proliferation. The hypothesis further suggests that by restoring normal m6A modification levels on these lncRNAs, their tumor-suppressive functions could be reactivated, offering a novel therapeutic strategy against HCC.

2. Originality Assessment:
The hypothesis offers an innovative integration of several cutting-edge concepts in the omics field, including epitranscriptomics (m6A modifications), lncRNA function, epigenetic regulation, and cellular metabolism reprogramming in cancer. While individual roles of m6A modifications and lncRNAs in cancer have been previously explored, linking aberrant m6A-dependent structural changes in tumor-suppressive lncRNAs specifically to impaired interactions with epigenetic regulators—and subsequently to metabolic reprogramming in HCC—is a relatively novel perspective. This nuanced interplay has the potential to distinguish the hypothesis from more conventional views, provided that experimental and mechanistic details are substantiated.

3. Validity and Strength Evaluation:
The hypothesis exhibits logical coherence by connecting several well-documented biological phenomena:
 • m6A modifications are known to influence RNA secondary structure and stability.
 • Alterations in lncRNA structures can feasibly affect their interactions with proteins, including epigenetic regulators.
 • Disruption of epigenetic regulatory mechanisms is a recognized contributor to aberrant gene expression profiles, which can underpin metabolic reprogramming and unchecked cell proliferation in cancer.
However, there are aspects that require strengthened evidence or further elaboration:
 • The direct causal relationship between specific aberrant m6A modifications on tumor-suppressive lncRNAs and their secondary structure changes needs more explicit supporting data or theoretical grounding.
 • The mechanistic link between the impaired interaction of these lncRNAs with epigenetic regulators and the precise process of metabolic reprogramming in HCC should be delineated more explicitly.
 • The hypothesis assumes that normalizing m6A levels alone is sufficient to restore lncRNA function and reverse tumorigenic processes, but the interdependency between m6A ‘writers’, ‘erasers’, ‘readers’, and the multifactorial nature of HCC progression requires further clarification.

4. Conclusion and Expert Recommendations:
Overall, the hypothesis is compelling and integrates several important molecular layers that contribute to HCC pathogenesis. However, to enhance its scientific rigor and persuasive power, the following recommendations should be considered:
 • Explicitly identify the tumor-suppressive lncRNAs of interest. Providing examples or candidate molecules will help ground the hypothesis in concrete biological systems.
 • Elaborate on the role of specific m6A regulators (i.e., writers, erasers, and readers) implicated in modifying these lncRNAs and how their dysregulation might lead to the observed structural changes.
 • Detail the experimental design or analytical approaches that could be used to validate the causal links—for instance, using high-throughput m6A mapping, structural probing of lncRNAs, and interaction assays with epigenetic regulators.
 • Address potential confounding variables by considering other modifications or regulatory mechanisms that could affect lncRNA structure and function.
 • Clarify the proposed mechanism underlying the restoration of tumor-suppressive functions upon normalization of m6A levels, perhaps by discussing possible therapeutic delivery methods or strategies for targeting the m6A modification machinery.
By addressing these gaps, the hypothesis would become more robust and provide a clearer roadmap for experimental validation, strengthening its potential impact in the field of cancer omics.
