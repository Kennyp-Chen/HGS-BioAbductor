1. Hypothesis Summary:
The hypothesis proposes that in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), hypoxic conditions induce m6A modifications on specific long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs). These modifications, in turn, promote the production of micropeptides from these lncRNAs. The generated micropeptides are posited to interfere with the normal degradation mechanisms of HIF-1α. As a result, HIF-1α is stabilized, leading to enhanced angiogenesis, metabolic reprogramming, and metastasis. The hypothesis ultimately suggests that therapeutic strategies aimed at inhibiting the m6A modification process or the function of these micropeptides might be effective in treating HCC.

──────────────────────────────
2. Originality Assessment:
• The idea of m6A modifications regulating the translation of lncRNAs into micropeptides is an emerging concept in molecular oncology. Although m6A modifications have been extensively studied in the context of RNA stability, localization, and translation, their specific role in driving micropeptide production from lncRNAs in HCC is less explored.
• The connection between these micropeptides and the regulation of HIF-1α stability, especially under hypoxic conditions, provides an innovative mechanistic link that distinguishes this hypothesis from more conventional studies on hypoxia and HIF-1α in cancer.
• Overall, the hypothesis offers a novel perspective by integrating epigenetic RNA modifications with non-canonical translation events and HIF-driven oncogenic processes, potentially opening new avenues for targeted therapy in HCC.

It follows with the novel claim that these modifications enable the production of micropeptides, which then disrupt HIF-1α degradation. The resulting stabilization of HIF-1α is known to impact various tumorigenic processes such as angiogenesis, metabolic reprogramming, and metastasis.

  - The mechanism by which these micropeptides disrupt HIF-1α degradation requires further elucidation. It is critical to determine whether these peptides inhibit the proteasomal or lysosomal pathways or interfere with other regulatory proteins that normally target HIF-1α.

However, robust direct evidence linking m6A-modified lncRNAs to micropeptide production and subsequent effects on HIF-1α degradation specifically in HCC is sparse. This gap makes the hypothesis intriguing but somewhat speculative at this stage.
• Proposal for Therapeutic Intervention: Targeting m6A modifications or the micropeptides themselves is an attractive idea.
