1. Hypothesis Summary  
─────────────────────────────  
The hypothesis proposes that in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), chronic liver stress and oncogenic signaling lead to the upregulation of m6A methyltransferases. This upregulation results in aberrant m6A modifications on tumor-suppressive long noncoding RNAs (lncRNAs), which in turn alter their secondary structure. The structural changes impair these lncRNAs’ ability to bind key epigenetic regulators such as EZH2 and DNMTs, which normally help repress metabolic and proliferative pathways. Consequently, the loss of this regulatory mechanism promotes cancer progression. Importantly, the hypothesis suggests that using innovative CRISPR-dCas13 RNA editing to normalize m6A marks—and validating the structural changes with SHAPE-MaP profiling—could restore the tumor-suppressive functions of these lncRNAs and thereby slow or reverse HCC advancement.

2. Originality Assessment  
─────────────────────────────  
The concept is fairly innovative, particularly in coupling RNA modifications with lncRNA structural dynamics and their downstream effects on epigenetic regulation. While m6A modification and its impact on RNA biology in cancer have been studied, the focus on how altered m6A marks might specifically disrupt lncRNA interactions with epigenetic regulators like EZH2 and DNMTs in HCC is a novel angle. Additionally, proposing CRISPR-dCas13-mediated RNA editing for targeted normalization of m6A marks provides an innovative therapeutic approach that extends beyond typical strategies targeting DNA or protein-level alterations. Thus, the integration of advanced RNA editing with structural profiling to counteract oncogenic processes presents a perspective that distinguishes itself from many established studies in the field.
