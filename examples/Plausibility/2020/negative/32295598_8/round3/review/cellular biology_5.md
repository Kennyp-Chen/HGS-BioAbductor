1. Hypothesis Summary

The second, novel axis postulates that oxidative stress directly activates the METTL3/METTL14 complex, which in turn selectively modifies a group of hypoxia-responsive long non-coding RNAs (e.g., lncRNA-X). This modification is hypothesized to enhance these lncRNAs’ affinity for the RNA-binding protein IGF2BP2, resulting in the stabilization of HIF-1α mRNA. The accumulation of HIF-1α protein then bolsters angiogenesis and reinforces an environment of oxidative stress, establishing a self-amplifying loop that could further promote tumor progression. Planned experimental approaches include m6A-seq, CLIP-seq, and CRISPR-based epitranscriptomic interventions in both in vitro and in vivo models.

This novel lncRNA-dependent axis offers a new layer of epitranscriptomic regulation in HCC, potentially opening up unexplored avenues in cancer biology and providing a creative angle distinct from conventional models of oxidative stress and tumorigenesis.
