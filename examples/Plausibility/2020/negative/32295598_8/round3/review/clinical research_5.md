1. Hypothesis Summary

The hypothesis proposes that in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), oxidative stress promotes tumor progression via two interlinked regulatory axes. The first axis involves the well‐documented pathway where HBXIP upregulates METTL3, leading to enhanced m6A modifications on crucial metabolic transcripts—including direct m6A modification of HIF-1α mRNA—which supports metabolic reprogramming and tumor growth. The second, more novel axis suggests that oxidative stress directly activates the METTL3/METTL14 complex, which specifically modifies a subset of hypoxia-responsive long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs), such as lncRNA-X. This epitranscriptomic modification purportedly increases these lncRNAs' affinity for the RNA-binding protein IGF2BP2, resulting in the stabilization of HIF-1α mRNA. The subsequent accumulation of HIF-1α protein boosts angiogenesis and further intensifies oxidative stress, creating a self-reinforcing loop. The model is intended to be rigorously tested using advanced technologies like m6A-seq, CLIP-seq, and CRISPR-based epitranscriptomic interventions in both in vitro and in vivo settings.

• The second axis is novel. It suggests a direct activation of the METTL3/METTL14 complex by oxidative stress and posits that specific hypoxia-responsive lncRNAs undergo epitranscriptomic modifications that facilitate IGF2BP2 binding and HIF-1α mRNA stabilization.
