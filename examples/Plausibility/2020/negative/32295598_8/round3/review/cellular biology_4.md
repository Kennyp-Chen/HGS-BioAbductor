1. Hypothesis Summary

The hypothesis posits that in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), chronic liver stress together with oncogenic signaling upregulates m6A methyltransferases. These enzymes then excessively modify tumor-suppressive lncRNAs (for example, MEG3) by depositing m6A marks abnormally. The added m6A modifications are proposed to change the secondary structure of these lncRNAs, compromising their ability to interact with key epigenetic regulators (EZH2, DNMTs) that normally act to suppress metabolic and proliferative signaling pathways. This disruption in epigenetic regulation is thought to facilitate oncogenic activation and drive HCC progression. To address and reverse this cascade, the hypothesis further proposes a dual-pronged experimental approach; one component involves CRISPR-dCas13-based RNA editing to restore normal m6A patterns, and the other utilizes SHAPE-MaP (Selective 2′-Hydroxyl Acylation analyzed by Primer Extension and Mutational Profiling) alongside in vitro RNA-binding assays to verify that restored m6A patterns lead to proper lncRNA folding and function.

──────────────────────────────
2. Originality Assessment

• Innovation in the Causal Chain: The hypothesis connects persistent chronic liver stress, m6A methyltransferase overexpression, and the subsequent epitranscriptomic alteration of tumor-suppressor lncRNAs to cause deregulation of epigenetic repression. While the role of m6A modifications in cancer is an actively investigated area, the specific link between m6A-induced structural alteration of lncRNAs like MEG3 and their impaired interaction with epigenetic regulators provides a nuanced, mechanistic perspective that is not widely represented in the current literature.

• Therapeutic Intervention Strategy: The proposal of employing CRISPR-dCas13-mediated RNA editing to “correct” aberrant m6A modifications is innovative. Coupling this with structure-probing (SHAPE-MaP) and binding assays to validate function adds a layer of experimental rigor that could significantly enhance the translational potential of the study.

Overall, the hypothesis does offer an innovative perspective by integrating epitranscriptomic modifications, lncRNA structure–function dynamics, and targeted RNA editing, although some related ideas have been explored in various forms in recent studies. Its combined therapeutic approach, however, distinguishes it within the field.

3. Validity and Strength Evaluation

• Logical Coherence:  
  - The causal sequence—from chronic stress and oncogenic signals to m6A methyltransferase overexpression, to aberrant lncRNA modification and altered secondary structure, and finally to loss of epigenetic repression—flows logically.  
  - The mechanistic link between RNA structure alterations and diminished binding to epigenetic regulators is plausible, given the known sensitivity of RNA-protein interactions to RNA folding dynamics.

• Theoretical Grounding:  
  - There is scientific literature supporting roles for both m6A modifications in cancer and the significance of lncRNA secondary structures in mediating their interactions.  
  - However, the hypothesis assumes that m6A modifications uniformly disrupt RNA secondary structure in a way that impairs epigenetic regulator binding. This aspect, while plausible, might depend on context-specific factors and the precise location of m6A sites within MEG3 or other lncRNAs.

• Evidence and Experimental Testability:  
  - The methods proposed (CRISPR-dCas13 for RNA editing and SHAPE-MaP for structural analysis) are contemporary and well-suited to provide the necessary data to validate the hypothesis.  
  - The mention of potential feedback loops and compensatory mechanisms indicates an awareness of the cellular network complexity, although these factors are noted only briefly and could be expanded upon.

4. Conclusion and Expert Recommendations

Notable Strengths:
• The hypothesis presents a coherent narrative linking chronic liver stress and oncogenic signaling to specific epigenetic dysregulation via m6A modifications.
• It proposes a dual-intervention method that is both innovative and experimentally tractable, following the current trend of precision RNA editing.
• The integration of structural biology (SHAPE-MaP) with functional assays provides a comprehensive strategy to test the proposed mechanism.

Weaknesses and Gaps:
• Specificity in m6A Effects: The hypothesis could benefit from additional clarification on how m6A modifications specifically alter the secondary structure of lncRNAs like MEG3. For example, are there particular m6A sites whose modification is critical for maintaining the binding interface with EZH2/DNMTs?  
• Heterogeneity of m6A Impact: It may be useful to discuss whether all m6A modifications uniformly lead to structure change or if there are cases of protective or compensatory structural retention.
• Feedback and Compensatory Loops: Although the hypothesis acknowledges potential feedback loops, a more elaborated discussion on how these loops might be identified and negated in the experimental setup would strengthen the argument.
• Therapeutic Delivery Challenges: The proposed CRISPR-dCas13 system, while conceptually robust, may face significant delivery and specificity challenges in vivo. Addressing these challenges, even briefly, could enhance the overall persuasiveness of the therapeutic approach.
