# Search Strategy and Reasoning
I used the minimal MeSH terms/keywords: "Oxidative Stress" and "METTL3 or METTL14" (as "METTL3/METTL14 complex" relates specifically to these two proteins forming a methyltransferase complex). The search focus was strictly on evidence for direct activation of METTL3/METTL14 by redox (oxidative stress)-sensitive modifications, i.e., not just their association, but explicit mention of redox-sensitive, oxidative modification-driven activation. I prioritized papers describing mechanistic links involving cysteine oxidation or other direct post-translational redox modifications regulating METTL3/METTL14 activity. I reviewed the most relevant abstracts and retrieved full text for key articles.

# literature evidence

## "Metformin enhances METTL14-Mediated m6A methylation to alleviate NIT-1 cells apoptosis induced by hydrogen peroxide" (PMID: 38312705)
Abstract sentence: "In conclusion, our findings indicate that Metformin induces METTL14-mediated alterations in m6A methylation levels, thereby shielding beta-cells from apoptosis and oxidative damage induced by oxidative stress."

Full text review: The paper shows that oxidative stress (via H2O2) impairs METTL14 function and m6A, and that metformin counters these changes, but does **not** state that oxidative stress activates the METTL3/METTL14 complex via direct redox-sensitive modifications, nor does it indicate any structural/biochemical post-translational oxidative modification as a regulatory switch.

## "METTL14 Mediates Glut3 m6A methylation to improve osteogenesis under oxidative stress condition" (PMID: 39737912)
Abstract sentence: "METTL14 Mediates Glut3 m6A methylation to improve osteogenesis under oxidative stress condition."
Full text analysis: The article demonstrates that oxidative stress decreases METTL14 expression and m6A methylation of Glut3, impacting osteogenesis, but does **not** show direct activation of METTL14 (nor the complex) by any redox-sensitive modification.

## "The m6A methylation enzyme METTL14 regulates myocardial ischemia/reperfusion injury through the Akt/mTOR signaling pathway" (PMID: 37436654)
Abstract sentence: "Knocking down m6A methylase METTL14 inhibits IR-induced myocardial apoptosis and necrosis, inhibits myocardial oxidative stress and secretion of inflammatory cytokines, and activates the Akt/mTOR signaling pathway."
Full text analysis: While METTL14 impacts cellular responses to oxidative stress, there is no evidence for its direct activation by redox-sensitive modifications.

## "Metformin enhances METTL14-Mediated m6A methylation to alleviate NIT-1 cells apoptosis induced by hydrogen peroxide" (PMID: 38312705)
(Already reviewed above.) No evidence of redox-sensitive post-translational modification directly activating the complex.

# Conclusion

Across the examined literature, METTL3/METTL14 are regulated in the context of oxidative stress and mediate part of the stress response, but there is **no** credible evidence for "direct activation via redox-sensitive modifications" (e.g., S-glutathionylation, nitrosylation, sulfenylation, etc.) of the METTL3/METTL14 complex by oxidative stress. All reports describe transcriptional or post-stress changes but do not attribute them to a direct redox regulatory switch on the protein complex.

Answer: False
