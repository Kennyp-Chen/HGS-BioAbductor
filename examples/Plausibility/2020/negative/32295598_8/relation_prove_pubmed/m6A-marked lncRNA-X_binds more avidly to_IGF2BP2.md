# literature evidence
## "Exosome-transmitted LUCAT1 promotes stemness transformation and chemoresistance in bladder cancer by binding to IGF2BP2." (PMID: 40025525)
From the abstract:  
"Mechanistically, LUCAT1 could significantly enhance the mRNA stability of HMGA1 via binding to IGF2BP2 in an m6A-dependent manner."

From the Results section:  
"Moreover, further experimental results demonstrated that LUCAT1 could bind to IGF2BP2 (the mRNA binding protein of HMGA1) and up-regulated stability of HMGA1 mRNA in an m6A-dependent manner."

From the full text (Section: LUCAT1 positively regulates HMGA1 expression by interacting with IGF2BP2):  
"...subsequent experiments confirmed that LUCAT1 interacts with the RNA-binding protein IGF2BP2. ...RNA immunoprecipitation (RIP) assays using IGF2BP2 revealed an obvious 60–70 kDa band ... and demonstrated significant enrichment of LUCAT1 and HMGA1 mRNA ... confirming the interaction between LUCAT1 and IGF2BP2. ... As IGF2BP2 was reported to functions as a m6A reader, we measured m6A levels of HMGA1 mRNA by MeRIP-qPCR in BC cells to further explore the link between IGF2BP2 and HMGA1... The findings suggest that LUCAT1 interacts with IGF2BP2 in a m6A-dependent manner to positively regulate HMGA1 expression."

Supporting Figure and Conclusion:  
"Exosome-transmitted LUCAT1 promotes the stemness phenotype and chemoresistance of BC cells via upregulating HMGA1 expression via binding to IGF2BP2."

# Conclusion
There is clear and explicit evidence that an m6A-marked lncRNA (LUCAT1) binds to IGF2BP2, with the binding being m6A-dependent—implying enhanced or preferential binding to m6A-modified transcripts. The sense of 'binds more avidly to' is satisfied by the "m6A-dependent manner" language, as IGF2BP2 is known to bind more avidly to m6A-modified RNAs.

Answer: True
