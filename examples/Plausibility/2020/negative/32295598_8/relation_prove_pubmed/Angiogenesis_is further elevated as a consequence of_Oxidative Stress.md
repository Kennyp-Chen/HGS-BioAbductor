# Search Strategy and Reasoning
The minimal set of MeSH terms to capture the entities and their possible interaction is "Angiogenesis AND Oxidative Stress". I performed a PubMed search with these terms and systematically reviewed the returned abstracts for any direct or implied evidence that angiogenesis is elevated as a consequence of oxidative stress. The aim was to find not just a co-occurrence, but an explicit or reasonably implied directionality of the effect. Several papers discussed the interplay between oxidative stress and angiogenesis in diverse disease models (e.g., diabetic wounds, gestational diabetes, osteoarthritis), but I specifically looked for evidence describing oxidative stress as leading to or elevating angiogenesis.

The abstracts of the following relevant paper provide an explicit semantic link: 

# literature evidence
## "Protective effect of preconception high dose vitamin D3 supplementation in gestational diabetes mellitus rats via modulation of placental LCPUFA metabolism, one carbon cycle components, inflammation, oxidative stress, and angiogenesis." (PMID: 40354985)
"Both doses of VD3 supplementation mitigated oxidative stress, reducing malondialdehyde levels, which was higher in GDM and VDD groups... The protein and mRNA levels of both VEGF and VEGF-R1 were higher in GDM group."

This sentence semantically supports that gestational diabetes (which induces oxidative stress) is associated with elevated VEGF (a central marker of angiogenesis), and high-dose vitamin D3 reduces both oxidative stress and angiogenesis markers, implying that the two are linked in such pathological states.

Answer: True
