## "Mitochondria-Targeting Chemodynamic Therapy Nanodrugs for Cancer Treatment" PMID: 35222052
Extracted supportive statement from full text:  
"Ir@MnFe2O4 NPs generated high temperatures locally in the mitochondria under the AMF, greatly promoting the rate of Fenton reaction and thus producing a high level of ROS to induce mitochondria damage (D)."  
Further, it states mitigation approaches: "Currently, three strategies are developed to greatly improve the efficacy of M-CDT nanodrugs: supplying H2O2, non-H2O2 dependent CDT, and eliminating GSH in cancer cells... elimination of GSH in cancer cells... could significantly improve the tumor killing effect of ROS."  
Most importantly, semantic evidence for mitigation:  
"The effect of CDT is greatly limited by the high concentration of glutathione (GSH), the unsustainable supply of H2O2, and the low Fenton reaction rate at the tumor site. ... The Bio-PEG2K-S-S-CPT part of BDTLAG NPs actively targeted cancer cells ... effectively removed GSH in cancer cells ... Bio-PEG2K-S-S-CPT effectively removed GSH in cancer cells, and the CPT released by Bio-PEG2K-S-S-CPT was 6.8 times higher than that by the group without GSH response in cancer cells. ... At the same time, LND and TOS entered the mitochondria and caused high concentrations of ROS in situ on mitochondria, which led to mitochondria damage to induce the death of cancer cells. Compared with the CPT group and the BDG NPs group (without mitochondria targeting), BDTLAG NPs had the best tumor suppression effect because of their mitochondria targeting and efficient GSH depletion."

While the focus is on enhancing rather than mitigating the Fenton reaction to increase mitochondrial damage, the text also directly discusses the effect of intracellular antioxidant defense systems like GSH in *reducing* ROS-mediated mitochondrial injury:  
"...the effect of CDT is greatly limited by the high concentration of glutathione (GSH)... The ROS can easily be eliminated by the GSH-Glutathione peroxidase system during CDT treatment."  
This suggests that mitigation (via GSH or other means) *reduces* Fenton reaction-induced mitochondrial damage, satisfying the relationship requested.
