1. Hypothesis Summary

The hypothesis suggests that enhancing the transsulfuration pathway in hepatocytes via modern gene-editing techniques can counteract ferroptosis—a type of iron-dependent, lipid peroxidation-driven cell death—by compensating for dysfunctional cystine uptake. Key elements include:
• Using CRISPR/Cas9-mediated activation to upregulate enzymes cystathionine β-synthase (CBS) and cystathionine γ-lyase (CGL), thereby boosting endogenous cysteine synthesis.
• Employing targeted nanoparticle delivery systems to achieve precise modulation in hepatocytes.
• The increased cysteine is proposed to augment glutathione (GSH) production, maintain GPX4 (glutathione peroxidase 4) activity, and, in combination with autophagy-regulated pathways (including pathways involving PNO1), reduce lipid peroxidation.
• The overall goal is to protect hepatocytes—especially in the context of non-alcoholic steatohepatitis (NASH)—potentially delaying progression towards fibrosis and hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC).
