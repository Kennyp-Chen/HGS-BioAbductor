1. Hypothesis Summary

The hypothesis posits that in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), hyperactivation of androgen receptor (AR) signaling leads to an upregulation of hexokinase 2 (HK2), which in turn enhances aerobic glycolysis (the Warburg effect). This metabolic reprogramming creates an acidic tumor microenvironment that suppresses the immune response and contributes to resistance against sorafenib, a standard therapeutic agent. The hypothesis suggests that by identifying a subset of HCC patients with this AR/HK2 hyperactivation — using advanced genomic and metabolomic profiling — one can implement a precision therapy approach. This approach would combine next-generation AR and HK2 inhibitors (potentially delivered or augmented by CRISPR/Cas9-mediated gene editing and RNA interference) alongside sorafenib treatment. Additionally, the hypothesis recommends monitoring alternative signaling pathways (such as HGF/c-Met) to preempt and mitigate compensatory resistance mechanisms.

• Linking AR signaling to HK2 upregulation in HCC is an innovative twist, as AR’s role in metabolic regulation particularly in liver cancer is less explored than in other cancers.
• The multi-pronged therapeutic approach that combines targeted inhibitors, advanced gene editing techniques, and sorafenib is an inventive strategy not common in conventional treatment paradigms.
• The proposal to use comprehensive omic profiling to stratify patients and monitor alternative pathways (like HGF/c-Met) adds a modern precision medicine dimension.

While each individual component (e.g., the role of aerobic glycolysis, sorafenib resistance, or AR in cancer biology) has been studied before, their integration into a cohesive hypothesis addressing sorafenib resistance via metabolic reprogramming is a fresh perspective within the field of HCC. However, caution is warranted as some connections (e.g., direct regulation of HK2 by AR) might have limited precedent and will require robust experimental validation.
