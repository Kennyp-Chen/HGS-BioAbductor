1. Hypothesis Summary  
─────────────────────────────  
The hypothesis proposes that by inhibiting the circRNA circMAT2B in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) cells using CRISPR-based methods, one can disrupt its regulatory control over the oncogenic enzyme PKM2. The premise is that circMAT2B normally enhances PKM2 activity—possibly through microRNA sponging or altered protein-protein interactions—thereby promoting aerobic glycolysis (the “Warburg effect”). Disrupting circMAT2B would prompt a metabolic switch from glycolysis to oxidative phosphorylation, reducing lactate production. Lower lactate levels would then impede the stabilization of HIF-1α, a critical regulator of angiogenesis and metastasis, thus potentially increasing the tumor’s responsiveness to sorafenib, a frontline therapeutic agent. To validate this cascade of events, the hypothesis suggests employing advanced metabolic flux assays combined with multi-omics analyses to dissect the underlying mechanisms and assess therapeutic efficacy.
