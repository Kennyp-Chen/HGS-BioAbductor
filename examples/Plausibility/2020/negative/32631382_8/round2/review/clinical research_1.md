1. Hypothesis Summary

The hypothesis suggests that in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), hyperactivated androgen receptor (AR) signaling increases the expression of hexokinase 2 (HK2), which in turn promotes aerobic glycolysis. This metabolic shift leads to an acidic and immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment (TME) that facilitates resistance to the drug sorafenib. The hypothesis further proposes that by using advanced genomic and metabolomic profiling, it is possible to identify a subgroup of HCC patients with pronounced AR/HK2 activation. For these patients, a combined therapeutic strategy using next-generation inhibitors targeted at AR and HK2—potentially delivered through gene editing (such as CRISPR/Cas9) and RNA interference—along with sorafenib, may disrupt this glycolytic reprogramming, thereby reducing tumor growth and overcoming drug resistance. The design also includes the monitoring of alternative pathways, like HGF/c-Met signaling, to address potential compensatory resistance mechanisms.

• Innovation in linking AR signaling to glycolytic reprogramming: Although both AR signaling and metabolic alterations (e.g., HK2-mediated glycolysis) have been studied separately in various cancers, the integration of these pathways in the specific context of HCC and sorafenib resistance is relatively novel and offers a fresh perspective on drug resistance mechanisms.

• Patient stratification based on AR/HK2 hyperactivation: Utilizing state-of-the-art genomic and metabolomic profiling to tailor treatment can be seen as an innovative personalized medicine approach, which is increasingly important but still emerging in HCC.

• Combined therapeutic strategy: The proposal to use next-generation inhibitors and advanced gene editing/ RNA interference techniques alongside traditional chemotherapy (sorafenib) is forward-thinking. It suggests an adaptive multifaceted intervention that might overcome resistance via multiple mechanisms simultaneously.

Overall, while elements of the hypothesis build on established findings in cancer biology, the specific integration and therapeutic strategy proposed display originality and potential clinical impact.
