1. Hypothesis Summary

The hypothesis posits that in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), using CRISPR-mediated inhibition of the circular RNA circMAT2B will interfere with its known regulatory functions (such as miRNA sponging and modulation of protein–protein interactions). This interference is proposed to disrupt the activity of the oncogenic enzyme PKM2, leading to a metabolic reprogramming from a glycolysis-dominated state to enhanced oxidative phosphorylation. As a result, lactate production is expected to decrease, which should reduce tumor microenvironment acidification, destabilize HIF-1α, and thus lower angiogenesis and metastatic potential. Overall, these tumor microenvironment and metabolic changes are theorized to improve the responsiveness of HCC tumors to sorafenib therapy. The hypothesis also outlines a plan to validate the proposed mechanism using CRISPR genome editing, RNA interference to reduce off-target effects, advanced metabolic flux analyses, and multi-omics approaches, with attention to compensatory pathways such as mTOR-mediated glycolytic regulation.

• It integrates a comprehensive multi-omics strategy to map the interplay between metabolic shifts and tumor microenvironment remodeling.
While studies have independently explored the roles of circRNAs and metabolic reprogramming in cancer, the specific hypothesis linking circMAT2B inhibition to PKM2 regulation and consequent effects on lactate production and microenvironment acidification is relatively novel and distinguishes itself by suggesting a mechanistic cascade that culminates in increased drug sensitivity.

3. Validity and Strength Evaluation

The logical flow of the hypothesis is as follows:
a. circMAT2B supports oncogenic PKM2 activity through regulatory interactions (miRNA sponging and protein–protein interactions).
b. Inhibition of circMAT2B disrupts these interactions, leading to a shift from glycolysis to oxidative phosphorylation.
c. Reduced glycolysis results in lower lactate production, which diminishes tumor microenvironment acidification.
d. A less acidic microenvironment destabilizes HIF-1α, reducing angiogenesis and metastasis, which in turn enhances sorafenib sensitivity.

Strengths:
• The hypothesized cascade integrates molecular regulation with metabolic outcomes and microenvironment changes, providing a multi-layered mechanism.
• The use of CRISPR and RNAi to dissect the role of a specific circRNA is methodologically robust and cutting-edge.
• Addressing off-target effects and ensuring the validation of parallel pathways (like mTOR-mediated regulation) shows foresight in experimental design.

Potential Weaknesses:
• The direct link between circMAT2B and PKM2 activity must be substantiated with concrete evidence. While circRNAs can regulate gene expression and protein functions, the specific mechanistic connection to PKM2 requires stronger theoretical or preliminary empirical backing.
• The cascade from metabolic reprogramming to microenvironment acidification and finally to altered angiogenesis/metastasis involves multiple complex steps. Each step is influenced by a multitude of factors, and isolating the direct contributions of circMAT2B might be challenging.
• Potential compensatory mechanisms (other than mTOR, for example, alternative metabolic pathways) are only briefly acknowledged. Their influence could undermine the predicted shift in metabolism or the downstream effects on HIF-1α and angiogenesis.
• CRISPR-mediated editing of circRNAs is still an evolving method. Ensuring specificity and efficiency in targeting a circular RNA (which might have overlapping sequences with the linear RNA counterparts) presents technical hurdles.
