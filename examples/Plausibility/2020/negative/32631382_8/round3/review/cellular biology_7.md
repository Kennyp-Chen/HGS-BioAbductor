1. Hypothesis Summary:
The hypothesis proposes that in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), using CRISPR-based inhibition to target the circular RNA circMAT2B will disrupt its regulatory roles—specifically, its miRNA sponging functions and its modulation of protein-protein interactions that promote oncogenic PKM2 activity. This disruption is expected to shift cancer cell metabolism from a glycolysis-dominant state to one that favors oxidative phosphorylation, leading to reduced lactate production. A decrease in lactate levels should in turn lessen the acidification of the tumor microenvironment, destabilize HIF-1α, and consequently reduce angiogenesis and metastatic potential. Ultimately, these changes are hypothesized to improve the tumor’s responsiveness to sorafenib therapy. The experimental approach includes CRISPR genome editing combined with RNA interference to control off-target effects, along with metabolic flux assays and multi-omics analyses to detail the molecular and metabolic alterations, while also considering the influence of parallel pathways such as mTOR-mediated regulation of glycolysis.
