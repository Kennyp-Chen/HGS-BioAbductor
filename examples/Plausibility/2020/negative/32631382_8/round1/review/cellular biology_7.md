1. Hypothesis Summary  
──────────────────────────────
The hypothesis posits that inhibiting the circular RNA circMAT2B in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) cells will lead to a metabolic shift from aerobic glycolysis (the Warburg effect) to oxidative phosphorylation. The proposed mechanism is that circMAT2B normally promotes the oncogenic activity of pyruvate kinase M2 (PKM2), thereby enhancing lactate production. Reduced lactate levels are hypothesized to decrease the stabilization of HIF-1α—a transcription factor known to drive angiogenesis and metastasis. Ultimately, these changes are suggested to not only suppress tumor progression (via reduced angiogenesis and metastasis) but also to enhance the response of HCC cells to sorafenib, a commonly used chemotherapeutic agent.

- Connecting circMAT2B inhibition directly to a decrease in PKM2 activity and a metabolic reprogramming represents a novel perspective, particularly if circMAT2B’s direct role in modulating PKM2 is not well documented in current literature.  
- The hypothesis further distinguishes itself by linking metabolic reprogramming with therapeutic sensitivity to sorafenib, which adds a translational edge.  
Overall, while research exists on individual components (e.g., the roles of PKM2 and HIF-1α in cancer metabolism, or the impact of circRNAs on cellular functions), the particular combination of these elements as they relate to circMAT2B in HCC is innovative and merits further exploration.

Conceptually, these links are plausible given the established roles of PKM2, lactate, and HIF-1α in cancer metabolism and progression.

  • The connection between lactate-mediated HIF-1α stabilization and the downstream effects is supported by existing literature; however, the extent to which a shift in metabolism quantitatively affects angiogenesis and metastasis in HCC remains to be clarified.

  • Clear experimental data showing that these metabolic changes translate into decreased HIF-1α stabilization, reduced angiogenesis/metastasis, and increased sensitivity to sorafenib.
