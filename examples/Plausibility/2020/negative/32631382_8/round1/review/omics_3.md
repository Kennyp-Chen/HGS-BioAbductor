1. Hypothesis Summary  
──────────────────────────────
The hypothesis posits that modifying the post-translational modifications (PTMs) that promote the formation of PKM2 dimers and its subsequent nuclear translocation can be leveraged to stabilize its tetrameric form—which is enzymatically active. This stabilization is proposed to interfere with PKM2’s role in promoting transcriptional activation of glycolytic genes, thereby potentially reversing resistance to sorafenib in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC).

2. Originality Assessment  
──────────────────────────────
The idea of manipulating PKM2’s oligomeric state is not entirely new; previous studies have reported the dual roles of PKM2 in metabolism and gene regulation and its association with cancer progression. However, your hypothesis introduces an innovative twist by specifically targeting the PTMs that favor dimer formation and nuclear localization as a means to restore the tetrameric state. This approach is less common in the literature concerning sorafenib resistance in HCC. Therefore, although the individual components (PKM2’s regulatory roles, its PTMs, and resistance mechanisms) are well-studied, the integration of these elements to overcome drug resistance offers a novel perspective that distinguishes your hypothesis from more conventional metabolic and resistance-targeting strategies.

──────────────────────────────

 • The hypothesis is built on the established notion that PKM2 exists in multiple oligomeric forms with distinct functions. The tetramer is associated with metabolic enzymatic activity, while the dimer and nuclear localization are linked to transcriptional regulation of genes that can favor tumor progression.  
 • By proposing to prevent PTMs that shift PKM2 toward its dimer/nuclear form, you outline a clear mechanistic chain: less nuclear PKM2 → reduced transcriptional activation of glycolytic genes → reversal of metabolic reprogramming → diminished resistance to sorafenib. This represents a logically consistent narrative.

b. Evidence and Theoretical Grounding  
 • There is supporting evidence in the literature regarding the role of PKM2 in cancer metabolism and its contribution to the transcriptional control of genes.

 • The hypothesis implicitly relies on the assumption that the PTM-driven shift from tetramer to dimer/nuclear form is a dominant driver of the transcriptional changes leading to sorafenib resistance. However, HCC is a complex and multifactorial disease; drug resistance often involves a network of signaling pathways.

4. Conclusion and Expert Recommendations  
──────────────────────────────

 • Weakness: The hypothesis does not specify which post-translational modifications are most critical in favoring the PKM2 dimer formation and nuclear translocation.

 • Weakness: While the metabolic role of PKM2 is well-documented, the hypothesis assumes a direct causal relationship between nuclear PKM2-mediated transcriptional activation and sorafenib resistance in HCC, which may oversimplify the complex resistance mechanisms.

This could involve functional assays showing that stabilization of the tetrameric form diminishes the expression of glycolytic or pro-survival genes specifically associated with drug resistance.

 • Weakness: The hypothesis does not address potential compensatory metabolic pathways or alternative mechanisms by which HCC cells might circumvent the therapy aimed at PKM2.  
 • Recommendation: Acknowledge these possible backup pathways and consider suggesting combination therapies or further molecular analyses that can distinguish the relative contribution of PKM2’s role in resistance.

 • Weakness: There is an absence of a detailed experimental plan or proposed methodology to test this hypothesis.
