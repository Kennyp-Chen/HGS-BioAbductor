1. Hypothesis Summary

The hypothesis proposes that fasting induces an increase in the levels of miR‑199a‑5p in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) cells. This rise in miR‑199a‑5p supposedly downregulates the glycolytic enzymes HK2 and PKM2, leading to a metabolic shift in tumor cells from predominantly aerobic glycolysis (the Warburg effect) toward increased reliance on oxidative phosphorylation. The altered metabolism is then proposed to reduce lactate production, which in turn diminishes lactate‑mediated immune evasion, ultimately reversing resistance to the therapeutic agent sorafenib in HCC.

2. Originality Assessment

The hypothesis is innovative in several aspects:
• It connects fasting—a dietary intervention—with molecular changes in miRNA expression (specifically miR‑199a‑5p) in cancer cells.
• It integrates metabolic reprogramming (from glycolysis to oxidative phosphorylation) with immune modulation (via reduced lactate and immune evasion).
• It goes a step further by linking these metabolic changes to an improvement in drug response, specifically the reversal of sorafenib resistance.
While individual components (such as fasting influencing cancer metabolism, or the involvement of miRNAs in regulating glycolytic enzymes) have been studied, the integrated narrative that spans fasting, miRNA‑mediated metabolic reprogramming, immune evasion, and drug resistance in HCC appears to offer a novel, multi‑faceted perspective within the field.
