1. Hypothesis Summary  
─────────────────────────────  
The hypothesis proposes that intermittent fasting (IF) leads to an increase in miR-122 levels within hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) cells. This upregulation allegedly results in the suppression of key glycolytic enzymes, prompting a metabolic shift from glycolysis to oxidative phosphorylation (OXPHOS). The shift is then said to generate higher levels of pro-apoptotic reactive oxygen species (ROS) and simultaneously reduce immunosuppressive factors in the tumor microenvironment. Together, these changes are hypothesized to improve the effectiveness of targeted therapies against HCC.
