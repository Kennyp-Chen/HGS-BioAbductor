1. Hypothesis Summary

The hypothesis posits that:
• Tumor-derived lactic acid functions as an epigenetic modulator.
• This modulation reprograms myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSCs) to adopt an enhanced immunosuppressive phenotype.
• Consequently, if lactate transport in the tumor microenvironment is blocked, MDSC differentiation would normalize.
• Normalization of MDSCs is predicted to improve the efficacy of checkpoint inhibitor immunotherapy.

──────────────────────────────
2. Originality Assessment

• Innovative Perspective: While the role of lactic acid in tumor immune evasion has been widely discussed, the specific framing of lactic acid as an epigenetic modulator of MDSCs adds a novel twist. Emphasizing epigenetic modifications expands the conventional view, which often centers on metabolic and signaling pathways.
• Distinction from Existing Studies: There have been studies linking tumor metabolism and lactate accumulation with immunosuppression. However, directly implicating lactic acid in reprogramming MDSCs through epigenetic changes—and connecting this process to the therapeutic outcome of checkpoint inhibitors—is less common. This offers a promising, albeit challenging, new direction.
• Originality Verdict: The hypothesis is relatively original. It ties together metabolic byproducts, epigenetic regulation, and immunotherapy in a novel way that could potentially extend our understanding of tumor microenvironment dynamics and resistance to immunotherapy.

  - Literature on lactic acid often focuses on metabolic stress or signaling roles; direct epigenetic modulation (e.g., histone modifications, DNA methylation changes) in MDSCs is emerging and not yet fully established.
  - The proposition to block lactate transport is logical, given that disrupting lactate gradients could affect intracellular lactate levels. However, the extent to which this intervention specifically “normalizes” MDSC differentiation remains to be firmly established.
