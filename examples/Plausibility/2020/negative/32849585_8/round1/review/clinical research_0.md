1. Hypothesis Summary  
────────────────────────────  
The hypothesis posits that if mTOR signaling is inhibited in mature neutrophils at the time they take up tumor-derived extracellular vesicles (EVs), this will block their conversion into immunosuppressive granulocytic myeloid-derived suppressor cells (g-MDSCs). The anticipated outcome is that by preventing the development of these immunosuppressive cells, tumor immune evasion will be curtailed and responses to immunotherapy will be enhanced.

2. Originality Assessment  
────────────────────────────  
• Novel Element: The hypothesis uniquely connects mTOR signaling in neutrophils with their transformation into g-MDSCs mediated by tumor-derived EVs.  
• Innovative Perspective: The idea to target a metabolic and signaling node (mTOR) during EV uptake—thereby interfering with cellular reprogramming—is a relatively innovative approach within the immuno-oncology field.  
• Relation to Existing Studies: There is established literature on mTOR’s role in numerous immune processes and on tumor EVs influencing the tumor microenvironment, but less is known regarding the specific interplay between mTOR activity in mature neutrophils and their conversion into suppressive cell types. This hypothesis therefore distinguishes itself by integrating these areas in a novel way.

 – The hypothesis could benefit from additional preclinical evidence or mechanistic studies that support the precise chain of events implied.
