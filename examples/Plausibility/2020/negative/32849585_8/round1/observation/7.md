1. Observation extraction:
 • Observation 1: Exosomal miRNAs (e.g., miR‑126‑3p) are shown to mediate key processes in tumor progression such as invasion, metastasis, angiogenesis, and modulation of the tumor microenvironment.
 • Observation 2: MDSCs are established as a major immunosuppressive cell type in the tumor microenvironment whose accumulation correlates with tumor progression and poor prognosis.
 • Observation 3: EV‐mediated intercellular communication can alter recipient cell behavior by delivering specific miRNAs that affect gene expression (e.g., changes in ADAM9, VEGF, or collagen production), thereby influencing cancer cell migration, invasion, and spheroid growth.

2. Causal analysis (individual):

 • For Observation 1:
  – Is the cause already established? Yes, many studies document that tumor‐derived EVs carrying diverse miRNAs contribute to tumor progression; this is an established paracrine signaling mechanism.
  – Would we see this observation if the hypothesis was true: If tumor-derived EVs carrying miR‑155 were suppressing IRF‑8 in myeloid cells, it might add to the broad spectrum of exosomal miRNA activities. However, the observation of EV-mediated effects on tumor progression is already explained by various EV cargos, and miR‑155 from tumor cells affecting myeloid cells is only one piece of a more complex puzzle.
  – Novel explanation? Not a missing piece, as exosomal miRNA signaling as a whole is an established mechanism and many factors likely contribute.

 • For Observation 2:
  – Is the cause already established? The accumulation and immunosuppressive function of MDSCs in tumors are well documented, with numerous studies attributing MDSC differentiation to a variety of tumor-derived factors.
  – Would we see this observation if the hypothesis was true: Yes, if miR‑155 delivered via EVs can epigenetically suppress IRF‑8, steering mature myeloid cells toward an MDSC phenotype, this could contribute to the observed expansion of MDSCs.
  – Novel explanation? While the idea that tumor signals can drive MDSC differentiation is known, proposing that EV-carried miR‑155 specifically targets IRF‑8 to achieve this is a potential specific mechanism. However, other cytokines and soluble factors have been more widely implicated, making this hypothesis a possible contributor rather than a uniquely missing explanation.

 • For Observation 3:
  – Is the cause already established? EV-mediated delivery of miRNAs affecting target cell behavior (e.g., migration, invasion) is an established concept.
  – Would we see this observation if the hypothesis was true: Yes, if tumor EVs contain miR‑155 that epigenetically represses IRF‑8 in myeloid cells, then a change in their differentiation into MDSCs could also be part of the overall effect of EV signaling on the tumor microenvironment.
  – Novel explanation? This is a specific twist on the broader phenomenon of EV-driven changes. Since other miRNAs (such as miR‑126‑3p) and factors are already known to impact recipient cells, this does not unambiguously fill a missing mechanistic gap.

3. Causal analysis (summary):
 Would we see some of the observations if the hypothesis was true: Yes, the hypothesis is consistent with observations that EVs carry functional miRNAs that can reprogram cell behavior and that tumor-derived signals promote MDSC accumulation. However, established literature already supports multiple EV-mediated and soluble factor-mediated pathways that lead to MDSC induction. Therefore, while the hypothesis could account for a subset of these observations, it does not uniquely or solely explain them—it represents one of several possible mechanisms.

4. Disproof analysis:
 Does some observations disprove the hypothesis: None of the observations directly contradict the proposed mechanism. However, the abstracts emphasize other miRNAs (e.g., miR‑126‑3p) and well‐known pathways in MDSC regulation, suggesting that while the EV-mediated transfer mechanism is plausible, there is no clear evidence pointing specifically to a miR‑155/IRF‑8 axis over established factors in driving MDSC differentiation.
