1. Observation extraction:
 • In several cancers, MDSCs accumulate in the tumor microenvironment and in peripheral blood, correlating with tumor burden, stage, and poor prognosis.  
 • MDSCs suppress antitumor immune responses by inhibiting T-cell activity, reducing NK cell and CTL function, and promoting regulatory T-cell expansion.  
 • The accumulation and activation of MDSCs have been linked to tumor progression, invasion, angiogenesis, metastasis, and resistance to immunotherapies.  
 • In liver cancer and other malignancies, pro-inflammatory cytokines and chemokines are highlighted as key drivers of MDSC expansion and recruitment.

 d. Explanation novelty: This mechanism is not currently the primary explanation in the abstracts which emphasize cytokine/chemokine signaling; therefore, it is “not a missing piece.”
