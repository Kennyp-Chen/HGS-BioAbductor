1. Hypothesis Summary

Your hypothesis suggests that tumor-derived extracellular vesicles (EVs) enriched with miR-155 and other modulators are taken up by mature neutrophils. The uptake triggers the activation of the mTOR pathway, which in turn drives metabolic reprogramming of the neutrophils and converts them into immunosuppressive granulocytic-myeloid-derived suppressor cells (granulocytic-MDSCs). Importantly, you propose that by applying temporally precise mTOR inhibition, combined with real-time live-cell imaging and single-cell multi-omics to track the reprogramming process, and by concurrently inhibiting compensatory signaling pathways that might be activated, one can block this conversion. This dual intervention strategy is aimed at reducing the generation of granulocytic-MDSCs, thereby limiting tumor immune evasion and improving patient responses to immunotherapy.

• It specifies the detailed mechanistic link between EV uptake, mTOR activation, and the metabolic reprogramming of neutrophils.  
• It incorporates a dual intervention strategy that not only targets mTOR but also aims to preempt or mitigate compensatory signaling pathways, which reflects a more sophisticated and anticipatory approach to intervention design.  
• The use of temporally precise inhibition combined with high-resolution techniques (live-cell imaging and single-cell multi-omics) offers a novel methodological angle that could provide unprecedented insight into the dynamics of neutrophil-to-MDSC conversion.
