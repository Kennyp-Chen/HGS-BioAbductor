 • The hypothesis asserts that tumor-derived extracellular vesicles (EVs) carry specific microRNAs—namely miR-21 and miR-155—that, when delivered to mature myeloid cells in the context of a supportive cytokine environment, downregulate critical transcription factors (IRF-8 and C/EBPα).  
 • This molecular reprogramming is proposed to redirect myeloid differentiation toward the generation and accumulation of immunosuppressive myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSCs).  
 • The hypothesis also posits that advanced molecular techniques, including single-cell RNA-sequencing and CRISPR-based screens, can unravel the precise mechanisms underlying this EV-mediated miRNA delivery and its downstream effects.  
 • Finally, it suggests that therapeutic strategies aimed at interrupting this EV-miRNA axis—via engineered EVs or miRNA antagonists—could restore normal myeloid differentiation and improve cancer immunotherapy outcomes.

 • The proposal to use high-resolution approaches like single-cell RNA-seq and CRISPR screens is forward-thinking, as it aims to dissect the heterogeneity and underlying molecular mechanisms with greater precision than traditional bulk analyses.

  – The emphasis on the cytokine milieu adds a layer of biological context, suggesting that the tumor microenvironment may potentiate EV-mediated effects.
