I then reviewed top abstracts for explicit or implied evidence that inhibition of DNA/histone demethylases leads to decreased H3K27me3 at cytokine gene promoters.

# literature evidence
None of the reviewed abstracts provide a direct or semantically implied link between inhibition of DNA/histone demethylases and decreased deposition of the repressive histone mark H3K27me3 at fibroblast-activating cytokine gene promoters. Instead, mechanistic studies consistently show that inhibition of H3K27 demethylases (such as JMJD3/KDM6B) tends to increase, not decrease, H3K27me3 levels at target promoters. For instance, the following abstracts are relevant but contradict the proposed relationship:

## "Histone H3K27 methyltransferase EZH2 and demethylase JMJD3 regulate hepatic stellate cells activation and liver fibrosis" (PMID: 33391480)
"JMJD3 inhibition by GSK-J4, a specific inhibitor of H3K27me3 demethylase, inhibits fibroblast activation and ameliorates experimental fibrosis."  
—This shows inhibition reduces fibroblast activation, but by increasing H3K27me3, not decreasing it.

## "Epigenetic Control of Autophagy Related Genes Transcription in Pulpitis via JMJD3" (PMID: 34434926)
"JMJD3, a histone demethylase reducing di- and tri-methylation of H3K27, regulated the expression of several key autophagy genes via demethylation of H3K27me3 at the gene promoters."
—The activity of the demethylase leads to **removal** of H3K27me3; inhibition would have the opposite (i.e., H3K27me3 increase), not a decrease.

No publications provide evidence that inhibiting DNA or histone demethylases leads to decreased H3K27me3 at cytokine gene promoters relevant to fibroblast activation.

Answer: False
