1. Hypothesis Summary:
The hypothesis proposes that in cholangiocarcinoma, cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs) induce stiffening of the extracellular matrix (ECM). This increased stiffness, through a process known as mechanotransduction, activates the YAP/TAZ signaling pathway in tumor cells. The activation of this pathway then leads to epigenetic modifications that enhance cancer stem cell properties and contribute to resistance against chemotherapy.

While several components of the hypothesis (CAF-induced ECM remodeling, YAP/TAZ activation in response to mechanical cues, and subsequent impacts on tumor cell behavior) have been areas of active investigation in various types of cancers, integrating these elements into a single, cohesive mechanism specific to cholangiocarcinoma provides a novel angle. The linkage of mechanotransduction to epigenetic alterations as a driver of cancer stemness and chemoresistance is emerging but not yet widely established in the context of cholangiocarcinoma, indicating that the hypothesis offers innovative perspectives compared to more well-trodden research avenues in other tumor types.

 • Mechanical cues from a stiffened ECM have been reported to activate YAP/TAZ, transcription co-activators that respond to mechanotransduction.
 • YAP/TAZ activity has been implicated in promoting cancer stem cell-like properties and chemoresistance in various cancers.
The linkage to epigenetic changes is an area of growing interest and theoretical support, though establishing direct causality from mechanical stress through YAP/TAZ to specific epigenetic modifications remains challenging.
