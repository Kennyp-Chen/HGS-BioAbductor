1. Hypothesis Summary

The hypothesis proposes that in cholangiocarcinoma, cancer-associated fibroblast (CAF)–derived exosomes serve as vehicles of epigenetic reprogramming. In particular, when isocitrate dehydrogenase (IDH) mutations are present, this reprogramming promotes epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EMT) and chemoresistance. As a therapeutic strategy, it suggests that inhibiting the release of these CAF-derived exosomes might re-sensitize tumors to chemotherapy.

  - The concept that extracellular vesicles (exosomes) can mediate epigenetic changes.
  - The impact of IDH mutations on tumor biology.
  - Linking these elements to EMT and chemoresistance in cholangiocarcinoma.
  
• Innovative Perspective: While each of these elements (CAFs, exosome-mediated signaling, epigenetics, and IDH mutations) has been explored separately in cancer research, combining them into a unified mechanism in cholangiocarcinoma represents an innovative and potentially transformative approach. This integrated view could lead to novel therapeutic strategies.
