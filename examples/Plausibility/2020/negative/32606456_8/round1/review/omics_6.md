1. Hypothesis Summary  
─────────────────────────────  
Your hypothesis proposes that exosomes secreted by cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs) contain specific non-coding RNAs (ncRNAs) that, when transferred to cholangiocytes, induce epigenetic modifications. These modifications subsequently drive epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EMT) and promote stemness. In the context of cholangiocarcinoma, this cascade of events leads to chemoresistance and increased tumor heterogeneity.

2. Originality Assessment  
─────────────────────────────  
The concept of cellular crosstalk via exosomal transfer of digital messages, such as ncRNAs, is well-documented in cancer research. Similarly, EMT and stemness acquisition as mechanisms of chemoresistance and tumor heterogeneity have robust literature support. However, the specific focus on CAF-derived exosomes in cholangiocarcinoma, and the chain of events from ncRNA-mediated epigenetic modifications to EMT and stemness, introduces a nuanced perspective. This hypothesis is innovative in its integration of these elements specifically within cholangiocarcinoma. Its originality lies in applying known mechanisms (exosome-mediated ncRNA signaling and epigenetic regulation) to the particular microenvironment of cholangiocarcinoma, which could open new avenues for targeted therapeutic interventions.

• EMT and stemness are established contributors to chemoresistance and tumor heterogeneity.
