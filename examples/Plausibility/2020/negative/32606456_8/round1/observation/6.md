1. Observation extraction:
• Observation 1: In cholangiocarcinoma, cells acquire EMT biomarkers and a pro-invasive phenotype. Although complete EMT (i.e., transformation of cholangiocytes into stromal cells) is not observed, there is a clear shift toward invasive behavior that has been attributed broadly to signals from the tumor stroma.  
• Observation 2: In the tumor microenvironment of CCA, cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs) produce an excess of stiff extracellular matrix (ECM) that can lead to immune exclusion and contribute to resistance (e.g., against immunotherapy).  
• Observation 3: CCA displays a desmoplastic microenvironment rich in CAFs, immune cells, and various soluble factors. These elements interplay to promote tumor aggressiveness, proliferation, chemoresistance, and heterogeneity.

2. Causal analysis (individual):

• For Observation 1:
 a. The acquisition of EMT markers and pro-invasive behavior is generally attributed to a variety of factors from the tumor stroma (e.g., cytokines, growth factors, microRNAs), a role that is already recognized.
 b. Would we see this observation if the hypothesis was true: Yes, if CAF-derived exosomes carrying specific non-coding RNAs induce epigenetic modifications that promote EMT and stemness in cholangiocytes, an increase in EMT biomarkers and a shift toward invasiveness would be expected.
 c. However, this explanation is not entirely novel because multiple tumor stroma components (soluble signaling molecules, other exosomal contents, and microenvironmental cues) are already known to contribute to such phenotypic shifts. 
 d. Conclusion for Observation 1: not a missing piece.

• For Observation 2:
 a. The link between CAFs, ECM stiffening, and immune exclusion is already established as a contributing factor to therapy resistance.
 b. Would we see this observation if the hypothesis was true: Not directly—while the hypothesis implicates CAF-derived exosomes in inducing EMT and stemness in cholangiocytes, the stiff ECM produced by CAFs serves as a physical barrier that limits drug and immune cell infiltration. The direct role of non-coding RNAs altering cholangiocyte behavior does not inherently explain ECM stiffening.
 c. Thus, even though CAFs are involved in both scenarios, the mechanism behind ECM stiffening is already clearly attributed to other factors produced by CAFs.
 d. Conclusion for Observation 2: not a missing piece.

• For Observation 3:
 a. The overall influence of the desmoplastic tumor microenvironment on tumor aggressiveness and chemoresistance is well understood, with factors such as cytokines, CAF secretions, and immune cell interactions playing established roles.
 b. Would we see this observation if the hypothesis was true: Yes, the hypothesis could account for enhanced tumor heterogeneity and chemoresistance if CAF-derived exosomes trigger epigenetic changes leading to EMT and stemness. These changes could, in turn, amplify tumor aggressiveness within the complex TME.
 c. However, this mechanism is one among several potential contributing factors, and many of the established components of the TME already provide a plausible explanation.
 d. Conclusion for Observation 3: not a missing piece.

3. Causal analysis (summary):
 Would we see some of the observations if the hypothesis was true: Yes, if CAF-derived exosomes carrying specific non-coding RNAs induce epigenetic changes, it could lead to EMT acquisition, stemness, and increased chemoresistance. However, these outcomes are also explained by multiple other well-established signals within the tumor microenvironment. Thus, while the hypothesis could contribute to some of these observations, its novelty is limited by the presence of alternative, widely recognized mechanisms.

4. Disproof analysis:
 Does some observations disprove the hypothesis: None of the observations directly contradict the hypothesis. All observed phenomena (EMT markers acquisition, ECM stiffening, and complex microenvironment interactions) remain consistent with a potential role of CAFs. However, they also have established explanations that do not exclusively require the exosomal non-coding RNA mechanism.

5. Conclusion:
 Although the hypothesis is consistent with several features of the tumor microenvironment in cholangiocarcinoma, the causes of these features are already established with multiple corroborating factors. The proposed mechanism of CAF-derived exosomes carrying specific non-coding RNAs is not uniquely required to explain the observations and is one of several potential contributors.
