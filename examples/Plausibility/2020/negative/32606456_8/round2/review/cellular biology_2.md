1. Hypothesis Summary

The hypothesis posits that in cholangiocarcinoma, chronic inflammatory signals trigger the activation of cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs), leading to extracellular matrix (ECM) remodeling and stiffening. This stiffer ECM, along with the release of cytokines and growth factors, activates mechanotransduction pathways—most notably YAP/TAZ, alongside pathways such as TGF-β signaling. This activation is proposed to induce a partial epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EMT) and foster the emergence of cancer stem cell–like properties, while simultaneously promoting an immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment. The cascade is suggested to be central in developing both chemoresistance and immunotherapy resistance. To interrogate this hypothesis, the authors advocate for the use of advanced experimental approaches (e.g., 3D culture systems, biomechanical sensors, high-resolution imaging, and AI-powered data analysis) to track ECM stiffness and YAP/TAZ dynamics, and ultimately to guide the development of combination therapeutic strategies that target YAP/TAZ and its compensatory pathways.
