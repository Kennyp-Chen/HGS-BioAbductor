1. Hypothesis Summary

The hypothesis posits that in cholangiocarcinoma, chronic inflammatory signals activate cancer‐associated fibroblasts (CAFs). These activated CAFs perform dual roles by remodeling and stiffening the extracellular matrix (ECM) and secreting a rich mixture of cytokines and growth factors. The resultant mechanical alterations, along with biochemical cues from the CAF secretome, trigger mechanotransduction in tumor cells leading to the activation of the YAP/TAZ signaling pathway. Once activated, YAP/TAZ is proposed to recruit or activate key epigenetic regulators (e.g., EZH2 and various HDACs), causing modifications in chromatin structure that drive the emergence of cancer stem cell traits and contribute to chemoresistance. The hypothesis also advocates the use of contemporary experimental platforms such as 3D organoid models, atomic force microscopy for quantifying ECM stiffness, and single-cell epigenetic analyses to rigorously dissect this pathway and identify potential therapeutic targets.
