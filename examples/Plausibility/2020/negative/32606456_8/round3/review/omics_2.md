1. Hypothesis Summary  
─────────────────────────────  
The hypothesis posits that in cholangiocarcinoma, chronic inflammatory signals activate cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs), which remodel and stiffen the extracellular matrix (ECM). This stiffer biomechanical milieu, combined with concurrent cytokine and growth factor signaling, activates mechanosensitive pathways mediated by YAP/TAZ and TGF-β. Activation of these pathways is proposed to induce a partial epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EMT), endowing tumor cells with stem cell-like properties and fostering an immune-suppressive microenvironment. Together, these changes promote resistance to both chemotherapy and immunotherapy. The hypothesis also outlines a sophisticated experimental strategy that involves 3D culture models, precise biomechanical sensors, high-resolution imaging, and AI-driven data analysis to dynamically track and dissect these processes, with the ultimate goal of identifying novel combination therapies targeting YAP/TAZ and related pathways.
