1. Hypothesis Summary:
The hypothesis posits that in IDH-mutant intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma (iCCA), the accumulation of the oncometabolite 2-hydroxyglutarate (2-HG) drives a specific epigenetic reprogramming at the promoters of genes encoding fibroblast-activating cytokines. This reprogramming is proposed to involve increased enrichment of activating histone marks (H3K4me3 and H3K27ac) and a decrease in the repressive H3K27me3 mark. The resulting upregulation of cytokines, such as TGF-β and IL-6, supposedly leads to the recruitment and activation of cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs), particularly those of hepatic stellate cell origin. These activated CAFs then remodel the tumor stroma and promote an extracellular matrix that confers chemoresistance. The investigation is planned to utilize advanced methodologies including ChIP-seq for locus-specific epigenetic profiling, scRNA-seq and spatial transcriptomics for CAF heterogeneity analysis, and CRISPR-based epigenome editing for functional studies.
