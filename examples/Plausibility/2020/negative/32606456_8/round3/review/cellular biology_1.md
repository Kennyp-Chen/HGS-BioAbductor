1. Hypothesis Summary
────────────────────────────────────────
Your hypothesis posits that in IDH-mutant intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma (iCCA), the accumulation of the oncometabolite 2-hydroxyglutarate (2-HG) drives a targeted epigenetic reprogramming at the promoters of fibroblast-activating cytokine genes. This epigenetic remodeling is hypothesized to upregulate the expression and secretion of cytokines such as TGF-β and IL-6. These cytokines in turn are believed to recruit and activate distinct cancer-associated fibroblast (CAF) subpopulations—predominantly those derived from hepatic stellate cells—which then remodel the tumor stroma to promote chemoresistance. The proposed study will leverage cutting-edge techniques, including ChIP-seq for locus-specific epigenetic profiling, scRNA-seq coupled with spatial transcriptomics for characterizing CAF heterogeneity, and CRISPR-based epigenome editing to functionally perturb the proposed regulatory cascade.

Additionally, the proposed use of state-of-the-art techniques (e.g., spatial transcriptomics paired with scRNA-seq and CRISPR epigenome editing) underscores a fresh approach to dissect a multi-cellular interplay that has not been widely explored in this context. Thus, the hypothesis offers an original perspective by linking specific epigenetic events to tumor–stroma interactions and chemoresistance in iCCA.

Nonetheless, potential challenges include:
 - The risk of confounding global versus locus-specific epigenetic effects of 2-HG.
 - The need to delineate whether the observed alterations in cytokine gene expression are a direct consequence of epigenetic remodeling or secondary to other signaling events.
 - The multifactorial nature of chemoresistance, where other pathways and microenvironmental factors might also play critical roles.
