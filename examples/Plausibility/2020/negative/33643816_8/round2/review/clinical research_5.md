1. Hypothesis Summary  
─────────────────────────────  
The hypothesis posits that certain long noncoding RNAs (lncRNAs), which are found both in the nucleus and cytoplasm (dual-localized), drive tumor immune evasion through two parallel mechanisms. In the nucleus, these lncRNAs reprogram transcription, while in the cytoplasm they sequester regulatory microRNAs (miRNAs). Together, these actions disrupt cytokine-mediated immune cell recruitment and antigen processing. The approach proposed is to use advanced, nanoparticle-based delivery platforms to administer compartment-targeted siRNAs or antisense oligonucleotides (ASOs). These therapeutics would selectively modulate the functions of the nuclear and cytoplasmic lncRNAs, ultimately aiming to reverse the immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment in cancers that are resistant to current immunotherapies.

2. Originality Assessment

This hypothesis offers an innovative perspective in several respects:  
• It emphasizes the dual (nuclear and cytoplasmic) functionality of lncRNAs in mediating immune escape—an angle that extends beyond studies focusing on single compartment functions.  
• The integration of compartment-specific modulation via advanced nanoparticle-based delivery systems is a forward-looking approach that goes beyond conventional RNA-targeting strategies.  
While aspects of lncRNA involvement in tumor biology and immune evasion have been previously noted, the specific idea of concurrently reprogramming nuclear transcriptional events and cytoplasmic miRNA dynamics for immunotherapy resistance is distinctive. The therapeutic strategy employing compartment-targeted intervention represents a conceptual advancement that could open new avenues for research, provided that the underlying mechanisms are precisely delineated and validated.

Strengths:
