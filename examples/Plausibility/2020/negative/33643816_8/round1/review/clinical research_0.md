1. Hypothesis Summary

The hypothesis proposes that small molecules derived from phytochemicals can (a) alter the structure of oncogenic long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) in the nucleus, thereby disrupting gene regulatory networks that promote tumor growth, and (b) simultaneously activate mitochondrial lncRNA‐mediated apoptotic signaling pathways. Through this dual action—modifying nuclear gene regulation and engaging mitochondrial apoptotic mechanisms—the approach is posited to reprogram tumor metabolism and improve the effectiveness of conventional chemotherapies.

While there is ongoing research into the anticancer effects of phytochemicals and the functional roles of lncRNAs, proposing that structurally remodeling oncogenic lncRNAs and triggering mitochondrial pathways via lncRNAs through the same class of compounds represents a fresh dual-mechanism approach.

• Distinction from Existing Studies: Many studies target oncogenes or examine phytochemicals separately in the context of chemotherapy enhancement. Integrating oncogenic lncRNA modulation along with targeting mitochondrial lncRNA-driven apoptosis provides a nuanced strategy that may distinguish this hypothesis from more conventional models of phytochemical activity in cancer therapy.
