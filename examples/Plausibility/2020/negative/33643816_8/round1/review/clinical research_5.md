1. Hypothesis Summary  
─────────────────────────────  
The hypothesis proposes that certain long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) which are present in both the nucleus and cytoplasm play a dual role in tumor immune evasion. In the nucleus, these lncRNAs allegedly reprogram transcriptional profiles that could contribute to an immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment. Simultaneously, in the cytoplasm, the same lncRNAs act as sponges for specific regulatory microRNAs (miRNAs) that normally would contribute to antitumor immune responses. Consequently, targeting these dual-localized lncRNAs with precision—addressing both their nuclear and cytoplasmic functions—is posited to restore antitumor immunity and potentially overcome resistance to current immunotherapies in cancer treatment.
