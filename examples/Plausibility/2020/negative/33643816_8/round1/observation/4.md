 • Observation 1: Numerous studies document that various lncRNAs are dysregulated in cancer. Their altered expression correlates with disease recurrence, metastasis, and prognosis, and in some cases overexpression promotes matrix invasion and tumor growth.

 • Observation 3: In functional studies, however, complete knockout (loss-of-function) of MALAT1 in both human tumor cells and mouse models did not lead to noticeable effects on proliferation, cell cycle progression, or overall tumor phenotype.

However, the observation from knockout studies strongly indicates that ablation of MALAT1 does not translate into significant phenotypic changes. This suggests that targeting its triple helix is unlikely to produce the profound reprogramming of tumor metabolism and reduction of drug resistance as postulated. In short, while some aspects of lncRNA dysfunction are in place, the hypothesis does not propose a novel mechanism for these observations but rather an intervention where other, better‐characterized explanations exist.

If complete loss of MALAT1 does not alter tumor cell viability or progression, it becomes unlikely that a targeted modulation of its triple helix structure would generate the significant anti-tumor immune response or overcome drug resistance, thereby contradicting the hypothesis.
