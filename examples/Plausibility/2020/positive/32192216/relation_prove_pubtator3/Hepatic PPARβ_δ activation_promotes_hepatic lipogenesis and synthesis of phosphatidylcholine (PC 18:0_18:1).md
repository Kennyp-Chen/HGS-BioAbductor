# Search Strategy and Reasoning
The search began with the minimal set of MeSH terms and keywords, such as "PPARβ/δ activation", "hepatic lipogenesis", and "phosphatidylcholine." The goal was to identify literature that directly or indirectly connects hepatic PPARβ/δ (PPARdelta) activation to hepatic lipogenesis and the synthesis of phosphatidylcholine, particularly PC 18:0/18:1, and whether there is evidence that this relationship is promoting (i.e., activation increases these processes). Because "hepatic PPARβ/δ" and "phosphatidylcholine synthesis" are closely associated mechanistically, I also refined the search to combinations like "PPAR beta hepatic phosphatidylcholine" and reviewed the top abstracts and, if promising, the full text.

# literature evidence
## "Ligand dependent interaction between PC-TP and PPARdelta mitigates diet-induced hepatic steatosis in male mice" (PMID: 37173315)
**Supporting sentence from the full text:**
"This phenomenon is regulated by a specific PC (PC (18:0/18:1)) known to be generated in the liver by processes transcriptionally controlled by PPARdelta. In the muscle, PC 18:0/18:1 activates PPARalpha, culminating in increased fatty acid uptake and utilization. The consequences of this liver-skeletal muscle axis controlled by PPARdelta is increased endurance during exercise, sensitization to glucose and insulin, and a decrease in lipid content in both the liver and the muscle."

This establishes that hepatic PPARδ activation transcriptionally controls the generation (synthesis) of PC 18:0/18:1 in the liver, supporting the claim that PPARβ/δ activation promotes hepatic synthesis of this phosphatidylcholine species.

Answer: True
