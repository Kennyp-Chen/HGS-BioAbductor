# Search Strategy and Reasoning
I searched PubMed using the minimal MeSH terms and functional equivalents: "PPAR alpha AND hydroxymethylglutaryl-CoA synthase 2". This directly targets the molecular factors involved – PPARα (peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor alpha) and hydroxymethylglutaryl-CoA synthase 2 (HMGCS2), which is the pivotal enzyme for ketogenesis. The abstracts retrieved were assessed for any semantic connection showing PPARα stimulation leading to increased HMGCS2 and thus ketogenesis. One recent paper (PMID: 38928261) specifically addressed this metabolic axis in human tissue, so I then examined its full text for unambiguous evidence of the relationship.

# literature evidence
## "Intestinal Ketogenesis and Permeability." PMID: 38928261
From the Introduction:  
"These effects require intact signaling from the peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor alpha (PPARα) that regulates the central pathways of fatty acid oxidation (FAO), such as ketogenesis and beta-oxidation [16]. ...In a previous study from our laboratory, we showed that an HFD resulted in markedly raised jejunal expression of the ketogenesis rate-limiting enzyme mitochondrial 3-hydroxy-3-methylglutaryl-CoA synthase (HMGCS2), resulting in the intestinal mucosa producing ketone bodies β-Hydroxybutyrate (βHB), not under starvation but under consumption of a diet with high-fat content [18,19]."  
And, explicitly:  
"HMGCS is activated via the free fatty acid binding nuclear receptor PPAR-α, and it is a key enzyme in ketone body synthesis..." (Abstract)

Therefore, the literature supports that PPARα **stimulates** ketogenesis via increased HMGCS2 expression.

Answer: True
