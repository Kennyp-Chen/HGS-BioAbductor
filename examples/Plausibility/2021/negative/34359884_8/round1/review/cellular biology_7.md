1. Hypothesis Summary  
─────────────────────────────  
The hypothesis posits that in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), inhibiting miR‐210—whose expression is induced by hypoxia‐inducible factors (HIFs)—can restore normal mitochondrial respiration. This restoration would lead to an increase in reactive oxygen species (ROS) production, which in turn would re-sensitize hypoxic tumor cells to tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs), thereby potentially overcoming drug resistance. Key components include:  
• The role of HIFs in upregulating miR‐210 under hypoxic conditions.  
• The impact of miR‐210 on mitochondrial respiration and ROS regulation.  
• The link between restored mitochondrial function/ROS levels and improved sensitivity to TKIs in resistant HCC cells.
