1. Hypothesis Summary:
The hypothesis posits that in hypoxic hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), inhibiting the MCT4 transporter—which mediates lactate export—can reduce the high levels of extracellular lactate that contribute to immunosuppression. This reduction is expected to restore the cytotoxic functions of natural killer (NK) cells and CD8⁺ T cells. Additionally, by lowering lactate-induced immunomodulation, PD-L1 expression would be downregulated. Together, these effects are anticipated to sensitize tumors to immune checkpoint inhibitors and help overcome drug resistance.

Although there is established knowledge on lactate’s immunosuppressive effects and on checkpoint inhibitor resistance, this hypothesis distinguishes itself by proposing a targeted metabolic intervention (MCT4 inhibition) as a dual strategy—impacting both immune cell function and checkpoint signaling pathways. This integrated approach is relatively novel and offers a promising avenue for overcoming immunotherapy resistance.
