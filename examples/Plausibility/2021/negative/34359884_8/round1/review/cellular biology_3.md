1. Hypothesis Summary  
─────────────────────────────  
The hypothesis posits that in hypoxic hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) cells, selective inhibition of the enzyme PHGDH (phosphoglycerate dehydrogenase) will:  
• Reduce NADPH production, which is crucial for maintaining redox homeostasis.  
• Lead to increased reactive oxygen species (ROS) levels, thereby triggering immunogenic cell death (ICD) in these tumor cells.  
• Enhance the tumor’s susceptibility to immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICI) by creating a more immunostimulatory tumor microenvironment.  
• Achieve these effects without significantly impeding the metabolism of effector T cells, suggesting a therapeutic window where tumor cells are selectively targeted while immune cells remain functional.
