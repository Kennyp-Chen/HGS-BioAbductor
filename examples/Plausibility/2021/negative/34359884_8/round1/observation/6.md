 • Observation 1: HCC typically exhibits metabolic reprogramming – including increased aerobic glycolysis, enhanced de novo lipid synthesis, glutamine depletion, and oxidative metabolism imbalance – which supports rapid tumor growth.

 • Observation 3: In hepatoma cells with mitochondrial defects, LDHB downregulation is linked to lactate accumulation and altered PDH activity, with monocarboxylate transporters (notably MCT1 in this study) modulating lactate dynamics.

However, the key component of reprogramming hepatic stellate cells into CAFs – and thereby driving drug resistance – is not addressed by these studies. The metabolic adaptations and lactate accumulation observed are more conventionally explained by intrinsic oncogenic reprogramming, rather than by a paracrine mechanism affecting the stroma. Thus, the hypothesis does not fill a missing mechanistic gap for these observations.

In fact, the studies focus on the cancer cell intrinsic metabolic changes and already established pathways (e.g., LDHB modulation and glycolysis), with no data on the interaction with hepatic stellate cells or on the expression/role of MCT4 – the suggested target for disrupting lactate export.
