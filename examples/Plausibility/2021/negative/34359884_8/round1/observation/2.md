Observation from Abstract 2: m6A-mediated stabilization of SSP transcripts is linked to sorafenib resistance.

Thus, while the hypothesis ties metabolism to epigenetic changes, it doesn’t directly account for the m6A mechanism that has already been identified, meaning it does not provide a clearly novel explanation.

Observation from Abstract 3: Metabolic reprogramming underlies cancer proliferation and drug resistance.  
 a. This is an established observation in cancer biology where metabolic shifts are known to support tumor growth and therapy resistance.  
 b. Would the hypothesis account for this?  
  “Would we see this observation if the hypothesis was true:” Yes, since HIF-mediated activation of the SSP and subsequent metabolic and epigenetic cascades would be a form of metabolic reprogramming. However, this observation is broad and can be explained by many factors; therefore, the proposed metabolic-epigenetic axis is just one of several existing explanations, not an unrecognized missing link.

3. Causal analysis (summary):  
 “Would we see some of the observations if the hypothesis was true:” Yes. A hypoxic environment activating HIF could plausibly enhance the SSP and increase one‐carbon metabolism, thereby elevating SAM production and fostering epigenetic gene repression. However, the observations from the abstracts already have established mechanistic explanations (epigenetic modifications in general, m6A-mediated regulation, and broad metabolic reprogramming) that account for the phenomena without necessarily implicating a specific HIF–SSP–SAM axis.

4. Disproof analysis:  
 “Does some observations disprove the hypothesis:” None of the observations directly contradict the hypothesis. Instead, they show that metabolic and epigenetic alterations are involved in HCC resistance. However, the specific chain proposed (hypoxia → HIF → SSP → one‐carbon metabolism → SAM → epigenetic repression of drug sensitivity genes) is not distinctly evidenced or isolated from other established pathways (like m6A regulation) in these studies.
