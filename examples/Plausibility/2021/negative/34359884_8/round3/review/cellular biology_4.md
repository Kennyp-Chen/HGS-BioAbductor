1. Hypothesis Summary

The hypothesis posits that under hypoxic conditions in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), activation of HIF leads to metabolic reprogramming that results in increased lactate production. This lactate acidifies the tumor microenvironment and, via engagement of lactate receptors (primarily GPR81), drives tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs) to adopt an M2, pro-tumorigenic phenotype. In the same acidic milieu, macrophages upregulate the carbonic anhydrase CA12, enhancing their survival and secretion of pro-tumor chemokines like CCL8. The secreted cytokines from these M2 macrophages (notably IL-10 and TGF-β) then act paracrine on tumor cells, triggering the induction of the serine synthesis pathway through upregulation of PHGDH. This metabolic shift increases NADPH production, which in turn sustains redox homeostasis and contributes to resistance against targeted therapies (such as TKIs and ICIs). The hypothesis also acknowledges that parallel metabolic pathways—like AMPK-mediated autophagy—might be active and interact with these processes. It further proposes the use of advanced techniques (single-cell RNA sequencing, spatial metabolomics, multiplex cytokine profiling, and CRISPR screening) to dissect and potentially target these mechanisms.

2. Originality Assessment

The hypothesis integrates several well-studied components in tumor biology (HIF-driven metabolic reprogramming, lactate’s role in acidifying the microenvironment, TAM polarization, cytokine-mediated signal transduction, and metabolic adaptations like enhanced serine synthesis for redox balance) in a novel, multi-step mechanistic cascade specific to HCC. While hypoxia and lactate's effects on the tumor microenvironment and macrophage polarization are established, linking these events to a coordinated upregulation of CA12 and the induction of the serine synthesis pathway (via PHGDH), with subsequent therapy resistance, provides a fresh perspective. The incorporation of detailed intervention points using state-of-the-art methodologies further underscores its innovative angle. Thus, it offers an integrative view that, while building on established knowledge, attempts to reveal new therapeutic targets and interdependencies in HCC.
