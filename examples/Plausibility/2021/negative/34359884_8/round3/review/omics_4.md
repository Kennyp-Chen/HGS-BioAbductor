1. Hypothesis Summary

The hypothesis proposes that under hypoxic conditions in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), HIF-mediated metabolic reprogramming leads to increased lactate production, thereby acidifying the tumor microenvironment. This acidification, along with lactate receptor engagement predominantly through GPR81, drives the polarization of tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs) to an M2 phenotype. In this acidic milieu, CA12 is upregulated in macrophages, promoting their survival and enhancing the secretion of pro-tumorigenic chemokines (e.g., CCL8). The M2 macrophages then secrete cytokines such as IL-10 and TGF-β, which act in a paracrine manner on tumor cells to induce the serine synthesis pathway via upregulation of PHGDH. The increased serine metabolism, in turn, boosts NADPH production, maintains redox homeostasis, and contributes to resistance against targeted therapies (e.g., TKIs and ICIs). The hypothesis also recognizes that other metabolic pathways, such as AMPK-mediated autophagy, might be concurrently active. It proposes using advanced tools—single-cell RNA sequencing, spatial metabolomics, multiplex cytokine profiling, and CRISPR screening—to dissect these interrelated mechanisms and identify precise intervention points to overcome therapy resistance.
