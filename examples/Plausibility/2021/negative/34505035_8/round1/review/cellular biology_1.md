1. Hypothesis Summary

The hypothesis proposes that continuously tracking the ALBI (Albumin–Bilirubin) grade in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) patients undergoing immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI) therapy can act as a dynamic biomarker. It suggests that fluctuations in the ALBI grade not only correlate with therapeutic response but also forecast the emergence of immune-related hepatotoxicity, by reflecting the real-time status of the hepatic immune microenvironment.

─────────────────────────────  
2. Originality Assessment

• Innovation Perspective:  
The concept of using the ALBI score—a marker traditionally utilized to assess liver function—as a predictive tool for response and toxicity during immunotherapy introduces a novel dynamic approach. In contrast to static baseline measurements, continuous monitoring might capture real-time alterations in liver health and immune status.

• Relation to Existing Studies:  
Although immune-related adverse events (irAEs) are well-studied, and the ALBI grade is established in evaluating liver function in HCC, combining these parameters dynamically to predict both treatment response and toxicity is less commonly explored. This synthesis of liver function tests and immunological outcomes represents an innovative integration that stands apart from many existing cross-sectional studies in the field.

Since immune checkpoint inhibition can provoke both antitumor immune responses and immune-mediated toxicity, monitoring a liver-specific parameter might indeed provide insights into the dual aspects of therapy.

• Theoretical Grounding:  
The underlying theory is that liver function dynamics (reflected in albumin and bilirubin levels) could serve as an indirect indicator of hepatic immune alterations. This idea is theoretically supported by known links between liver inflammation, immune responses, and hepatic biomarker fluctuations. However, the precise mechanistic underpinning of why ALBI changes would predict ICI response versus immune-related hepatotoxicity requires further elucidation. For instance, the direct molecular or cellular mediators connecting ALBI grade changes to the specific immune events are not fully defined.

Current literature provides examples where static ALBI scores correlate with outcomes in HCC. Yet, evidence supporting the predictive power of dynamic ALBI monitoring during immunotherapy remains preliminary or anecdotal. The hypothesis would benefit from referencing preliminary studies, retrospective analyses, or exploratory clinical data that suggest such correlations.

The dual prediction claim, however, might necessitate a more refined analytical approach to segregate overlapping signals (i.e., distinguishing beneficial immune activation from toxic adverse effects).
