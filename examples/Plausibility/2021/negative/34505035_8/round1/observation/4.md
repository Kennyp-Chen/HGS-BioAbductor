1. Observation extraction:
 • Abstract 1: A higher ALBI grade (reflecting poorer liver function) is strongly associated with worse overall survival in HCC patients receiving TACE. The study also notes that factors like age, tumor size, and alcohol-related liver disease contribute to heterogeneity across ALBI grades.
 • Abstract 2: Both albumin grade and modified ALBI grade are shown to be effective at prognostic stratification of HCC patients across different stages and treatments (including TACE, curative surgery, ablation, and systemic therapies); however, differences between the grading systems are minimal, indicating that they perform similarly in practice.
 • Abstract 3: ALBI and mALBI grades are confirmed as independent predictors of overall survival for HCC patients undergoing various anticancer therapies, with the mALBI grade further differentiating prognostic groups based on liver functional reserve.

 • For ALBI and mALBI predicting overall survival in patients receiving anticancer therapy:
  – Cause established? Yes. Their prognostic value is attributed to reflecting hepatic function.

3. Causal analysis (summary):
 Would we see some of the observations if the hypothesis was true: Although the hypothesis could, in theory, add an explanation by suggesting that optimal ALBI scores enhance anti-tumor immunity, the observations from these abstracts are entirely consistent with the known role of ALBI as a surrogate for liver functional reserve. There are no specific immune response measures or ICI efficacy data presented; thus, the apparent prognostic associations are already understood in terms of liver function rather than the novel mechanism of immune modulation.

4. Disproof analysis:
 Does some observations disprove the hypothesis: None of the observations directly contradict the hypothesis; however, they do not provide any evidence for immune modulation. In all cases, the established explanation (liver reserve) suffices to explain the outcomes, leaving little room for the proposed direct immune-modulatory mechanism to be the causal factor.

5. Conclusion:
 Each observation is well explained by the established role of ALBI grading in reflecting liver function reserve, and there is no supporting evidence (or measured immune outcomes) to indicate that ALBI directly modulates the hepatic immune microenvironment and alters ICI efficacy. Other explanations, specifically the prognostic value due to liver functional status, are more likely.
