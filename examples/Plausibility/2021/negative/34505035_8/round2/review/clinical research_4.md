1. Hypothesis Summary:
The hypothesis posits that the ALBI score—traditionally used as a marker of liver functional reserve—can be expanded beyond assessing hepatic synthetic capacity by integrating advanced omics profiling and digital health monitoring. This enhanced immune microenvironment is then proposed to indirectly contribute to superior clinical outcomes in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) patients receiving immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI) therapy, by both boosting antitumor immune responses and reducing risks of immune-related hepatotoxicity.

This hypothesis brings together several innovative components:
• It integrates traditional liver function assessment (i.e., the ALBI score) with state-of-the-art technologies (omics profiling and digital health monitoring) to capture a more holistic picture of the liver’s status.
• It proposes an unconventional link between liver synthetic function and the hepatic immune microenvironment—a concept that is not widely explored in standard clinical practice or current literature.
• The hypothesis innovatively connects nutritional and oxidative balance with immune cell function (specifically dendritic cell activation and T-cell priming), and eventually with therapy outcomes in the domain of immunotherapy for HCC.
Thus, the approach is relatively original and distinguishes itself from the existing paradigms that usually treat liver function and immune responses as separate domains.

This immune enhancement is then connected to better responses to immunotherapy and a reduction in immune-related hepatotoxicity.

• Mechanistic Detail: The link between the ALBI score (a surrogate of liver synthetic function) and the immune microenvironment is hypothesized but not underpinned by extensive direct evidence. The steps connecting nutritional/redox status to dendritic cell activation need deeper mechanistic explanation.

However, liver disease, especially HCC, is subject to multiple cofounders (e.g., viral coinfection, metabolic syndrome), which might affect both liver function and immune responses. Addressing these potential confounders or proposing stratification criteria would enhance the scientific rigor.
