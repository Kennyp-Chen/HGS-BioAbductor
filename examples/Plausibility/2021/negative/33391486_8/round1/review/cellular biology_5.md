 The hypothesis proposes that under stress conditions, cells produce tRNA-derived fragments (tRFs) that are packaged into exosomes. These exosomes are then delivered to recipient immune cells, where the tRFs interfere with or modulate the translational machinery. This translational alteration specifically reduces the synthesis of antiviral proteins, ultimately leading to an environment that favors viral replication.

However, combining these elements—specifically the stress-induced production of tRNA-derived fragments, their packaging into exosomes, and their targeted effect on immune cell translation to diminish antiviral protein synthesis—is a distinctive and integrative perspective. While aspects of each component have been studied separately, linking them directly to promoting viral replication through translational repression in immune cells represents an innovative hypothesis that distinguishes itself from more conventional views on antiviral responses.
