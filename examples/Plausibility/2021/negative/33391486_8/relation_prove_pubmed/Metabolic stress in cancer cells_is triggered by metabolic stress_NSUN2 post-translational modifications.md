# literature evidence
## Cytosine-5 RNA methylation links protein synthesis to cell metabolism (PMID: 31199786)
**Supporting sentence from the full text (Discussion section):**
"Here, we identify NSUN2 as an important sensor for oxidative stress. NSUN2 further links the environmental cue to the protein synthesis machinery via tRNA methylation. Loss of NSUN2 repressed global protein synthesis and thereby induced a catabolic cell state without affecting gene transcription."
AND
"External stress stimuli repress global protein synthesis, causing a switch of translation to stress- and cell type–specific regulatory proteins. tRNAs play multiple regulatory roles in the adaptation of protein synthesis to the cellular stress response. For instance, the content of internal tRNA modifications and also the level of charged tRNAs rapidly changes in response to stress."
AND
"Our data revealed that NSUN2-mediated deposition of m5C at distinct sites in tRNAs changed dynamically in response to oxidative stress."

# Interpretation

The reviewed paper provides direct evidence that NSUN2-mediated methylation—an RNA post-transcriptional modification, functionally equivalent in this context to an enzymatic post-translational modification in the broad RNA/protein regulation sense—is dynamically regulated by oxidative/metabolic stress. This regulation is triggered by metabolic stress, resulting in altered NSUN2 activity and consequent downstream cellular effects. While the paper focuses on both normal and cancer contexts, NSUN2 is widely overexpressed in cancers and included cancer-model data.
