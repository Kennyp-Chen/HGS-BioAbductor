1. Hypothesis Summary

The hypothesis proposes that, under tumor stress conditions (specifically hypoxia and oxidative stress), cancer cells generate a unique set of tRNA-derived fragments (tRFs). These fragments are selectively packaged into exosomes through interactions with specific RNA-binding proteins. Once exosomes are secreted into the tumor microenvironment, they are preferentially taken up by tumor-associated macrophages. Within these macrophages, the internalized tRFs interact with intracellular RNA-binding proteins to modulate gene expression, shifting the macrophage phenotype toward an immunosuppressive, M2-like state that supports tumor growth and metastasis. Moreover, the hypothesis suggests that this molecular pathway can be dissected using advanced techniques such as high-resolution exosome profiling, single-cell transcriptomics, and CRISPR-mediated functional screens, which may also reveal new therapeutic targets in cancer immunotherapy.
