1. Hypothesis Summary  
────────────────────────────  
The hypothesis posits that under conditions of metabolic stress, cancer cells exhibit a reduction in NSUN2-mediated m5C methylation on tRNAs. This loss of methylation renders tRNAs more susceptible to cleavage by the enzyme angiogenin, leading to the generation of a specific subset of tRNA-derived fragments (tRFs). These tRFs are proposed to function as molecular switches that alter the translation machinery by displacing key initiation factors and enhancing ribosome biogenesis. Consequently, this modulation of translation preferentially upregulates mRNAs that encode proteins involved in cell survival and metabolic adaptation. The hypothesis also outlines a comprehensive experimental approach involving high-throughput RNA sequencing (for tRF identification), ribosome profiling (to uncover changes in translation output), and CRISPR-based perturbations (to map causal relationships within the epitranscriptomic landscape).

2. Originality Assessment

The hypothesis is innovative in several ways:
- It integrates metabolic stress, epitranscriptomic modifications, and translational control—a linkage that is not broadly established in the literature.
- The focus on NSUN2-mediated m5C methylation and its influence on tRNA stability under metabolic stress adds a novel angle, as previous studies have mainly centered on the role of tRNA fragments in translation repression rather than stimulation.
- The proposal that specific tRFs can actively displace initiation factors and promote ribosome biogenesis to drive selective mRNA translation is a fresh perspective, potentially distinguishing this model from existing paradigms that often view tRFs as inhibitory.
- The use of advanced high-throughput technologies along with CRISPR-mediated targeting imparts a modern and robust methodological framework that enhances the originality and feasibility of dissecting the proposed molecular network.
