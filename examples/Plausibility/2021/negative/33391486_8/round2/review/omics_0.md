1. Hypothesis Summary:
The hypothesis proposes that stress induced by viral infection leads to an increased expression of specific tRNA demethylases (such as ALKBH3). This upregulation results in the targeted demethylation of tRNAs, rendering them more susceptible to cleavage by the enzyme angiogenin (ANG). The cleavage particularly generates tRNA-derived fragments (tRF-5 fragments), which are then selectively loaded into noncanonical Argonaute complexes (Ago1, Ago3, and Ago4). These tRF-5 molecules are believed to reprogram the cell's mRNA translation and stability networks, a shift that ultimately weakens host antiviral defenses, bolsters viral replication, and activates oncogenic pathways. The hypothesis also outlines that the investigation will utilize advanced methodologies (CRISPR-Cas9 mediated gene editing, RNA immunoprecipitation sequencing [RIP-seq], and ribosome profiling) to dissect and potentially target this cascade.
