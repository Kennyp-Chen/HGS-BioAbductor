1. Hypothesis Summary  
─────────────────────────────  
The hypothesis posits that during viral infection-induced stress, there is an upregulation of specific tRNA demethylases (e.g., ALKBH3). This enzymatic activity removes methyl groups from tRNAs, making them more structurally vulnerable to cleavage by angiogenin (ANG). As a result, there is an increased production of specific tRNA-derived fragments (tRF-5 fragments). These tRF-5 molecules are then selectively incorporated into noncanonical Argonaute complexes (Ago1, Ago3, Ago4), which in turn reprogram the cell’s mRNA translation and stability. This reprogramming purportedly weakens host antiviral responses, bolsters viral replication, and may simultaneously activate oncogenic pathways—as observed in certain cancer-related tRF dysregulation scenarios. The approach employs advanced methods (CRISPR-Cas9, RIP-seq, ribosome profiling) to dissect and ultimately target these mechanisms.
