 • Abstract 1: tRNA‐derived fragments (tRFs) are produced from pre‐ or mature tRNAs and act via mechanisms including microRNA‐like gene silencing, binding RNA‐binding proteins, promoting ribosome biogenesis, interfering with translation initiation, and even modulating apoptosis (e.g., by binding cytochrome c).  • Abstract 2: tRNA‐derived small RNAs (tsRNAs, including both tiRNAs and tRFs) participate in gene silencing, regulation of RNA stability, reverse transcription, and translation. Their abnormal expression is linked with diverse cancers, suggesting they play roles in key cellular processes such as proliferation, migration, cell cycle, and apoptosis.
 • Abstract 3: A specific tRF (Gly-tRF) in hepatocellular carcinoma was shown to promote characteristics of liver cancer stem cells and to enhance cell migration and epithelial–mesenchymal transition by targeting NDFIP2 and activating the AKT signalling pathway.
