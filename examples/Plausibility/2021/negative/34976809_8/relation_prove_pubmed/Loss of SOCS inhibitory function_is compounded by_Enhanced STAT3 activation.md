# Search Strategy and Reasoning
For the query, the minimal MeSH terms are "SOCS" and "STAT3". These keywords were used to search for literature connecting the loss of SOCS inhibitory function (SOCS are inhibitors of the JAK/STAT pathway, including STAT3) and enhanced STAT3 activation. The goal was to find direct or semantically equivalent statements showing that loss or reduction in SOCS function leads to greater STAT3 activation.

The search yielded several papers directly investigating SOCS, STAT3, and their regulatory relationship. Abstracts were reviewed for evidence of a compounded effect of SOCS loss leading to increased STAT3 activation. One relevant paper was selected for full-text review due to its detailed molecular mechanistic investigation.

# literature evidence

## "MiR-203 improved renal cell injury in diabetic nephropathy by targeting SOCS6/SOCS7 and inhibiting JAK/STAT pathway activation." PMID: 40155732
From the full text:  
"SOCS6 deficiency effectively rescued the damaged effects caused by HG, indicating miR-203 improved the function of NRK cells by down-regulating the expression of SOCS6... As shown in Fig. 5, HG treatment obviously increased the phosphorylation of JAK1 and STAT3. And these effects were inhibited by miR-203a-5p mimic transfection. Whereas the inhibitor group showed enhanced phosphorylation of JAK and STAT3 compared with those in the HG group. These results revealed 203a-5p could inhibit JAK/STAT signaling in NRK cells treated with HG."

And crucially, regarding the compounded relationship:  
"Our current study revealed HG treatment would damage the NRK cells and down-regulate the expression of miR-203. And miR-203 mimic could rescue the injury effects on NRK cells caused by HG via targeting SOCS6 and SOCS7 and inhibiting the activation of JAK/STAT signaling."

# Final Evaluation
The study provides direct evidence that loss of SOCS inhibitory function (via down-regulation or targeting of SOCS6 and SOCS7) leads to increased JAK/STAT3 pathway activation (as measured by increased phosphorylation of STAT3) — matching the relationship "enhanced STAT3 activation is compounded by loss of SOCS inhibitory function."

Answer: True
