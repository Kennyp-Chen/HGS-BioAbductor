1. Hypothesis Summary  
─────────────────────────────  
The hypothesis proposes that in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), the transcription factor STAT3 upregulates α-fetoprotein (AFP), which in turn participates in a positive feedback loop that enhances IL-6 signaling. This loop is suggested to promote the emergence and maintenance of cancer stem cells while also facilitating immune escape mechanisms. As a result, the hypothesis posits that disrupting the interaction between AFP and STAT3 could break this feed‐forward cycle and potentially overcome drug resistance and tumor progression.

• Existing literature supports the roles of STAT3 and IL-6 in HCC progression, immune evasion, and the maintenance of cancer stem cells. However, AFP is traditionally viewed primarily as a biomarker rather than an active participant in signaling pathways.  
• The novelty in this hypothesis lies in attributing an active regulatory role to AFP by linking it directly to STAT3 activity and establishing a feed-forward loop that amplifies IL-6 signaling.  
• If substantiated, this perspective distinguishes itself from conventional views by proposing AFP not just as a diagnostic marker but also as a functional mediator in HCC biology.  
• Thus, while aspects of the hypothesis build on established pathways (i.e., IL‐6/STAT3 axis), the suggestion that AFP is an active driver of the signaling loop and contributes to immune escape is relatively innovative and introduces a novel target for therapeutic intervention.
