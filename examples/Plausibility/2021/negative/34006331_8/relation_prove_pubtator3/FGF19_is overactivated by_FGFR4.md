# Search Strategy and Reasoning
The minimal keywords for this relationship are "FGF19" and "FGFR4". Both terms are unique and biologically specific, and their interaction is well-studied in multiple contexts (notably in cancer biology). The query "FGF19 FGFR4" was used, and a very large number of results are returned. The goal was to find direct or semantically implied evidence of "overactivation" of FGFR4 by FGF19. I reviewed abstracts for explicit or implied statements that FGF19 triggers, activates, or causes overactivation/upregulation of FGFR4 or the pathway.

# literature evidence
## "Dissecting the Role of the FGF19-FGFR4 Signaling Pathway in Cancer Development and Progression" (PMID: 32154250)
"FGFR4 is highly activated in certain types of cancer and its activation is closely associated with its specific ligand, FGF19. Indeed, FGF19-FGFR4 signaling is implicated in many cellular processes including cell proliferation, migration, metabolism, and differentiation. Since active FGF19-FGFR4 signaling acts as an oncogenic pathway in certain types of cancer, the development and therapeutic evaluation of FGFR4-specific inhibitors in cancer patients is a topic of significant interest. ... We also discussed the underlying molecular mechanisms of oncogenic activation of FGFR4 by FGF19."

## "Novel Regulatory Factors and Small-Molecule Inhibitors of FGFR4 in Cancer" (PMID: 33981224)
"Fibroblast growth factor receptor 4 (FGFR4) is a tyrosine kinase receptor that is a member of the fibroblast growth factor receptor family and is stimulated by highly regulated ligand binding. Excessive expression of the receptor and its ligand, especially FGF19, occurs in many types of cancer. Abnormal FGFR4 production explains these cancer formations, and therefore, this receptor has emerged as a potential target for inhibiting cancer development."

## "Targeting the FGF19-FGFR4 pathway for cholestatic, metabolic, and cancerous diseases." (PMID: 38212977)
"However, chronic administration of FGF19 drives oncogenesis in mice by activating the FGFR4-dependent mitogenic or hepatic regenerative pathway, which could be a concern in humans. Agents that block FGF19 or FGFR4 signaling have shown great potency in preventing FGF19-responsive hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) development in animal models."
