1. Hypothesis Summary  
─────────────────────────────  
The hypothesis posits that in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), overexpression of PCSK9 acts as a dual facilitator of immune evasion. First, it is proposed that PCSK9 promotes the degradation of MHC class I molecules, thereby impairing effective antigen presentation. Second, PCSK9 is suggested to reprogram lipid metabolism in a way that shields tumor cells from ferroptosis, a form of iron-dependent cell death. The hypothesis further proposes that modern genomic and proteomic technologies can specifically inhibit PCSK9. By doing so, MHC class I expression would be restored and the metabolic defenses against ferroptosis would be disrupted. Finally, when this approach is combined with PD-1 checkpoint blockade (which relieves T-cell inhibition), a synergistic reactivation of CD8+ T-cell responses is expected, leading to a robust anti-tumor immune effect.

2. Originality Assessment  
─────────────────────────────  
• The link between PCSK9 and immune escape, predominantly through modulation of lipid metabolism, has been explored in some recent studies, but the explicit connection to MHC class I degradation in the context of HCC is less well documented.  
• The addition of ferroptosis protection as a mechanism is particularly innovative. This dual mechanism—involving both antigen presentation impairment and metabolic reprogramming—adds a layer of novelty compared to more traditional views of immune evasion solely focused on checkpoint molecules.  
• The proposed combinatorial treatment (PCSK9 inhibition plus PD-1 blockade) reflects an emerging trend in cancer immunotherapy but is distinctive in targeting a non-canonical player like PCSK9 in HCC, thereby offering a fresh perspective to the field.
