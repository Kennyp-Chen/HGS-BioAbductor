1. Hypothesis Summary  
─────────────────────  
The hypothesis posits that in HBV-associated hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) tumors marked by elevated levels of ERα36 and PD-L1, treatment with Icaritin can reprogram the tumor microenvironment. It suggests that Icaritin operates through two major mechanisms: firstly, by reducing PD-L1 expression via inhibition of the IL-6/JAK2/STAT3 signaling pathway; secondly, by modulating oncogenic and epigenetic signals driven by ERα36. This dual-action is theorized to counteract cancer stem cell (CSC)-mediated immune evasion, decrease chronic inflammation, and enhance CD8+ T cell–mediated antitumor immunity, which in turn would bolster the therapeutic efficacy of immune checkpoint inhibitors.

• It uniquely combines targeting of the IL-6/JAK2/STAT3 axis and ERα36-driven epigenetic modifications within a specific context of HBV-associated HCC.  
• Linking Icaritin’s potential molecular effects with immune checkpoint inhibitor efficacy presents an inventive angle that could extend beyond conventional monotherapies.  
While each individual component (Icaritin’s anti-inflammatory effects, the involvement of IL-6/JAK2/STAT3 in PD-L1 regulation, and the role of ERα36 in tumor biology) has been previously explored, the hypothesis distinguishes itself by proposing a comprehensive, dual-modulatory strategy that addresses both the tumor microenvironment and immune evasion mechanisms.
