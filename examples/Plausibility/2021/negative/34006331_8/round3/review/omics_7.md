1. Hypothesis Summary  
─────────────────────────────  
• The hypothesis posits that in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), overexpression of PCSK9 facilitates tumor immune evasion through two distinct mechanisms:  
  (i) It promotes degradation of MHC class I molecules, thereby impairing antigen presentation and thus reducing the recognition of tumor cells by CD8+ T-cells.  
  (ii) It reprograms cellular lipid metabolism to confer resistance to ferroptosis, enhancing tumor cell survival.  
• It further proposes that using precise modern genomic tools (e.g., CRISPR/Cas9) and advanced proteomic inhibitors to target PCSK9 can restore MHC class I levels and reverse the metabolic reprogramming.  
• Finally, the combination of PCSK9 inhibition with PD-1 checkpoint blockade is hypothesized to synergistically reactivate CD8+ T-cell antitumor activity, promoting robust tumor clearance.

─────────────────────────────  
2. Originality Assessment  
─────────────────────────────  
• The idea that PCSK9 may modulate immune evasion through regulation of MHC class I is emerging and has been recently explored, particularly given PCSK9’s known roles in receptor degradation.  
• Linking PCSK9 with cellular lipid metabolism reprogramming and ferroptosis resistance in HCC is a relatively novel perspective not widely established in the literature.  
• The approach of combining genomic editing or proteomic inhibition of PCSK9 with PD-1 checkpoint blockade is an innovative therapeutic angle that integrates molecular targeting with immune modulation, distinguishing it from more conventional single-target studies.  
• Overall, the hypothesis offers an innovative multi-mechanistic perspective that expands the understanding of PCSK9’s role in tumor biology.

  - While the link between PCSK9 and lipid metabolic reprogramming, particularly in establishing ferroptosis resistance, is less established, recent research in metabolic reprogramming provides a plausible theoretical framework.
