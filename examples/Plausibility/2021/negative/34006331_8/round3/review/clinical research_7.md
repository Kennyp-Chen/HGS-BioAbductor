1. Hypothesis Summary:
The hypothesis posits that in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), overexpression of PCSK9 serves a dual role in tumor immune evasion. First, it leads to the degradation of MHC class I molecules, thereby disrupting antigen presentation and hindering CD8+ T-cell tumor recognition. Second, PCSK9 reprograms cellular lipid metabolism in a way that confers resistance to ferroptosis—a regulated form of cell death—thus enhancing tumor cell survival. The hypothesis further suggests that by targeting PCSK9 using modern genomic tools (e.g., CRISPR/Cas9) and advanced proteomic inhibitors, it may be possible to both restore MHC class I levels and reverse the metabolic reprogramming. Moreover, when this approach is combined with PD-1 checkpoint blockade therapy, the reactivation of CD8+ T-cells could promote enhanced antitumor immunity and more robust tumor clearance.

2. Originality Assessment:
The hypothesis is notable for integrating two distinct but complementary mechanisms underlying tumor immune evasion:
‐ The role of PCSK9 in modulating MHC class I expression is a novel angle that is not widely established, though there have been emerging discussions regarding PCSK9’s involvement beyond lipid metabolism.
‐ The connection between PCSK9, lipid metabolism, and the development of ferroptosis resistance in HCC is innovative and provides a fresh perspective on how metabolic reprogramming can interface with cell death pathways in cancer.
Additionally, combining PCSK9 inhibition with PD-1 checkpoint blockade for a potential synergistic effect is conceptually appealing and relatively unique compared to more traditional monotherapies. Overall, while individual components (immune evasion via MHC downregulation, and resistance to ferroptosis) have been explored in other contexts, their combined role as facilitated by PCSK9 in HCC introduces an original and integrative perspective.

• Combining PCSK9 inhibition with PD‐1 checkpoint blockade is a logical extension that leverages the complementary mechanisms of both innate tumor vulnerabilities and adaptive immunotherapy.

Points Requiring Further Clarification or Evidence:
• The causal relationship between PCSK9 overexpression and MHC class I degradation must be more thoroughly validated. Although there is emerging literature on PCSK9’s role in immunomodulation, direct mechanistic evidence in HCC remains limited.
• The link between PCSK9-mediated lipid metabolic reprogramming and ferroptosis resistance requires a more detailed mechanistic explanation. Additional preclinical data (or references to evidence in similar cancer types) would strengthen the theoretical underpinning.
