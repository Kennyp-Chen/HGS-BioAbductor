1. Hypothesis Summary

The hypothesis proposes that in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), the enzyme PCSK9 promotes immune evasion by driving the degradation of MHC class I molecules. This degradation reduces the presentation of tumor antigens, thereby impairing the immune system’s ability to recognize and eliminate cancer cells. Consequently, inhibiting PCSK9 is predicted to restore MHC class I expression and antigen presentation, enhancing anti-tumor immunity. Furthermore, when used in combination with PD-1 blockade—which is already established as an immune checkpoint inhibitor—the PCSK9 inhibitor may synergistically improve patient outcomes by more effectively reactivating T cell responses against tumors.

2. Originality Assessment

The hypothesis integrates areas of research that have gained attention in recent years, namely the role of PCSK9 beyond cholesterol metabolism and its potential link to tumor immunology. While PCSK9 has been predominantly studied in the context of cardiovascular diseases, recent studies have begun exploring its role in cancer immune evasion. The proposed mechanism—that PCSK9 contributes to the degradation of MHC class I molecules and that its inhibition might enhance antigen presentation—offers an innovative perspective. The combination with PD-1 blockade further distinguishes it by suggesting a dual strategy: restoring antigen presentation while removing the inhibitory signals that limit T cell activity. Although there are emerging studies hinting at similar pathways, the explicit linking of PCSK9 activity in HCC with immune evasion and its therapeutic inhibition to synergize with checkpoint inhibitors represents a fresh and notable perspective in the field.
