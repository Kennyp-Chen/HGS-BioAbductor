I used the minimal MeSH-based combination of keywords "PARP inhibitors" and "DNA damage response" (DDR) to determine if there is evidence that the administration of PARP inhibitors is mediated by further compromised DDR. The logic was that if PARP inhibitors work by further compromising the DNA damage response (i.e., exploiting existing DDR deficiencies or directly leading to additional impairment of DNA repair pathways), then the literature should report this mechanistic relationship. I carefully examined relevant abstracts for explicit or semantically equivalent statements linking PARP inhibitor administration to further compromise of DDR mechanisms.

# literature evidence

## "PARP and PARG inhibitors in cancer treatment" (PMID: 32029455)
"PARP inhibitors act through synthetic lethality with mutations in DNA repair genes and were approved for the treatment of BRCA mutated ovarian and breast cancer. PARP inhibitors destabilize replication forks through PARP DNA entrapment and induce cell death through replication stress-induced mitotic catastrophe."

## "Targeting the DNA damage response: PARP inhibitors and new perspectives in the landscape of cancer treatment." (PMID: 34800653)
"Poly-ADP-Ribose-Polymerase (PARP)-inhibitors, the first approved targeted agents able to tackle DNA damage response (DDR), have demonstrated antitumor activity, particularly when homologous recombination impairment is present."

These statements together provide clear evidence that administration of PARP inhibitors compromises or leverages already-compromised DDR (synthetic lethality in DDR-defective cells) and can further disrupt DNA repair by destabilizing replication forks and increasing replication stress, leading to cell death.
