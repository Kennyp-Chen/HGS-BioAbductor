1. Hypothesis Summary

The hypothesis proposes that in biliary tract cancer (BTC), simultaneously targeting two specific pathways—DNA damage response (DDR) and TGF-β signaling—will modify the tumor microenvironment in a way that enhances the effectiveness of immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs). The key components are:

• DDR Pathways: Inhibiting DDR is posited to elevate the presence of cytosolic DNA, which in turn would stimulate the cGAS-STING pathway. This activation is expected to boost innate immune responses within the tumor.

• TGF-β Signaling: Blocking TGF-β signaling is believed to relieve the immunosuppressive conditions within the tumor microenvironment, potentially making it more receptive to immune attack.

• Outcome: The combination of these actions is hypothesized to convert “cold” tumors, which are less immunogenic, into “hot” tumors that are inflamed and more likely to respond to ICIs. The synergy between these mechanisms is seen as a strategy to overcome resistance to immunotherapy in BTC.

• However, aspects of the strategy have been explored in other tumor types, meaning that the originality mainly lies in the specific application to BTC and the conceptual synergy proposed rather than in entirely new mechanistic insights.
