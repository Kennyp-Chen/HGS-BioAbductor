1. Hypothesis Summary:
The hypothesis posits that by simultaneously targeting two regulatory systems in biliary tract cancer, one can modify the tumor microenvironment to be more receptive to immune checkpoint inhibitors. Specifically, it suggests that:
• Inhibiting DNA damage response (DDR) pathways will activate the innate immune system via the cGAS-STING pathway.
• Inhibiting TGF-β signaling will reduce immunosuppression.
Together, these interventions are expected to shift the tumor microenvironment from “cold” (non-immunogenic, non-inflammatory) to “hot” (immunogenic, inflammatory), thereby producing a synergistic effect when combined with immune checkpoint blockade therapy.

The hypothesis integrates two emerging strategies in cancer therapy:
• Exploiting DDR inhibitors to stimulate innate immune responses via the cGAS-STING axis.
• Inhibiting TGF-β signaling, which is recognized as a potent mediator of tumor-induced immunosuppression.
While both approaches have been explored individually in several tumor types, their combined application in biliary tract cancer represents a novel conceptual strategy. Biliary tract cancer is notoriously resistant to conventional immunotherapies, and the idea of dual-targeting to modulate the tumor microenvironment is innovative. The integration differentiates itself from conventional monotherapies and provides an inventive framework that builds on current immuno-oncology research.

This immune activation can contribute to the priming of adaptive immune responses. Concurrently, TGF-β acts in many cancers to dampen immune responses and promote cellular plasticity, so its inhibition is a reasonable strategy to reduce immunosuppression.
• Strength of Relationship: The strength of the proposed relationship relies on the assumption that both DDR and TGF-β pathways play critical roles in maintaining a ‘cold’ microenvironment in biliary tract cancer. Preclinical models in other cancers lend support to the concept; however, the specific interplay between these pathways in the context of biliary tract tumors remains to be fully elucidated.

Further mechanistic studies and preclinical models would be needed to confirm the synergy and to determine optimal dosing and combination strategies.
