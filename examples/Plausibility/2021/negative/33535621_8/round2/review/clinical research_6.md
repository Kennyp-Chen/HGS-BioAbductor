1. Hypothesis Summary

The hypothesis proposes that in biliary tract cancer (BTC), a precision-guided dual targeting of the DNA damage response (DDR) and the TGF-β pathways can transform the tumor microenvironment. The key components include:  
• Utilizing advanced genomic profiling, targeted drug delivery, and digital immune biomarker monitoring to precisely modulate these pathways.  
• Inhibiting DDR to promote accumulation of cytosolic DNA fragments that activate the cGAS-STING pathway and subsequently the type I interferon response.  
• Concurrently blocking TGF-β to relieve immunosuppressive signaling, thereby facilitating robust T-cell infiltration.  
• Using this strategy to “heat up” a typically “cold” tumor milieu and synergize with immune checkpoint inhibitors to enhance therapeutic outcomes in BTC.

• The incorporation of state-of-the-art genomic profiling, sophisticated targeted drug delivery systems, and digital monitoring of immune biomarkers to guide therapy.

• The proposed synergy with immune checkpoint inhibitors is consistent with emerging evidence in immuno-oncology.
