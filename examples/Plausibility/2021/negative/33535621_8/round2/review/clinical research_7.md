1. Hypothesis Summary

The hypothesis posits that in biliary tract cancer (BTC), specific deficiencies in the DNA damage repair (DDR) machinery lead to an accumulation of cytosolic DNA. This aberrant DNA build-up is proposed to activate the cGAS-STING pathway, which in turn promotes robust interferon signaling, upregulates PD-L1 expression, and enhances T cell infiltration into the tumor microenvironment (TME). The hypothesis further argues that contemporary genomic and transcriptomic profiling can be used in real time to identify BTC tumors with these specific molecular characteristics. Finally, it suggests that administering PARP inhibitors will exacerbate DDR impairment, thereby intensifying cGAS-STING-mediated immune activation. When PARP inhibitors are combined with immune checkpoint inhibitors, the intent is to convert immunologically “cold” tumors into “hot” tumors, ultimately increasing the clinical efficacy of immunotherapy in this challenging cancer type.
