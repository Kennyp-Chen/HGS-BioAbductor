1. Hypothesis Summary

The hypothesis posits that in biliary tract cancers (BTCs) with DNA damage response (DDR) gene alterations, increased accumulation of DNA damage leads to higher tumor mutational burden (TMB) and neoantigen load. Paradoxically, this accumulation also activates intrinsic stress responses that upregulate TGF‐β signaling. In combination with IFN‐γ signaling, this results in a compensatory elevation of PD-L1 expression and recruitment of immunosuppressive cells, collectively producing an “immune cold” microenvironment that makes immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) less effective. The hypothesis then suggests that dual therapeutic inhibition of both TGF‐β and PD-L1 would restore tumor immunogenicity and enhance clinical responses. The experimental framework involves using CRISPR-based models to modulate DDR gene status, deploying multi-omics analyses to map the cascade of molecular events, and applying advanced in vivo imaging to monitor tumor-immune dynamics and develop stratification biomarkers.

 • Proposing a direct mechanistic link between DDR gene alterations and an upregulated TGF‐β response.
 • Suggesting that IFN‐γ and TGF‐β function synergistically to drive compensatory PD-L1 expression and immune suppression.
 • Advocating a dual inhibition strategy aimed at simultaneously targeting TGF‐β and PD‐L1, specifically within the context of BTCs harboring DDR mutations.
The approach, which couples state-of-the-art CRISPR-based manipulation with multi-omics and in vivo imaging technology for real-time evaluation, further distinguishes the study from previous investigations.
