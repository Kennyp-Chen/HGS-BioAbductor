1. Hypothesis Summary

Your hypothesis proposes that using Boron Neutron Capture Therapy (BNCT) in combination with a post-treatment administration of toll-like receptor (TLR) agonists will lead to enhanced activation of dendritic cells and improved antigen presentation. This, in turn, is expected to stimulate strong, tumor-specific T-cell responses, generating both local and systemic (abscopal) anti-tumor effects. The hypothesis specifically targets cancers that are typically resistant to systemic immunotherapy, suggesting that the combined treatment can overcome such resistance by engaging innate and adaptive immune pathways.

The hypothesis exhibits innovative potential by bridging a specialized localized treatment modality (BNCT) with immunomodulatory therapy (TLR agonists). While both BNCT and TLR agonists have been studied independently in oncology and immunotherapy contexts, their combination to harness a dual effect—direct tumor cell damage through BNCT and subsequent immune activation via TLR signaling—is a less common therapeutic strategy. This integration of a localized physical treatment with immune adjuvant therapy may indeed offer novel insights:
 • It attempts to transform the immunologically ‘cold’ environment often seen in resistant tumors.
 • It leverages the concept of an abscopal effect, which is gaining interest in radiotherapy and immunotherapy combinations.
Thus, the hypothesis provides an innovative perspective by proposing a synergistic interaction that has not been exhaustively explored in current research.
