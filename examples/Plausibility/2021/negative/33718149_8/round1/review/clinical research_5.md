1. Hypothesis Summary

The hypothesis proposes that combining Boron Neutron Capture Therapy (BNCT) with immune-modulatory strategies—either through local adjuvant delivery or systemic immune checkpoint blockade—will produce a synergistic effect. This synergy is expected to accomplish two key outcomes: (a) enhanced local tumor ablation and (b) activation of systemic anti-tumor immunity. A central claim is that this combined approach can convert immunologically “cold” tumors (which are less responsive to immune interventions) into “hot” tumors, which are characterized by higher immune cell infiltration and reactivity. This conversion is hypothesized to improve overall treatment outcomes in cancer therapy.

2. Originality Assessment

The idea of combining local tumor-directed therapies with systemic immunotherapy is not entirely new; similar synergistic strategies have been investigated with conventional radiotherapy and checkpoint inhibitors. However, using BNCT—a unique form of radiation therapy that relies on boron compounds and neutron capture reactions—adds an innovative twist. The integration of BNCT with immune-modulatory approaches is less extensively explored compared to other radiotherapy approaches, and thus it offers a novel perspective within the clinical research landscape. The hypothesis stands out by proposing a specific mechanistic pathway (i.e., conversion of cold tumors to hot) that could potentially break new ground in the treatment of resistant tumor types.
