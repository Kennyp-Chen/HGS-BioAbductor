# literature evidence
## "An auto-photoacoustic melanin-based drug delivery nano-platform for self-monitoring of acute kidney injury therapy via a triple-collaborative strategy." (PMID: 35643195)
Exact supporting sentence from abstract:
"Because melanin nanoparticles possess inherent photoacoustic (PA) imaging capability, they can not only serve as a drug carrier but also perform self-monitoring for real-time tracking of GMP biodistribution and renal uptake in a murine AKI model through PA imaging.... These results indicated that the nanoplatform could realize the targeted delivery and curative effect monitoring under the guidance of PA imaging...."

From the full text:
"The nanoparticles exhibited good dispersibility, solubility, and broad-spectrum ROS scavenging ability... Because melanin nanoparticles possess inherent photoacoustic (PA) imaging capability, they can not only serve as a drug carrier but also perform self-monitoring for real-time tracking... In vivo experiments showed that the GMP nanoparticles could effectively reduce oxidative stress, apoptosis, and inflammatory response... the nanoplatform could realize the targeted delivery and curative effect monitoring under the guidance of PA imaging..."

However, the study describes "real-time imaging" for self-monitoring (tracking and guidance), but not "dosimetry feedback" nor "precise orchestration" of PARP inhibitor release by real-time imaging feedback (adaptive, feedback-controlled release). The release mechanism is pH and ROS responsive, but not explicitly orchestrated or altered in real-time by imaging or dosimetry feedback signals.

# Conclusion
There is evidence for real-time imaging enabled monitoring of the nanoparticle and its PARP inhibitor payload in vivo, as well as the ability for the system to be image-guided. However, no literature evidence was found that shows release of the PARP inhibitor is "precisely orchestrated by real-time imaging and dosimetry feedback."

Answer: False
