1. Hypothesis Summary  
─────────────────────────────  
Your hypothesis proposes a combinatorial cancer treatment strategy that integrates nanoparticle-enhanced Boron Neutron Capture Therapy (BNCT) with real-time imaging and biomarker-guided immune modulation. The key components are:  
• Nanoparticles (e.g., cRGD-modified agents) to deliver boron selectively into tumors, aiming for high linear energy transfer (LET) radiation-induced tumor cell death while also causing a controlled release of danger signals (DAMPs) and tumor antigens.  
• Real-time imaging modalities and biomarker assays to dynamically monitor tumor antigen release and identify an optimal time window for additional immune interventions.  
• The administration of low-dose immune adjuvants or checkpoint inhibitors during the identified window to enhance dendritic cell activation and T-cell recruitment, thereby potentially converting immunologically 'cold' tumors into 'hot' tumors, and ultimately promoting both local tumor eradication and systemic (abscopal) anti-tumor responses.

• Integrating real-time imaging and biomarker tracking to temporally coordinate the induction of an immune response is a cutting-edge concept that moves beyond the traditional static treatment schemes.  
• Strategically timing immune adjuvant administration to exploit the dynamic window of antigen release is an inventive approach that seeks to bridge local therapy with systemic immune activation.  

While elements of BNCT, nanoparticle delivery, and immunotherapy are individually established, their integrative application as described here distinguishes your hypothesis from existing studies. It suggests an interdisciplinary innovation that could inspire new therapeutic protocols if experimentally validated.
