1. Hypothesis Summary

The hypothesis proposes an advanced dual-function nanoparticle system designed for boron neutron capture therapy (BNCT) in cancers with high boron affinity. The key components include:
• A nanoparticle engineered for selective tumor targeting and optimized for enhanced boron uptake.
• An environment-sensitive release mechanism that is triggered by tumor-specific conditions.
• Real-time imaging and dosimetry integration to enable patient-specific monitoring during treatment.
• Co-delivery of a PARP inhibitor, timed to interfere with DNA repair mechanisms.
Together, the system aims to maximize BNCT efficacy by facilitating high linear energy transfer (LET) during neutron irradiation (creating focal double-strand breaks) and concurrently poisoning the cancer cell’s DNA repair machinery. This dual-action strategy, by promoting accumulation of unrepaired DNA damage, is predicted to significantly increase tumor cell death.

2. Originality Assessment

• The integration of real-time imaging with dosimetry in a nanoparticle system is a relatively modern approach and represents an innovative direction in personalized cancer therapy.
• Merging BNCT with a PARP inhibitor for synergy is a novel strategy, as it combines the advantages of high LET-induced DNA damage with pharmacological inhibition of repair.
• While individual aspects—like targeted nanoparticles, BNCT, and PARP inhibition—are established research areas, their combined use in a dual-function platform is not widely reported. This suggests the hypothesis does offer an innovative perspective that distinguishes itself from more conventional single-modality treatments.
