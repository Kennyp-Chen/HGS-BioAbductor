1. Hypothesis Summary:
The hypothesis proposes a dual-action nanoparticle designed to enhance Boron Neutron Capture Therapy (BNCT). The particle is engineered to deliver both boron compounds for localized high-LET (linear energy transfer) tumor cell killing and a Toll-like receptor (TLR) agonist that is released in a controlled, post-irradiation manner. This strategy is intended not only to intensify the local cytotoxic effects of BNCT but also to activate dendritic cells and promote tumor antigen presentation, thereby eliciting a systemic immune response that could overcome the limitations of standard BNCT. Additionally, the hypothesis suggests the incorporation of contemporary imaging techniques for real-time monitoring of nanoparticle biodistribution and the potential integration of immune checkpoint inhibitors to optimize local control and provoke an abscopal effect to manage metastatic disease.

• Overall, while the broad concept of combining radiotherapy with immunomodulation is not completely new, this hypothesis presents a creative and integrated design that bridges targeted BNCT with systemic immunotherapy, thereby offering an innovative perspective.

The notion of combining imaging techniques for real-time monitoring adds a layer of precision and adaptability.
• Theoretical Grounding: The use of TLR agonists to mature dendritic cells and boost antigen presentation is well-documented, as is the concept of immune checkpoint inhibitors enhancing antitumor immunity. The hypothesis relies on established biological principles; however, the precise timing and coordination between BNCT, TLR agonist release, and immune activation require further empirical validation.
• Evidence Basis: Although the components individually have evidence backing, the hypothesis’s success depends on the successful engineering of nanoparticles that are reliably stimuli-responsive with precise control over the release kinetics of the TLR agonist. There is also an assumption regarding the effective synergy between the localized radiotherapy and the systemic immune response, which, although plausible, needs robust preclinical evaluation.
