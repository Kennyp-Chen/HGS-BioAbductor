1. Observation Extraction:
 • In Abstract 1, HCC tumors under hypoxia display a HIF-driven transcriptional program. Inhibition of HIF-1/2 (using compound 32-134D) alters the tumor’s immune microenvironment by reducing immunosuppressive cells (tumor-associated macrophages and MDSCs) and increasing cytotoxic cells (CD8+ T cells and NK cells), leading to improved responses when combined with PD-1 blockade.
 • In Abstract 2, altering cancer cell metabolism via AMPK activation (with l-THP) triggers autophagy-mediated metabolic switching, resulting in decreased mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation and tumor cell death, suggesting that metabolic modulation significantly impacts HCC progression.
 • In Abstract 3, hypoxic HCC cells rely on proper mitochondrial function (via PTPMT1) to survive; disruption of mitochondrial cardiolipin synthesis impairs electron transport chain assembly and promotes ROS accumulation, ultimately reducing cell viability under hypoxia.

 Would we see some of the observations if the hypothesis was true: Yes, if ENTPD2 inhibition reduces adenosine accumulation, one might observe shifts in the immune microenvironment (fewer MDSCs and more cytotoxic T cells), which is similar to what is seen with HIF inhibition.
