1. Hypothesis Summary  
──────────────────────────────
The hypothesis posits that in hypoxic hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), inhibiting the enzyme ENTPD2 will decrease the production of adenosine, thereby reducing the recruitment of myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSCs). This reduction in immunosuppressive cell infiltration is expected to relieve the suppression on cytotoxic T lymphocytes (CTLs), thus enhancing the antitumor effects of PD-1/PD-L1 checkpoint blockade therapy. Additionally, the hypothesis recognizes potential compensatory mechanisms, such as alternative ectonucleotidases (e.g., CD39/CD73) and metabolic reprogramming (via polyamine metabolism and AMPK-autophagy signaling). To accurately dissect ENTPD2's role, the strategy includes employing CRISPR-Cas9 gene editing combined with single-cell metabolomic and transcriptomic profiling to monitor not only the targeted pathway but also possible collateral metabolic shifts.
