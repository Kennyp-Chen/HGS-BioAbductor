# Search Strategy and Reasoning
I began by extracting the minimal MeSH-like terms: "A2a receptor" and "Adenosine signaling". Initial PubTator3 searches with these terms directly yielded several abstracts, but no clear, explicit statement about diminution of adenosine signaling by A2a receptor blockade. I refined the search to "A2A receptor antagonist adenosine signaling", focusing on studies with antagonists/blockade and adenosinergic signaling.

From the resulting abstracts, several mentioned A2A antagonists and signal pathways, but without a direct link to the phrase "blockade of A2a receptor diminishes adenosine signaling". The closest semantic connection would be evidence where blockade or inhibition of A2a receptors leads to diminished adenosine-mediated signaling. Abstracts did contain references to A2A antagonists (e.g., SCH58261, ZM241385) modulating adenosine signaling effects in various contexts such as neuroinflammation, behavior, or disease models.

To confirm if blockade of A2a receptors actually diminishes (reduces) adenosine signaling, I examined full texts, particularly for PMID: 34211393, which discusses the effect of A2A receptor antagonists in retinal diseases, and referenced antagonists by name. However, the full text provided merely enumerated the use of antagonists and didn't state that blockade "diminishes adenosine signaling" in a mechanistic sense—it focused on reduced inflammation, not receptor signaling per se.

Therefore, despite related literature on pharmacological blockade and downstream effects, there wasn't a direct, unequivocal statement that blockade of the A2a receptor generally causes a global or system-wide "diminution" of adenosine signaling as a mechanistic generality.
