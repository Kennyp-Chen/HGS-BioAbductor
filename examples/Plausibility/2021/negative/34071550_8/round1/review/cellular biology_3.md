1. Hypothesis Summary:
The hypothesis posits that concurrently inhibiting ENTPD2—a molecule implicated in modulating MDSC (myeloid-derived suppressor cell) function—and blocking the PD‐1/PD‐L1 immune checkpoint axis will work together (synergize) to lessen MDSC‐mediated immunosuppression. This dual intervention is expected to reinvigorate cytotoxic T lymphocyte (CTL) activity, ultimately boosting the effectiveness of immunotherapy in hepatocellular carcinoma.

2. Originality Assessment:
The concept of combining checkpoint blockade (PD‐1/PD‐L1 inhibition) with approaches targeting tumor microenvironment components is not entirely new. Numerous studies have explored combination immunotherapies to overcome resistance arising from immunosuppressive cells such as MDSCs. However, focusing specifically on ENTPD2 as a target to modulate MDSC function in hepatocellular carcinoma introduces an innovative perspective. This dual-targeting strategy could provide a novel angle by integrating metabolic or ectonucleotidase modulation with established immunotherapy approaches, potentially distinguishing it from more conventional combination therapies.

3. Validity and Strength Evaluation:
The logical coherence of the hypothesis is generally sound. It builds on the established role of PD‐1/PD‐L1 in immune evasion and the recognized importance of MDSCs in fostering an immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment. By proposing that ENTPD2 inhibition can reduce MDSC-mediated suppression, the hypothesis links molecular modulation to immune cell function and improved CTL activity.

Nonetheless, several aspects require careful validation:
 • The precise role of ENTPD2 in MDSC function, recruitment, or activation within hepatocellular carcinoma must be firmly established. While there is supportive rationale, additional mechanistic evidence is needed to directly correlate ENTPD2 activity with MDSC immunosuppression.
 • The assumption of synergistic effects between ENTPD2 inhibition and PD‐1/PD‐L1 blockade demands rigorous experimental corroboration. It is important to demonstrate that the two interventions do not simply add up their individual effects, but rather interact in a way that produces a clinically meaningful enhancement.
 • Consideration of potential compensatory mechanisms or toxicity arising from dual inhibition should be addressed. The hypothesis would benefit from a discussion of how these challenges might be mitigated.

4. Conclusion and Expert Recommendations:
In conclusion, the hypothesis is innovative and logically structured, offering an intriguing new angle for enhancing immunotherapy in hepatocellular carcinoma. However, to bolster its scientific rigor, it is important to:

 • Provide or reference detailed mechanistic studies that clarify how ENTPD2 influences MDSC function in the hepatocellular carcinoma microenvironment.
 • Design preclinical experiments (e.g., in vitro and in vivo models) to demonstrate that dual inhibition results in a synergistic rather than merely additive improvement in CTL activity and tumor regression.
 • Address safety concerns and evaluate potential side effects associated with concomitant blockade of metabolic enzymes and immune checkpoints.
 • Consider integrating biomarker studies that could predict which tumors would most likely benefit from dual inhibition, thereby strengthening the translational potential of the hypothesis.
