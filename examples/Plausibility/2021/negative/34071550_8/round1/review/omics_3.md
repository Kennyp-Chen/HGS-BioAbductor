1. Hypothesis Summary  
The hypothesis posits that simultaneously targeting ENTPD2 and the PD‐1/PD‐L1 signaling axis will work synergistically to diminish the immunosuppressive activity of myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSCs) and reinvigorate cytotoxic T lymphocyte (CTL) responses. This dual inhibition is proposed to improve the efficacy of immunotherapy in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) by countering tumor-induced immune evasion mechanisms.

• This hypothesis reflects an innovative approach by combining inhibition of a purinergic enzyme (ENTPD2)—which is implicated in the modulation of the tumor immune microenvironment—with the more conventionally targeted PD‐1/PD‐L1 pathway.  
• While PD‐1/PD‐L1 blockade is a well-established strategy in cancer immunotherapy, integrating ENTPD2 inhibition is less explored in the context of HCC, particularly as it relates to MDSC-mediated immunosuppression.  
• Thus, the dual-targeting strategy offers a novel perspective that distinguishes it from more standard monotherapy or single-pathway combination approaches, although related ideas about combining metabolic modulation with immune checkpoint inhibition exist in emerging literature.
