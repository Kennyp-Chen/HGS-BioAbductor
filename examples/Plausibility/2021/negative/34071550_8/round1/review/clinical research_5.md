Below is a step‐by‐step evaluation of the hypothesis:

1. Hypothesis Summary:
The hypothesis posits that in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), inhibition of the enzyme ENTPD2—which catalyzes the conversion of extracellular ATP to 5′ AMP—will disrupt the recruitment of myeloid‐derived suppressor cells (MDSCs). The reduced presence of MDSCs is expected to alleviate tumor‐associated immunosuppression and thereby improve the effect of immune checkpoint blockade therapies used in cancer treatment.

2. Originality Assessment:
This hypothesis is innovative insofar as it connects the metabolic function of ENTPD2 with the modulation of the tumor immune microenvironment, specifically by influencing MDSC recruitment. While targeting adenosine metabolism in tumors is an area of active investigation, focusing on ENTPD2 as a specific mediator in HCC distinguishes this hypothesis from more general studies on adenosine pathways. It appears to offer a new angle by proposing that blockading a single enzymatic step in the ATP to 5′ AMP conversion could have downstream effects on immune cell recruitment and thus overcome resistance to checkpoint blockade therapy.

3. Validity and Strength Evaluation:
The logical framework of the hypothesis is coherent:
 • It is based on the premise that ENTPD2 activity influences the local concentrations of ATP and its metabolites, molecules implicated in immunomodulation.
 • The assumption that reducing 5′ AMP—and ultimately downstream adenosine production—might diminish MDSC recruitment is plausible, given the known role of adenosine in promoting immunosuppressive environments.
 • Enhancing immune checkpoint inhibition by mitigating immunosuppression adds a clear translational relevance to the hypothesis.
However, there are areas that need further theoretical and experimental support:
 • The multifactorial control of MDSC recruitment: MDSC accumulation is influenced not only by adenosine signaling but also by various chemokines, cytokines, and additional enzymes (for instance, CD39 and CD73) that can compensate or act in parallel with ENTPD2.
 • Specificity of ENTPD2’s role: Although ENTPD2 is implicated in ATP metabolism, the exact cascade leading from its inhibition to altered MDSC dynamics needs more explicit delineation. It is necessary to clarify if the reduction in 5′ AMP directly correlates with diminished adenosine receptor signaling on MDSCs.
 • The potential redundancy in nucleotide metabolism pathways in HCC might mean that inhibitors of ENTPD2 alone may not fully disrupt the immunosuppressive milieu.
 • Pre-existing evidence linking ENTPD2 activity specifically with MDSC recruitment in HCC is limited, so additional mechanistic data would strengthen the argument.

4. Conclusion and Expert Recommendations:
Overall, the hypothesis is well-conceived and provides a novel perspective on combining metabolic pathway targeting with immunotherapy in HCC. Notable weaknesses include the need for:
 • More detailed mechanistic insights: Provide direct evidence or a clearer theoretical rationale linking ENTPD2 inhibition to reduced MDSC recruitment. This could include data on how extracellular ATP, 5′ AMP, and adenosine levels affect MDSC chemotaxis in the HCC microenvironment.
 • Addressing potential compensatory mechanisms: Discuss and experimentally investigate whether other ectonucleotidases (e.g., CD39, CD73) may partially bypass ENTPD2 inhibition, thus mitigating the anticipated immunomodulatory effect.
 • Establishing causality: Preclinical models that demonstrate a direct cause-effect relationship between ENTPD2 blockade and improved immune checkpoint inhibition outcomes will significantly bolster the hypothesis.
Actionable suggestions include incorporating studies that directly measure the levels of ATP, 5′ AMP, and adenosine in the tumor microenvironment following ENTPD2 inhibition, and simultaneously evaluating MDSC recruitment and function. Additionally, it would be useful to explore combinatorial approaches where ENTPD2 inhibition is paired with blockade of parallel adenosine-generating pathways to fully understand the therapeutic potential in HCC.

By addressing these gaps, the hypothesis can be strengthened and made more persuasive from both a mechanistic and therapeutic viewpoint.
