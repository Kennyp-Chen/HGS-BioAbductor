1. Hypothesis Summary  
─────────────────────────────  
The hypothesis proposes that bioengineered, tumor‐specific exosomes loaded with the microRNA miR-128-3p and modified for real-time tracking can reverse the epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EMT) in oxaliplatin-resistant colorectal cancer cells. Mechanistically, these exosomes are designed to downregulate Bmi1—a factor implicated in drug resistance—thereby reducing MRP5-mediated efflux of oxaliplatin and impacting other EMT-regulatory mechanisms. In addition to their therapeutic cargo, the tracking modifications are intended to monitor payload distribution and therapeutic effects, ultimately minimizing off-target actions and compensatory cellular signaling.
