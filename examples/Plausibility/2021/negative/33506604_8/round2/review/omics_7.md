1. Hypothesis Summary  
─────────────────────────────  
The hypothesis proposes that engineered exosomes, which are modified to serve as targeted delivery vehicles, can be co-loaded with two agents: miR-200 family mimics and a DNMT inhibitor. When functionalized with cancer-targeting ligands, these exosomes are expected to home in on cancer cells. The combined action is intended to suppress the expression of the ZEB1 transcription factor—a known driver of epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EMT) and associated drug resistance. By doing so, the approach aims to:  
• Re-establish miR-200 homeostasis (thereby addressing its loss or dysregulation),  
• Overcome epigenetic roadblocks that contribute to the downregulation of miR-200,  
• Reverse EMT to a more epithelial (MET) state,  
• Reduce cancer stem cell–like properties, and  
• Restore chemosensitivity when compared to miR-200 monotherapy.
