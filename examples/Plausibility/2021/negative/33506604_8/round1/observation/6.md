1. Observation extraction:
 • Abstract 1 shows that hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) cells undergoing epithelial‐mesenchymal transition (EMT) via TGF‐β stimulation secrete exosomes with altered miRNA profiles. These exosomes (e.g., enriched in miR-374a-5p) promote proliferation, migration, and invasion when taken up by other HCC cells.
 • Abstract 2 demonstrates that exosomal non‐coding RNAs (ncRNAs) from HCC cells contribute to reprogramming the tumor microenvironment, inducing immune suppression and promoting tumor proliferation, metastasis, and drug resistance.
 • Abstract 3 reviews how exosome-mediated epigenetic communication in HCC affects malignant features and discusses the potential of engineered exosome mimetics as both diagnostic tools and therapeutic vehicles.

3. Causal analysis (summary):
 Would we see some of the observations if the hypothesis was true: Engineered exosomes as delivery vehicles align with the observations, and if miR-375 mimics coupled with vitamin D could effectively target c‐Myc, one might expect reversal of EMT and restored sensitivity to mTOR inhibitors. However, the TGF‐β–driven EMT and immune microenvironment remodeling already have established mechanisms, and the specific roles of vitamin D in this context are not described in the abstracts. Thus, while the hypothesis proposes a novel intervention, it does not directly replace or fill a missing piece in the established causal explanations of these observations.

4. Disproof analysis:
 Does some observations disprove the hypothesis: No observation directly contradicts the idea that engineered exosomes can be used as carriers. However, the dominant established mechanisms (e.g., TGF‐β-induced EMT, miRNA like miR-374a-5p in regulating proliferation and metastasis) suggest that the known drivers of HCC progression are not primarily linked to c‐Myc modulation by miR-375 or affected by vitamin D. This gap means the hypothesis has yet to align with or explain the observations in a way that supersedes the already supported causal pathways.

5. Conclusion:
The hypothesis introduces a novel combination (vitamin D plus engineered exosomes with miR-375 mimics targeting c‐Myc) to reverse EMT and restore mTOR inhibitor sensitivity. However, the observations already have established causal explanations (e.g., TGF‐β-induced EMT and exosomal modulation by other miRNAs) and do not specifically indicate that c‐Myc targeting via miR-375 or vitamin D supplementation plays a leading role in these processes. Therefore, although the concept of using engineered exosomes is supported, the particular mechanistic pathway proposed is not clearly validated nor shown to be necessary in the current observations.
