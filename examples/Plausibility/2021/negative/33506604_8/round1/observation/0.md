1. Observation extraction:
   • In Abstract 1, TGF-β stimulation of Hep3B liver cancer cells induces EMT and changes the exosomal miRNA cargo (e.g., upregulation of miR-374a-5p), which in turn promotes proliferation, migration, and invasion.  
   • Abstract 2 shows that SUMOylation of the RNA‐binding protein HuR in hepatocellular carcinoma is linked to enhanced proliferation and invasion, with altered RNA binding driving tumor progression.  
   • Abstract 3 emphasizes that exosomes with oncogenic miRNAs mediate intercellular communication, facilitating EMT induction, metastasis, and even therapeutic resistance in the tumor microenvironment.

2. Causal analysis (individual):
   • For Abstract 1 – Exosomal miRNAs and EMT:
     a. The cause (TGF-β–induced changes in exosomal miRNAs) is already established.
     b. Would the hypothesis (inhibition of hnRNPA2B1 sumoylation disrupting oncogenic miRNA (miR-155) loading) explain this observation? In principle, if hnRNPA2B1 were critical for sorting certain oncogenic miRNAs, its inhibition could alter exosomal content. However, the observed effect is linked to TGF-β–driven changes (and the validated miRNA is miR-374a-5p, not miR-155).
     c. Would we see this observation if the hypothesis was true: Possibly altered exosome cargo, but the current evidence points to a TGF-β mechanism and targets a different miRNA; thus, this mechanism is not a novel missing link.
     d. Explanation: The exosomal miRNA changes here are already explained by TGF-β activation, not by a deficit in hnRNPA2B1 sumoylation – not a missing piece.
     
   • For Abstract 2 – SUMOylation of HuR:
     a. The cause (SUMOylation of HuR leading to tumor-promoting alterations) is already established.
     b. Could the hypothesis play a role here? Although both involve SUMOylation of RNA‐binding proteins, the hypothesis targets hnRNPA2B1 rather than HuR. The mechanisms and targets differ.
     c. Would we see this observation if the hypothesis was true: SUMOylation influences RNA-binding proteins in tumor progression, so inhibiting hnRNPA2B1 sumoylation might have an effect; however, since HuR (not hnRNPA2B1) is implicated in this observation, the hypothesis does not offer a novel explanation.
     d. Explanation: There is an existing established pathway (HuR SUMOylation) that accounts for the observation – not a missing piece.
     
   • For Abstract 3 – Exosomal miRNAs in cell communication:
     a. The cause (exosomal miRNA-mediated intercellular communication driving EMT, metastasis, and chemoresistance) is well documented.
     b. Could the hypothesis contribute here? Yes, if hnRNPA2B1 sumoylation is required for proper exosomal loading of miRNAs (such as miR-155), then its inhibition could reduce the transfer of pro-tumorigenic signals. However, the specific miRNA mentioned (miR-155) and mechanism of hnRNPA2B1 sumoylation are not addressed in the abstracts.
     c. Would we see this observation if the hypothesis was true: Yes, a disruption in exosomal loading of oncogenic miRNAs could alter cell communication and affect EMT and chemoresistance. Nonetheless, the broader phenomenon is already well explained by general exosomal signaling.
     d. Explanation: Even though the hypothesis could influence one component of exosomal signaling, the observations provide an established background where multiple mechanisms (including those independent of hnRNPA2B1 sumoylation) are responsible – not a novel missing piece.

3. Causal analysis (summary):
   Would we see some of the observations if the hypothesis was true: Yes, if inhibition of hnRNPA2B1 sumoylation indeed disrupts oncogenic miRNA (like miR-155) packaging, we might observe reduced EMT induction and lower chemoresistance. However, the observations already attribute these phenomena to known mechanisms (TGF-β stimulation, HuR SUMOylation, and established exosomal miRNA signaling). Therefore, the hypothesis does not provide a clear, novel causal explanation but rather an alternative mechanism among several already described.

4. Disproof analysis:
   Does some observations disprove the hypothesis: None of the observations directly contradict the hypothesis. However, the presence of well-documented mechanisms (TGF-β–induced exosomal regulation and HuR SUMOylation) that explain EMT, invasion, and chemoresistance suggests that the hypothesis’s mechanism (via hnRNPA2B1 sumoylation) is not uniquely necessary or currently supported by these abstracts.

   hypothesis: other explanations more likely.
