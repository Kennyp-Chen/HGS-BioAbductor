# Search Strategy and Reasoning
The relationship to validate is whether 'Bmi1' is suppressed by enhanced EMT signaling driven by loss of epithelial marker expression (epithelial phenotype). The minimal MeSH terms are "Bmi1" and "epithelial marker expression" (a key representative marker being E-cadherin). Since EMT (epithelial-mesenchymal transition) signaling corresponds to loss of epithelial marker expression and gain of mesenchymal markers—the search focused on papers linking Bmi1 to EMT and epithelial marker expression. 

I first searched for combinations of "Bmi1", "epithelial marker expression", and "EMT". I then focused on direct connections between Bmi1 and the repression/loss of epithelial markers (e.g., E-cadherin) during EMT. The most relevant results were followed to the full text for evidence on whether enhanced EMT signaling (and resulting epithelial marker suppression) leads to suppression of Bmi1.

# literature evidence
## "BMI‑1 promotes invasion and metastasis in endometrial adenocarcinoma and is a poor prognostic factor." (PMID: 32323819)
"Western blotting showed that the expression levels of the mesenchymal markers N-cadherin, vimentin and the downstream EMT transcription factor, Slug, were decreased in the si-BMI‑1 transfected cells, whereas the expression levels of the epithelial markers E-cadherin and keratin were increased relative to the respective control cells."

This sentence shows that when Bmi1 is knocked down, epithelial marker expression (E-cadherin) increases. However, it indicates Bmi1 **promotes** EMT and is associated with *loss* of epithelial markers. In other words, Bmi1 itself is an *EMT-promoting factor* and suppressed during loss of EMT signaling—NOT suppressed by EMT signaling.

# Conclusion
There is no evidence that Bmi1 is suppressed by enhanced EMT or loss of epithelial marker expression. Instead, the opposite is found: Bmi1 is associated with EMT promotion and with the loss of epithelial phenotype.
