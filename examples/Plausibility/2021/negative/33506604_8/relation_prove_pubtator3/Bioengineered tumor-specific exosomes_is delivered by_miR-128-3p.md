# literature evidence
## "Exosomal miR-128-3p Promotes Epithelial-to-Mesenchymal Transition in Colorectal Cancer Cells by Targeting FOXO4 via TGF-beta/SMAD and JAK/STAT3 Signaling" (PMID: 33634112)
"High expression of miR-128-3p was identified in exosomes derived from EMT-induced HCT-116 cells. ... We revealed that exosomal miR-128-3p regulates EMT by directly suppressing its downstream target gene FOXO4 to activate TGF-beta/SMAD and JAK/STAT3 signaling, and the properties of the miR-128-3p/FOXO4 axis were horizontally transferred via exosomal delivery."

## "Gastric cancer cell-derived exosomal miRNA-128-3p promotes angiogenesis by targeting SASH1" (PMID: 39664191)
"Among these miRNAs, microRNA-128-3p (miR-128-3p) was highly expressed in serum exosomes isolated from gastric cancer patients, as confirmed by high-throughput sequencing and subsequent experiments. Coculture of gastric cancer-derived exosomes overexpressing miR-128-3p with human umbilical vein endothelial cells (HUVECs) significantly enhanced HUVEC proliferation, migration and angiogenesis."

# Analysis
The literature shows that exosomes, including those derived from cancer cells (which can be considered tumor-specific in origin), deliver miR-128-3p to recipient cells. However, none of the reviewed abstracts or available evidence specify the use of "bioengineered" exosomes targeted specifically for tumor application delivering miR-128-3p. They only describe endogenous or cancer-cell-derived exosomes as vehicles.

Answer: False
