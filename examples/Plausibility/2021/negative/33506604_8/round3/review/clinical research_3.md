• This restoration is expected to trigger a phenotypic switch from epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EMT) or cancer stem cell-like states to mesenchymal-to-epithelial transition (MET), thereby resensitizing tumor cells to chemotherapy.
• The approach will be investigated using advanced methodologies such as single-cell sequencing, digital droplet PCR, and live-cell imaging to validate and delineate the molecular pathway.

• The coupling of genome-editing techniques (inducible CRISPR/Cas9) with RNA interference to fine-tune complex cellular processes is an innovative aspect that distinguishes this approach from more traditional single-target strategies.

  - That correction of cell cycle dysregulation will directly ameliorate exosome biogenesis defects.
  - That modulation of the ESCRT complex via RNAi can be precisely controlled to restore miR-200 packaging without off-target effects.
• Evidential Support: The hypothesis draws on well-established roles of p53, Rb, and miR-200 in cancer biology, yet it would benefit from additional preliminary data or literature evidence demonstrating a direct link between these processes in NSCLC.
