1. Observation extraction:
 • Abstract 1 shows that 3D decellularized human liver ECM scaffolds—when used in mono- and co-cultures—affect pro-fibrogenic and cancer marker expression in response to stimuli (e.g., TGF-β1) and drug treatments (Sorafenib, Regorafenib) by recreating a complex microenvironment.
 • Abstract 2 demonstrates that HepG2 cells grown on decellularized rat liver scaffolds maintain higher levels of functional markers and exhibit drug responses more comparable to in vivo conditions than 2D cultures.
 • Abstract 3 describes a 3D-printed, endothelialized liver lobule-like construct that recapitulates native liver architecture and morphology, leading to altered drug resistance compared to simpler models.

2. Causal analysis (individual):
 Observation from Abstract 1:
  a. Cause already established? Yes – the use of decellularized ECM and its inherent biochemical cues naturally recapitulates in vivo microenvironments that modulate cell phenotypes and drug responses.
  b. Could the hypothesis be a causal factor? The hypothesis posits that a custom-engineered 3D scaffold enhances tight junction formation and engages integrin–FAK signaling to activate mechanotransductive (Wnt/β-catenin and YAP/TAZ) and epigenetic pathways. 
  c. Would we see this observation if the hypothesis was true: Possibly, since enhanced cell–cell/ECM interactions could modify downstream signaling and drug responses. 
  d. Novel explanation? Not a missing piece. Current explanations already attribute the effects to the natural, complex ECM cues rather than a specific sequence of integrin-mediated FAK signaling and targeted epigenetic reprogramming.

 Observation from Abstract 2:
  a. Cause already established? Yes – the improved functional capacity and in vivo-like drug responses are explained by the natural composition and architecture of the decellularized liver scaffold.
  b. Could the hypothesis be a causal factor? The hypothesis’s requirement for specific mechanical and biochemical tuning might mimic some of the benefits of decellularized scaffolds; enhanced tight junctions and pathway activation could conceivably contribute.
  c. Would we see this observation if the hypothesis was true: It is possible, as more robust cell–cell and cell–ECM interactions can lead to more realistic cellular functions.
  d. Novel explanation? Not a missing piece. Established literature already attributes these effects to the scaffold’s inherent ECM properties without invoking a sequential integrin/FAK to epigenetic reprogramming cascade.

 Observation from Abstract 3:
  a. Cause already established? Yes – the recreation of liver lobule-like structure using a controlled 3D printing process naturally leads to drug resistance patterns that mirror native tissue, largely due to spatial arrangement and ECM mimicry.
  b. Could the hypothesis be a causal factor? The hypothesis could theoretically contribute to such organized structures through enhanced cell–cell/ECM interactions but does not offer a uniquely distinct mechanism compared to established physical and biochemical cues.
  c. Would we see this observation if the hypothesis was true: Some aspects, like improved tight junction formation and activation of mechanotransduction pathways, might contribute; however, the observation is more directly explained by the physical replication of tissue architecture.
  d. Novel explanation? Not a missing piece—standard ECM-based architectural replication already provides a robust explanation for the observed phenomena.

3. Causal analysis (summary):
 Would we see some of the observations if the hypothesis was true: Yes, a scaffold engineered with adjustable mechanical properties and specific ligands might enhance cell interactions and function. However, the observed effects in the abstracts are already well accounted for by the established fact that natural or engineered 3D ECM scaffolds inherently recapitulate in vivo-like cell behavior. Thus, the sequential integrin-mediated FAK signaling leading to Wnt/β-catenin, YAP/TAZ activation and subsequent epigenetic reprogramming is not clearly required to explain the observations; standard ECM effects provide a robust, simpler explanation.

4. Disproof analysis:
 Does some observations disprove the hypothesis: None of the observations directly contradict the hypothesis. However, they do not require the specific mechanotransductive and epigenetic causal chain proposed, as the conventional explanation based on ECM mimicry is sufficient to explain the improved cell functions and drug responses.
