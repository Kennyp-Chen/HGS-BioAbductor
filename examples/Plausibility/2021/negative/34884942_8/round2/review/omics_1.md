These signals are proposed to initiate epigenetic modifications at cytochrome P450 gene promoters (manifested as increased histone acetylation and decreased DNA methylation), thereby restoring or enhancing the expression of CYP isoforms. The overall goal is to leverage advanced techniques (high-content imaging, real-time PCR, and ChIP-seq) to establish a HepG2 model that more faithfully mimics human hepatocyte drug metabolism.

This hypothesis integrates several contemporary themes in cellular biology: the influence of 3D culture systems on cell behavior, the role of mechanotransduction in determining cellular phenotype, and the connection between these mechanical cues and epigenetic regulation of drug-metabolizing enzymes. While the use of 3D scaffolds and the study of mechanotransductive pathways in hepatocyte-like cells are not entirely novel, the particular convergence of these elements to actively reprogram epigenetic marks at CYP gene promoters is innovative. By proposing a dual signal cascade (physical and biochemical) leading to a therapeutically relevant functional outcome (drug metabolism), the hypothesis introduces a fresh perspective that could potentially bridge gaps between tissue engineering and epigenomic regulation.

Each step follows a plausible biological rationale, though the chain of events is complex and involves numerous regulatory checkpoints.

  - Mechanotransductive pathways like Wnt/β-catenin and YAP/TAZ are well known to respond to changes in the cellular microenvironment.
