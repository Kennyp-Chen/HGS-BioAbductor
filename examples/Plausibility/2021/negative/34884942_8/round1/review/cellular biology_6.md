1. Hypothesis Summary  
─────────────────────────────  
The hypothesis proposes that in HepG2 cells—a widely used hepatocyte-derived cell line—the targeted inhibition or genetic silencing of DLK1, which is known as a downstream effector of abnormal Wnt/β‐catenin signaling, will induce a shift toward a more mature hepatocyte phenotype. This phenotypic maturation is expected to occur through the restoration of key drug-metabolizing enzymes, thereby making the cell line more suitable for applications in drug metabolism and toxicity testing.

2. Originality Assessment

• It represents an innovative approach by suggesting that modulating a single downstream effector (DLK1) could resolve multiple downstream issues—namely, the immature state of HepG2 cells and their deficient expression of drug-metabolizing enzymes.  
• While the Wnt/β‐catenin pathway’s involvement in liver development and carcinogenesis is well recognized, positioning DLK1 as a critical nexus for restoring mature hepatocyte functions offers a fresh perspective that may extend beyond conventional differentiation protocols.  
• Thus, in the context of current literature, the hypothesis has elements of novelty and is sufficiently distinct from established paradigms that primarily focus on broader pathway modulation rather than specific downstream effectors.
