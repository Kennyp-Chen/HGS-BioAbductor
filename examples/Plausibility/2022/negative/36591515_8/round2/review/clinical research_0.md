1. Hypothesis Summary:
 • The hypothesis proposes that in the hypoxic tumor microenvironment, cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs) activate autophagy via HIF-1α signaling. This activation leads to the overproduction of IL-6, a process potentially amplified by fibroblast activation protein (FAP)-mediated pathways.  
 • The secreted IL-6 then binds to its receptors on adjacent tumor cells, triggering the JAK2/STAT3 signaling cascade. This cascade results in STAT3 translocation into the nucleus and the upregulation of genes that promote epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EMT) and chemoresistance, thereby facilitating tumor invasion and resistance to therapy.  
 • The hypothesis further contends that using advanced single-cell and spatial transcriptomics to delineate these interactions, along with CRISPR-based targeted knockdown of autophagy regulators (e.g., ATG5/7) specifically in CAFs, could selectively inhibit this pathway. This inhibition should reduce IL-6 secretion, diminish the activation of the JAK2/STAT3 axis in tumor cells, and improve treatment outcomes—potentially through integrated therapies (like IL-6 neutralization and JAK2 inhibition).
