1. Hypothesis Summary  
The hypothesis posits that by selectively modulating the phosphorylation of p62 at a key residue (Ser349) using targeted kinase modulators and CRISPR-engineered phosphomutants, one can finely regulate the p62-Keap1-Nrf2 signaling axis in liver cells (hepatocytes). The goal is to induce a temporary, optimal elevation of Nrf2 activity that provides robust protection against oxidative stress without triggering harmful chronic Nrf2 activation, which could lead to metabolic reprogramming, liver fibrosis, and tumor formation. Furthermore, the hypothesis integrates advanced phosphoproteomics and real-time multi-omics analyses to quantitatively distinguish between transient beneficial and prolonged deleterious activation states, thereby refining therapeutic strategies for liver disease.

The integration of cutting-edge multi-omics and phosphoproteomic techniques to dynamically monitor the pathway's behavior further distinguishes this work from many existing studies. While elements of the concept are rooted in established literature, the way these elements are combined to achieve a controlled, transient versus chronic activation state offers a distinct and progressive perspective within the field.
