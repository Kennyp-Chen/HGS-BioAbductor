1. Hypothesis Summary

The hypothesis posits that a precision-guided, dual-intervention strategy can therapeutically recalibrate the p62/Keap1/Nrf2 signaling axis in liver diseases. This strategy involves two coordinated interventions: first, the targeted and intermittent induction of selective autophagy through nanoparticle-mediated delivery (or optogenetic regulators) to periodically clear accumulated p62; and second, the controlled pharmacological activation of Nrf2 using real-time biomarker-adjusted dosing. The aim is to create an intermittent, protective pattern of Nrf2 activity that minimizes oxidative injury while preventing the pathological metabolic shifts that lead to fibrosis and tumorigenesis, leveraging real-time biosensor feedback, advanced drug delivery systems, and time-resolved proteomics.

• Innovative Combination: The integration of nanotechnology-based drug delivery, optogenetic regulation, and real-time biomarker feedback to achieve temporal control of both autophagy and Nrf2 activation is a novel and ambitious approach. • Distinct Perspective: The idea of intermittently resetting Nrf2 activity by clearing p62—a central node in the regulation of the signaling axis—is a fresh perspective compared to more traditional approaches that target these pathways in a continuous, unmodulated manner.

• State-of-the-Art Methods: Leveraging real-time biosensing and advanced drug delivery systems also distinguishes the hypothesis from many existing studies, which typically lack such dynamic modulation techniques.

• Biological Plausibility: The hypothesis is rooted in a solid understanding of the p62/Keap1/Nrf2 axis, where p62 accumulation can drive sustained Nrf2 activation, sometimes leading to deleterious metabolic shifts. The strategy to periodically clear p62 via autophagy induction to “reset” this axis is logically coherent and aligns with the current mechanistic insights into these pathways.

• Intervention Strategy: The combination of two interventions (selective autophagy induction and controlled Nrf2 activation) is theoretically sound. The proposal to use nanoparticle-mediated delivery or optogenetic regulators for autophagy induction, along with real-time biomarker-based dosing for Nrf2 activation, adds an attractive degree of precision control. However, the complexity of synchronizing these interventions raises questions about the feasibility and reproducibility of timing and dosage in a clinical or even preclinical setting.

• Technological Feasibility: While advanced drug delivery and biosensor technologies have progressed, integrating them into a seamless clinical workflow that provides real-time adjustments remains a formidable challenge. The use of optogenetics, in particular, although well established in experimental models, may face significant hurdles in translation to human liver physiology.

• Evidence and Theoretical Grounding: The hypothesis is grounded in current scientific concepts, but it would benefit from more detailed mechanistic evidence or preliminary data supporting both the intermittent modulation approach and the interplay between the two interventions. The logical link between periodic p62 clearance and the prevention of fibrosis and tumorigenesis is compelling, yet it needs further substantiation in experimental models.
