1. Hypothesis Summary

The hypothesis posits that chronic alcohol exposure disrupts hepatocyte autophagy, leading to the accumulation of the protein p62. This build‐up of p62 is suggested to sequester Keap1, which in turn results in sustained activation of the transcription factor Nrf2. Initially, Nrf2 activation is seen as protective—limiting oxidative stress—but when activation is prolonged, it purportedly drives an anabolic shift characterized by increased lipogenesis and cellular proliferation. Moreover, the hypothesis integrates additional layers by implicating ethanol-induced impairment of retinoid signaling and possible interactions with inflammatory pathways, collectively suggesting that these events contribute to the progression from alcoholic liver disease to hepatocellular carcinoma. The planned experimental approach involves advanced CRISPR screening and single-cell transcriptomic analyses to dynamically map changes in Nrf2 activity and metabolic gene expression over time.

Furthermore, integrating the disruption of retinoid signaling and its interplay with inflammatory pathways offers a multifaceted perspective that is less commonly addressed in one unified model. While each component has precedents in the literature, the synthesis of these components and the innovative use of CRISPR screening along with single-cell transcriptomic analyses to define the transition threshold is a creative and original approach that distinguishes this work from more traditional, linear explanations of alcohol-induced liver injury.
