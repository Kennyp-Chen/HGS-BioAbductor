1. Hypothesis Summary

The hypothesis proposes that chronic alcohol exposure disrupts autophagy in liver cells (hepatocytes), causing the accumulation of the protein p62. This buildup of p62 sequesters Keap1, a protein that normally regulates the degradation of Nrf2. With Keap1 inhibited, Nrf2 becomes persistently activated. Although such activation may initially serve a protective role by reducing oxidative damage, chronic Nrf2 activation triggers anabolic reprogramming. This reprogramming drives lipogenesis (fat production) and cellular proliferation, ultimately contributing to the progression from alcoholic liver disease (ALD) to hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC).
