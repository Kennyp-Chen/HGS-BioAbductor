1. Hypothesis Summary

The hypothesis proposes that lithocholic acid (LCA), at physiological concentrations typically found in breast tissue, activates the TGR5 receptor on tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs). This activation triggers a signaling cascade via cAMP/PKA and NF-κB pathways that reprograms these macrophages from a pro-tumor (M2) phenotype to an anti-tumor (M1) phenotype. The resulting repolarization is suggested to enhance local immune surveillance, promote tumor cell apoptosis, and thereby inhibit breast cancer progression while also potentiating the effects of chemotherapy. Additionally, the hypothesis advocates for using modern nanotechnology-based targeted delivery of LCA along with high-resolution omics technologies to gain a deeper mechanistic insight and improve the therapeutic strategy.
