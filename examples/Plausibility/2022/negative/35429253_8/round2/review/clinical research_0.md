1. Hypothesis Summary  
──────────────────────────────
The hypothesis proposes that by using a precision intervention strategy—which combines AI-driven personalization of dietary and probiotic plans based on detailed metagenomic and metabolomic profiling—it is possible to alter the gut microbiota. The intended modulation aims to shift the bile acid composition by elevating levels of ursodeoxycholic acid (UDCA) relative to deoxycholic acid (DCA) and lithocholic acid (LCA). This altered bile acid profile is projected to favor bile acid receptor activity (with selective stimulation of FXR and VDR), thereby inhibiting inflammatory and proliferative pathways mediated by NF-κB and MAPK. Ultimately, the hypothesis envisions that these changes will lead to a reduction in epithelial–mesenchymal transition (a key process in cancer metastasis) and tumor cell proliferation, particularly in cancers such as colorectal and esophageal adenocarcinoma.

- Although individualized interventions and gut microbiota modulation are recognized in current research, the explicit combination of these methods to reprogram bile acid receptor signaling for targeting cancer pathways (including epithelial–mesenchymal transition) offers a fresh perspective that distinguishes it from existing studies.
