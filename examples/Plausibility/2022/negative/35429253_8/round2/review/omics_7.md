1. Hypothesis Summary:

The hypothesis proposes that strategic modulation of the gut microbiome—using targeted interventions (strain-specific probiotics, prebiotics, or fecal microbiota transplantation)—in combination with nanodelivery systems to administer bile acid receptor modulators, will alter bile acid metabolism in the gut. Specifically, it suggests that these interventions will shift the bile acid profile toward a “favorable” state (increasing levels of ursodeoxycholic acid [UDCA] while decreasing deoxycholic acid [DCA]). This new profile is expected to preferentially stimulate anti-tumorigenic receptors (such as FXR, TGR5, and PXR) which in turn would suppress NF-kB–mediated inflammatory pathways, enhance apoptotic and anti-inflammatory signaling, and inhibit both epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EMT) and the maintenance of cancer stem cells. The hypothesis further asserts that comprehensive multi-omics analyses can be employed to quantitatively and mechanistically verify these molecular changes and ultimately link them to a reduced risk of colorectal cancer.
