1. Hypothesis Summary:
The hypothesis proposes that at physiological concentrations in breast tissue, lithocholic acid (LCA) binds and activates the TGR5 receptor on tumor-associated macrophages. This activation triggers a cAMP/PKA signaling cascade that interacts with NF-κB, causing these macrophages to shift from an immunosuppressive M2 phenotype to an immunostimulatory M1 state. In addition, the hypothesis suggests that employing a targeted, nanotechnology-enabled delivery system can locally increase LCA’s bioavailability within the tumor microenvironment, thus enhancing immune surveillance, promoting tumor cell apoptosis, and improving the efficacy of chemotherapeutics. Validation of each step is intended to be accomplished using high-resolution omics platforms.

2. Originality Assessment:
The idea of using bile acids such as LCA to modulate immune cell phenotypes via the TGR5 receptor is relatively innovative, particularly in the context of reprogramming tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs) within the breast tumor microenvironment. Incorporating targeted nanotechnology for delivery further distinguishes the approach from established paradigms, as it attempts to overcome physiological concentration limitations and improve localized therapeutic action. Although both macrophage polarization and nanodelivery systems are active areas of research, combining these strategies in the context of using LCA and targeting TGR5 provides a cross-disciplinary angle that is both novel and promising for future studies.

The mechanistic chain is plausible, as TGR5 activation is known to influence intracellular cAMP/PKA pathways, and NF-κB plays a pivotal role in immune responses. Nonetheless, the precise molecular interplay between these signaling molecules in the context of macrophage polarization remains complex and not fully elucidated.

  - The initial premise that LCA, at physiological concentrations, can activate TGR5 in tumor-associated macrophages is grounded in emerging literature; however, more evidence may be necessary to confirm that these concentrations are sufficient for a measurable effect in situ.
