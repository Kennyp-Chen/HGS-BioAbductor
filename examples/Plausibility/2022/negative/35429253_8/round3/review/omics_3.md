1. Hypothesis Summary  
The hypothesis posits that at physiological concentrations in breast tissue, lithocholic acid (LCA) engages the TGR5 receptor on tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs), thereby activating a cAMP/PKA signaling cascade that interacts with NF-κB. This interaction reprograms these macrophages from an immunosuppressive M2 phenotype to an immunostimulatory M1 state. To overcome natural concentration limitations of LCA in the tumor microenvironment, the hypothesis further proposes the use of targeted, nanotechnology-enabled delivery systems. This approach is anticipated to elevate local LCA activity, which in turn should boost immune surveillance, enhance tumor cell apoptosis, and improve the efficacy of chemotherapeutic treatments. Integrated high-resolution omics platforms are proposed as the means to rigorously validate each step of this mechanistic chain.

At the convergence of immunometabolism, bile acid biology, and cancer immunotherapy, this hypothesis introduces a novel and innovative perspective. While the role of bile acids (and TGR5 signaling) in metabolic regulation is established, repurposing LCA to directly modulate TAM polarization in the tumor microenvironment is less explored. Furthermore, the proposal to combine nanotechnology-enabled targeted delivery with the modulation of immune cell phenotypes provides a distinctive integration of therapeutic strategies that distinguishes this hypothesis from more conventional immunotherapy approaches. Hence, it offers an innovative perspective by bridging molecular signaling with advanced drug delivery systems.

3. Validity and Strength Evaluation

• The chain of events—from LCA binding to TGR5, triggering a cAMP/PKA cascade, modulating NF-κB activity, and ultimately shifting macrophage polarization—is conceptually coherent. Each step leverages known signaling modules, lending plausibility to the sequence as presented.

• The involvement of the cAMP/PKA pathway and its crosstalk with NF-κB adds mechanistic detail, although the specifics of how this interaction precisely leads to M1 polarization in TAMs need further experimental clarification.  

Strengths:

Considerations and Potential Limitations:  
• The hypothesis assumes that physiological concentrations of LCA, even when boosted via nanodelivery, will exclusively and effectively reprogram TAMs without off-target effects, which might not hold true given the pleiotropic nature of bile acids.  
• The precise molecular interplay between cAMP/PKA signaling and NF-κB activity in macrophage polarization is not fully elucidated, which leaves a gap in the mechanistic explanation.  
• The potential impact of other components within the tumor microenvironment on both LCA bioactivity and macrophage behavior is an area that warrants further exploration.
