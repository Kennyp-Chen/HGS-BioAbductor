1. Observation extraction:
 • Bile acids are produced in the liver as primary bile acids and are converted by gut bacteria into diverse metabolites, including secondary bile acids.
 • Gut bacteria modulate bile acid metabolism, leading to both pro‐carcinogenic (e.g., deoxycholic acid, DCA) and potentially tumor‐suppressive (e.g., UDCA) effects.
 • Differential expression of bile acid receptors (e.g., FXR versus TGR5) among cancers contributes to variable responses, with some cancers showing anticancer effects at physiological/basal concentrations of bile acids.
 • Nanodelivery of bile acid receptor modulators has been shown to change the tumor immune microenvironment in liver cancer.
 • The dual (Janus-faced) nature of bile acids in carcinogenesis is highlighted, where concentration and type of bile acid determine their pro- or anti-tumor effects.
 • Gut microbiota-derived metabolites (including bile acid metabolites and SCFAs) are implicated in gastrointestinal carcinogenesis.
