1. Hypothesis Summary  
──────────────────────────────
The hypothesis posits that in fibrotic livers a self-reinforcing loop exists where increased extracellular matrix (ECM) stiffness drives tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs) toward an M2 (pro-tumorigenic) phenotype. In turn, these M2 TAMs secrete factors that further increase ECM stiffness, thereby promoting hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) progression. A central mechanistic claim is that integrin/FAK-mediated mechanotransduction in TAMs plays a pivotal role in both the initial polarization and the perpetuation of this loop. Therefore, by interrupting the integrin/FAK signaling pathway in TAMs, it is proposed that one could repolarize these cells toward an M1 (anti-tumorigenic) phenotype and suppress liver cancer progression.
