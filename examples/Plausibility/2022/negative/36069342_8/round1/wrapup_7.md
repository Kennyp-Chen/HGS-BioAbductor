Below is a synthesized meta-analysis report that integrates the insights and recurring critique points from the provided reviews. The focus is on identifying common issues and offering actionable recommendations for researchers designing future proposals in the area of exosome‐mediated immunometabolic reprogramming in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC).

──────────────────────────────
1. Overarching Critique of the Hypothesis Concept

Across all reviews, the hypothesis—in which targeting the exosomal PKM2–PFKFB3 signaling axis is proposed as a means to reprogram tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs) from a pro-tumoral M2 phenotype to an antitumoral M1 phenotype, thereby enhancing CD8⁺ T cell–mediated responses and immune checkpoint blockade efficacy—is recognized as innovative. However, the reviewers consistently noted that—as compelling as the concept is—it currently relies on several key assumptions and lacks robust mechanistic detail.

──────────────────────────────
2. Recurring Critique Points

A. Insufficient Mechanistic Detail
 • All analyses emphasize that while the general framework is logical, there is a need for a step-by-step elucidation of the signaling cascade.  
 • Critical intermediate steps remain unspecified—for example, how exosomal PKM2 modulates PFKFB3 activity and which downstream mediators govern macrophage reprogramming.  
 • A detailed mechanistic model would increase the hypothesis’s scientific robustness and aid in designing targeted experiments.

B. Over-Simplification of Macrophage Phenotypes
 • The binary interpretation of macrophage polarization (M1 vs. M2) is repeatedly flagged as an oversimplification.  
 • Recent studies reveal that macrophage activation operates along a continuum; therefore, reliance solely on classical markers may not capture the full complexity.  
 • Future proposals should incorporate additional phenotypic and functional markers to provide a nuanced view of TAM plasticity.

C. Gaps in Direct Evidence
 • There is a noted lack of direct experimental data linking the exosomal PKM2–PFKFB3 axis with effective TAM repolarization and subsequent CD8⁺ T cell activation.  
 • The reviewers call for more preclinical validation—both in vitro and in vivo—to establish causality rather than relying on parallel lines of evidence from metabolism, exosome biology, and immunotherapy separately.

D. Consideration of Tumor Microenvironment Complexity
 • Several critiques addressed the need to account for the heterogeneity of the HCC microenvironment, including potential compensatory pathways and off-target effects.  
 • Confounding variables and the impact of other exosomal cargo on TAM phenotype should be thoroughly discussed and experimentally controlled.

──────────────────────────────
3. Actionable Insights for Future Proposals

Based on the recurring issues raised by the reviews, researchers should consider the following recommendations when developing future proposals:

A. Elaborate the Mechanistic Pathway
 • Develop a detailed schematic that traces the steps from exosomal release of PKM2 to PFKFB3 activation in TAMs.  
 • Identify and characterize intermediary signaling molecules or transcription factors that mediate the phenotypic switch from M2 to M1.  
 • Clearly delineate how metabolic reprogramming in TAMs leads to enhanced CD8⁺ T cell–mediated immunity.

B. Expand Experimental Evidence
 • Design targeted in vitro models to validate the functional impact of the PKM2–PFKFB3 axis on TAM polarization.  
 • Incorporate in vivo studies using HCC models that can assess changes in TAM populations, immune cell infiltration, and the overall efficacy of immune checkpoint blockade when the axis is modulated.  
 • Utilize genetic and pharmacological manipulation strategies to isolate the effects of the PKM2–PFKFB3 pathway from other concurrent metabolic or exosomal signaling cascades.

C. Refine Macrophage Phenotyping Strategies
 • Move beyond the classic M1/M2 dichotomy by integrating broader panels of markers that capture the spectrum of macrophage activation states.  
 • Use single-cell analysis or multiplex assays to map TAM heterogeneity and better correlate metabolic alterations with functional immune outcomes.

D. Address the Broader Microenvironment
 • Acknowledge and investigate additional components of exosomes and other signaling pathways that may influence TAM behavior.  
 • Consider combination strategies that might simultaneously target multiple axes within the tumor microenvironment to overcome its complexity and redundancy.

──────────────────────────────
4. Final Remarks

The collective reviews acknowledge the innovative potential of targeting the exosomal PKM2–PFKFB3 axis to reprogram TAMs and boost immunotherapy in HCC. However, a stronger emphasis on mechanistic depth, direct experimental evidence, comprehensive phenotyping, and an appreciation of tumor microenvironment complexity is essential. Addressing these points will not only strengthen the hypothesis but also provide clearer experimental roadmaps for future studies. This integrated approach can ensure that subsequent proposals are both mechanistically robust and translationally relevant in the rapidly evolving field of cancer immunometabolism.
