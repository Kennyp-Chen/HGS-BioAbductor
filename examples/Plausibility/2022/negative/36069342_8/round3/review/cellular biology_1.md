 • The hypothesis posits that in fibrotic liver disease, increased extracellular matrix (ECM) stiffness triggers two parallel mechanotransduction pathways. In contrast, liver-resident macrophages respond through an integrin β5/FAK/MEK/ERK cascade, which polarizes them toward an immunosuppressive (M2-like) state. These M2-like macrophages then secrete factors such as TGF-β, LOXL2, and other ECM remodeling enzymes, which further increase matrix stiffness and thus establish a self-reinforcing, pro-tumorigenic feedback loop. Moreover, the hypothesis suggests that high stiffness conditions also induce the activation of YAP/TAZ signaling in both cell types, further sustaining this pathogenic condition. As a therapeutic strategy, the authors propose combining inhibitors of the integrin β1 and β5 pathways with YAP/TAZ modulators, with their effects monitored via advanced real-time single-cell transcriptomics and stiffness-sensing technologies, to disrupt this loop and reduce hepatocellular carcinoma progression.

 • The specific separation of integrin β1 signaling in cancer cells and integrin β5 signaling in macrophages offers a novel perspective, as integrin β5’s role in macrophage polarization is less prominently featured in existing studies.
 • The integrated therapeutic approach using pathway-specific inhibitors monitored by real-time single-cell measurements also adds a forward-thinking angle that could set this hypothesis apart from more traditional or isolated mechanistic studies.
