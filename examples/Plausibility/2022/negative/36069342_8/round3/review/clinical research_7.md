1. Hypothesis Summary  
─────────────────────────────  
The hypothesis posits that in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), reprogramming tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs) from an immunosuppressive (M2-like) state to an antitumoral (M1-like) state can be achieved by simultaneously targeting two interconnected metabolic and signaling pathways. Specifically, it proposes that:  

• Inhibition of the exosomal PKM2–PFKFB3 metabolic axis (using TAM-targeted nanocarriers to deliver PKM2 inhibitors or siRNA, alongside PFKFB3 modulators) will reduce glycolysis-induced PD-L1 expression in TAMs.  
• Concurrent modulation of the hypoxic tumor microenvironment—through reducing lactate accumulation and interfering with Wnt signaling—will further reinforce the antitumor immune response.  
• These interventions are expected to restore CD8+ T cell functionality and synergize with immune checkpoint blockade therapies for improved tumor control in HCC.  
• State-of-the-art real-time imaging and biosensors are incorporated to dynamically monitor metabolic and immunologic changes, enabling precise therapeutic adjustments.

─────────────────────────────  
• Innovative Integration:  
  The hypothesis creatively integrates several emerging concepts—metabolic reprogramming of TAMs via the exosomal PKM2–PFKFB3 axis, targeted drug delivery using nanocarriers, and modulation of the hypoxic and Wnt signaling tumor microenvironment. Although TAM reprogramming and metabolic interventions in HCC have been explored separately in various studies, combining these approaches in a single, multipronged strategy is relatively novel.  

• Differentiation from Existing Studies:  
  While aspects of targeting glycolysis and immune checkpoints are established areas of research, the specific coupling of exosome-mediated metabolic regulation with TAM reprogramming and microenvironment modulation (hypoxia/lactate and Wnt signaling) distinguishes this hypothesis. It offers an innovative perspective by proposing that simultaneous intervention at multiple levels may create a synergistic effect, potentially overcoming limitations of monotherapies.
