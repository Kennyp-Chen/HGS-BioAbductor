1. Hypothesis Summary
────────────────────────────
The hypothesis asserts that in fibrotic liver disease, increased extracellular matrix (ECM) stiffness activates distinct, cell-specific signaling pathways that establish a self-reinforcing pro-tumorigenic niche. Specifically, in cancer cells, ECM stiffness triggers the integrin β1/FAK pathway, which enhances malignant behavior. In contrast, liver-resident macrophages respond to the same stiffness through integrin β5/FAK/MEK/ERK signaling, leading them to adopt an immunosuppressive M2 phenotype. These M2-like macrophages, in turn, secrete factors such as TGF-β, LOXL2, and other ECM remodeling enzymes that further stiffen the ECM, forming a feedback loop that bolsters both tumor growth and fibrosis. Additionally, the hypothesis highlights the compensatory activation of YAP/TAZ signaling in both cell types under high stiffness conditions, contributing to the persistence of this pathogenic state. It concludes by proposing a combinatorial therapeutic strategy targeting integrin β1 and β5 pathways along with YAP/TAZ modulation, monitored using real-time single-cell transcriptomics and advanced stiffness-sensing methods, to disrupt this cycle and mitigate hepatocellular carcinoma progression.

• Novelty in Integration: While research on ECM stiffness, integrin-mediated mechanotransduction, and the role of YAP/TAZ in cancer and fibrosis is established, this hypothesis uniquely integrates these facets by delineating cell-type–specific responses in the liver’s microenvironment.  
• Dual Pathway Divergence: The differentiation between integrin β1 signaling in malignant cells and integrin β5 signaling driving macrophage polarization is a unique angle, offering an innovative perspective on how ECM stiffness may concurrently modulate both tumor and immune cell behavior.  
• Therapeutic Approach: The proposition of a combinatorial therapeutic strategy that simultaneously targets these converging mechanical and biochemical pathways, using real-time single-cell approaches for monitoring, underscores a forward-thinking and potentially impactful treatment concept.  
• Overall: Although many individual elements have been explored in separate studies, their systematic and concurrent involvement in a feed-forward loop in fibrotic liver disease and hepatocellular carcinoma development provides a novel, integrative hypothesis that distinguishes itself from more reductionist or single-pathway studies.
