1. Hypothesis Summary

The hypothesis posits that in fibrotic liver disease, increased extracellular matrix (ECM) stiffness elicits two distinct mechanotransduction responses. In cancer cells, enhanced stiffness activates integrin β1/FAK signaling, thereby promoting malignant behavior. In parallel, liver-resident macrophages sense this stiffness via integrin β5, triggering a signaling cascade through FAK/MEK/ERK that polarizes these cells into an immunosuppressive M2 phenotype. The M2-like macrophages, in turn, secrete pro-fibrotic and ECM-remodeling factors such as TGF-β and LOXL2, which further stiffen the matrix. This establishes a self-perpetuating pro-tumorigenic feedback loop. Additionally, persistent high-stiffness conditions induce compensatory activation of YAP/TAZ signaling in both cell types. Based on these interconnected pathways, the hypothesis further proposes that a therapeutic combination targeting both integrin β1 and β5 pathways along with modulators of the YAP/TAZ pathway, monitored using sophisticated techniques (e.g., real-time single-cell transcriptomics and stiffness-sensing technologies), will disrupt the loop by repolarizing macrophages and lowering cancer cell malignancy, ultimately impeding hepatocellular carcinoma progression.

   - Differential integrin isoform usage (β1 in cancer cells and β5 in macrophages).
   - The downstream coupling of these pathways with YAP/TAZ signaling.
   - A comprehensive therapeutic strategy that not only targets these pathways but also employs advanced real-time monitoring.

• While each pathway or component has been individually addressed in the literature, the coupling of these specific mechanisms into one self-reinforcing feedback loop and the concomitant targeting strategy represents an innovative perspective. The dual pathway focus (cancer cells and macrophages) underscores a level of sophistication that can drive new directions in the field.

   - The incorporation of YAP/TAZ signaling as a compensatory mechanism under persistent stiffness conditions is consistent with current findings in mechanotransduction literature.

   - The hypothesis is built on a solid mechanistic framework that integrates extracellular signals with intracellular responses. However, the current body of literature may not completely support such a neatly divided role for the two integrins and their exclusive downstream pathways. Empirical discrepancies or context-specific variations (e.g., tissue-specific responses or heterogeneity within macrophage populations) need to be acknowledged.

However, the feasibility and resolution limits of these technologies in a dense fibrotic environment should be critically considered.

• Strength:
