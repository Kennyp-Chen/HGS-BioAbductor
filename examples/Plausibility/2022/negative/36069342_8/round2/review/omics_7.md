1. Hypothesis Summary

The central idea posits that by targeting an exosome-mediated metabolic axis—specifically the PKM2–PFKFB3 pathway—in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) and simultaneously considering the influence of a hypoxia-driven lactate environment and modulatory signals like Wnt signaling, it is possible to shift tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs) from a tumor-promoting (M2) state to a tumor-fighting (M1) phenotype. The strategy involves using precision nanocarriers to deliver either PKM2 inhibitors or siRNA and PFKFB3 modulators directly into TAMs. This targeted inhibition is expected to dampen glycolysis-driven PD-L1 expression, thereby restoring CD8+ T cell activity and enhancing the effectiveness of immune checkpoint blockade treatments. Overall, the approach integrates metabolic reprogramming with immune modulation to counteract the suppressive HCC tumor microenvironment.

• Accounting for the additional layers of complexity, such as the hypoxia-induced lactate milieu and Wnt signaling influences, further distinguishes the approach, suggesting a multifaceted attack on tumor biology.

• Incorporation of established concepts: Hypoxia and lactate have well-documented roles in shaping the tumor microenvironment, and targeting these could synergize with metabolic interventions.
• Advanced delivery strategies: Precision nanocarriers provide a logical means to overcome challenges of drug delivery specificity, which is critical for affecting TAMs without systemic off-target effects.

• Mechanistic clarity: While the hypothesis suggests that modulating these pathways will reduce PD-L1 expression through decreased glycolysis, detailed mechanistic insights are needed to establish a direct causal link between modulation of the PKM2–PFKFB3 axis and the reprogramming of TAMs.
• Translational hurdles: The precision nanocarrier system, though promising in theory, may encounter challenges related to targeting efficiency, stability, and controlled release in the complex in vivo tumor microenvironment.
