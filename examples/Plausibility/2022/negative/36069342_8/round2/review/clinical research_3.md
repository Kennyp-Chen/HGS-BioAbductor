1. Hypothesis Summary

The hypothesis posits that in the fibrotic liver, an increase in extracellular matrix stiffness—either by itself or in combination with inflammatory cytokines—activates a dual mechanotransduction cascade in tumor‐associated macrophages (TAMs). Specifically, it is proposed that integrin β5 triggers two signaling routes: one via FAK/MEK/ERK and another via YAP/TAZ. These dual pathways then lead to the upregulation of HIF‐1α and LOXL2. The cascade is hypothesized to drive a shift in TAM metabolism toward heightened fatty acid oxidation and glycolysis, resulting in elevated PD‐L1 expression. This, in turn, suppresses CD8+ T cell cytotoxicity and promotes tumor progression. Finally, the hypothesis outlines that the utilization of cutting‐edge bioengineering tools (like tunable 3D hydrogels), precise gene‐editing (e.g., CRISPR-Cas9), and high-resolution live-cell imaging will allow dissection and eventual therapeutic targeting of these pathways to restore antitumor immunity in liver cancer.

• The sequential connection from these pathways to metabolic reprogramming—specifically the enhanced fatty acid oxidation and glycolysis—and ultimately to PD‐L1 expression is a novel integration of mechanical and metabolic influences on immune suppression.
