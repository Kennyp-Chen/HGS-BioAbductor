1. Hypothesis Summary

• The hypothesis posits that in the fibrotic liver, increased extracellular matrix (ECM) stiffness, both independently and combined with inflammatory cytokines, initiates a dual mechanotransduction cascade in tumor‐associated macrophages (TAMs).  
• This cascade involves two key signaling routes: one triggered by integrin β5‐mediated activation of the FAK/MEK/ERK pathway and another involving the YAP/TAZ signaling pathway.  
• Activation of these pathways is proposed to upregulate HIF‐1α and LOXL2, thereby driving a metabolic shift in TAMs toward increased fatty acid oxidation and glycolysis.  
• The metabolic reprogramming ultimately leads to enhanced PD‐L1 expression on TAMs, which suppresses CD8+ T cell cytotoxicity and promotes tumor progression.  
• The hypothesis further suggests that this molecular mechanism can be elucidated and potentially targeted using advanced bioengineering constructs (e.g., tunable 3D hydrogels), precise gene-editing (e.g., CRISPR-Cas9), and high-resolution live-cell imaging.

• The inclusion of advanced experimental tools to both dissect and intervene in this cascade further enhances the originality, suggesting a translational approach that distinguishes it from many purely descriptive studies.  
• Overall, the hypothesis is both forward-thinking and integrative, offering a comprehensive framework that could bridge gaps between fibrosis, mechanobiology, immunometabolism, and cancer immunotherapy.
