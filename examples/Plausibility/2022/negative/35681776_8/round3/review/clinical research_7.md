1. Hypothesis Summary  
─────────────────────────────  
The hypothesis proposes a novel therapeutic strategy for hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) that combines several advanced techniques. Specifically, it suggests using hepatocyte-specific nanoparticles to deliver a CRISPR/Cas9 system engineered for dual targeting. This system is designed to simultaneously suppress the anti-apoptotic protein survivin and modulate epigenetic regulators (DNMT1 and HDACs) to reprogram the tumor microenvironment. The intended outcome is twofold: to restore apoptotic pathways in tumor cells and to reduce the release of immunosuppressive exosomal non-coding RNAs. These effects would promote immunogenic cell death (marked by the release of calreticulin, ATP, and HMGB1), thereby enhancing dendritic cell activation and the recruitment of CD8+ T cells. Furthermore, when combined with PD-1 checkpoint inhibitors, the hypothesis posits that this approach can overcome residual T cell inhibition and synergistically amplify anti-tumor immune responses.

• The focus on altering exosomal non-coding RNA content to disrupt immune evasion provides a fresh perspective, supplementing recent research on exosome biology in cancer.  
• Combining genetic reprogramming with immunotherapy (i.e., PD-1 checkpoint blockade) to create a synergistic anti-tumor effect is conceptually innovative, although multi-modal approaches are increasingly common in the literature.  

Overall, while each individual component has precedence in existing studies, their integration into a unified platform represents an innovative and potentially impactful approach within the field of HCC treatment.
