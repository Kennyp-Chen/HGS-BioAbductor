1. Hypothesis Summary

The hypothesis proposes a novel therapeutic strategy for hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) that uses dual-targeting nanoparticles to deliver CRISPR/Cas9 systems directly to tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs). By specifically disrupting TGF-β receptor (TGF-βR) signaling within these TAMs, the treatment is expected to reprogram them from an M2 (immunosuppressive) to an M1 (pro-inflammatory) phenotype. This reprogramming should enhance antigen presentation and T-cell activation, thereby synergizing with standard immunotherapies to produce superior antitumor responses. The approach emphasizes advanced nanoparticle design with ligand-directed targeting, built-in safety mechanisms to minimize off-target gene editing, and rigorous preclinical monitoring to overcome shortcomings seen in earlier translational efforts—all while maintaining the known link between chronic liver injury, TAM polarization, and the immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment.
