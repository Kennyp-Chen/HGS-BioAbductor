1. Observation extraction:
 • Abstract 1: A combined therapeutic approach using lipid nanoparticles to deliver sorafenib along with siRNA against midkine increases HCC cell sensitivity to chemotherapy. Specific targeting (via the SP94 peptide) and pH‐sensitive lipid modifications further enhance the efficacy and specificity of drug delivery.
 • Abstract 2: Engineered extracellular vesicles (EVs) delivering CRISPR/Cas9 cargo targeting IQGAP1 and FOXM1 overcome sorafenib resistance by reducing cancer stem cell (CSC) properties in HCC, demonstrating that gene editing strategies can tackle resistance mechanisms.
 • Abstract 3: Theragnostic nanoparticles loaded with Survivin siRNA enable active tumor targeting and dual imaging, and suppress tumor growth through autophagy induction and destabilization of DNA integrity in HCC cells.

2. Causal analysis (individual):
 • For Abstract 1:
  – Cause established: The combined effect of sorafenib with MK-siRNA is known to increase drug sensitivity and cytotoxicity.
  – Would we see this observation if the hypothesis was true: If CRISPR/Cas9‐mediated knockout of survivin were applied, one might also observe increased apoptosis in HCC cells; however, Abstract 1 focuses on midkine silencing, not survivin. While targeting survivin could plausibly induce apoptosis, the observation is already explained by the effects of chemotherapy sensitization via MK silencing.
  – Conclusion: Not a missing piece.
 • For Abstract 2:
  – Cause established: The use of CRISPR/Cas9 delivered via EVs targeting IQGAP1/FOXM1 is a proven method to reverse sorafenib resistance by diminishing CSC traits.
  – Would we see this observation if the hypothesis was true: CRISPR/Cas9 editing is indeed an effective tool in HCC therapy. However, the hypothesis focuses on knocking out survivin to induce apoptosis and boost immunogenicity in combination with checkpoint blockade—a different target and mechanism than the one described.
  – Conclusion: Not a missing piece; alternative well‐explained pathways are presented.
 • For Abstract 3:
  – Cause established: The therapeutic suppression of HCC growth via Survivin siRNA demonstrates that interfering with the survivin pathway can suppress tumor growth.
  – Would we see this observation if the hypothesis was true: Targeting survivin via CRISPR/Cas9 might similarly reduce survivin levels to induce apoptosis. Nevertheless, the siRNA approach already achieves this effect, and the added benefits of CRISPR knockout—specifically boosting tumor immunogenicity and synergizing with immune checkpoint blockade—are not supported by these observations.
  – Conclusion: Not a missing piece; a similar outcome (decreased survivin function) is achieved, but through a different (and already validated) method.

3. Causal analysis (summary):
 Would we see some of the observations if the hypothesis was true: Yes, if survivin is indeed a critical anti-apoptotic factor, its knockout via CRISPR/Cas9 could lead to increased apoptosis in HCC cells. However, the observations already demonstrate that downregulation of survivin (via siRNA) or targeting alternative oncogenic pathways using CRISPR/Cas9 can suppress tumor growth. The hypothesis’s additional claim—boosting tumor immunogenicity and synergizing with immune checkpoint blockade—is not reflected in the abstracts. Therefore, while survivin targeting is validated, using CRISPR/Cas9 on survivin to enhance immunogenicity does not represent a novel or uniquely causal explanation over already established methods.

4. Disproof analysis:
 Does some observations disprove the hypothesis: No observation directly contradicts the possibility of survivin knockout inducing apoptosis. However, the data indicate that effective tumor suppression has already been achieved via established methods (siRNA delivery and CRISPR/Cas9 targeting other genes) without the need for the hypothesized mechanism of boosting immunogenicity with immune checkpoint blockade. This suggests that while the hypothesis is plausible, the offered causal chain is not uniquely required given the existing evidence.

5. Conclusion:
 The observations support targeted gene manipulation (using siRNA or CRISPR/Cas9) as effective strategies for HCC therapy but indicate that alternative, already validated mechanisms exist for inducing tumor cell death. The hypothesis’s approach of using CRISPR/Cas9 to target survivin and boost immunogenicity for checkpoint synergy does not appear as a novel causal explanation compared to the methods and targets already demonstrated.
