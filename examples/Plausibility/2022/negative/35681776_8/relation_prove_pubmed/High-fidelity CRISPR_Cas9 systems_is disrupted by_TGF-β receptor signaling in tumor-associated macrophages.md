I began by searching for literature specifically linking CRISPR/Cas9 systems with TGF-β receptor and tumor-associated macrophages, but found no direct hits. I then broadened the terms, searching for CRISPR/Cas9 in combination with TGF-β receptor and macrophages more generally. This yielded four relevant papers. Out of the results, PMID: 32393780 described the creation of CR3-deficient monocytes using CRISPR-Cas9 and tracked downstream effects on TGF-β receptor signaling, but not specifically in tumor-associated macrophages. Another relevant paper (PMID: 27780930) used CRISPR/Cas9 to create a dominant-negative TGF-β receptor II in prostate cancer cells, which are not macrophages.

The key evidence would be a sentence indicating that use of CRISPR/Cas9 directly disrupts TGF-β receptor signaling specifically in tumor-associated macrophages. I checked the full text of the most promising article (PMID: 32393780), since it used CRISPR/Cas9 in immune cell manipulation relating to TGF-β signaling, in order to confirm the details.

# literature evidence
## "Immune modulation by complement receptor 3-dependent human monocyte TGF-β1-transporting vesicles" (PMID: 32393780)
From the methods: "CR3-dependence is demonstrated using CR3-deficient (CD11b knockout) monocytes generated by CRISPR-CAS9 genome editing..." and from the results: "CR3 receptor subunit CD11b was knocked out in monocytic THP-1 cells using the clustered regularly interspersed short palindromic repeats (CRISPR)/CRISPR-associated protein 9 (CAS9) system... CD11b knockout (KO) THP-1 cells lost expression of CR3, as shown by flow cytometry and western blot analysis." However, the signaling examined was through the complement receptor CR3, not direct CRISPR/Cas9 editing of TGF-β receptors. The downstream readout did involve TGF-β vesicle signaling, but this occurs in monocytes/macrophages responding to Candida infection, and not in tumor-associated macrophages or direct CRISPR editing of the TGF-β receptor.

No sentence in this (or any other found article) indicates that CRISPR/Cas9 (high-fidelity or otherwise) has been used to disrupt TGF-β receptor signaling in tumor-associated macrophages.

Answer: False
