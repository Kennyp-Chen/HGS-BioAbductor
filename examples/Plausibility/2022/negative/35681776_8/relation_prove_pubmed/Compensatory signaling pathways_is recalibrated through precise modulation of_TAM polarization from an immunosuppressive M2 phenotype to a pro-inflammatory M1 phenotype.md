## "Tumor-associated macrophages remodel the suppressive tumor immune microenvironment and targeted therapy for immunotherapy." PMID: 40380196
"Therefore, compared with inhibition of macrophage recruitment or depletion, reprogramming of macrophages to adopt an 'immune-supportive' phenotype has emerged as an alternative strategy for antitumor therapy. ... TAMs in TME are in a constant state of transition under the regulation of different signaling pathways. Several cellular signaling pathways are also involved in the polarization of M0 macrophages. ... TAMs are dynamic tumor promoters and immune suppressors throughout the various stages of tumor initiation and advancement via the expression of cell surface receptors and regulatory factors … the complex role of TAMs is tightly linked to tumor progression and antitumor immune responses. Interestingly, the dual functions and remarkable plasticity of TAMs have provided ample motivation and opportunities for TAM-targeted strategies to fight cancer."
  
Further, explicit mention of compensatory mechanisms in the context of recalibrating TAMs:
"In mouse models of breast cancer, interruption of CCL2/CCR2 signaling leads to acceleration of tumor metastasis as a result of massive release of monocytes, indicating that more potential compensatory factors should be considered to achieve optimal and durable effects when exploring TAM-targeted strategies."  
"Inhibition of CSF-1R also promotes the reprogramming of TAMs towards the M1 phenotype. Since CSF-1/CSF-1R blockade may interfere with Tregs and DCs in the TME, the use of CSF1R inhibitors may also trigger compensatory effects, induce other pro-survival pathways and create limited outcomes."
