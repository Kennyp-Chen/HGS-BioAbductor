3. Causal analysis (summary):
Would we see some of the observations if the hypothesis was true: Lowering SAM/MTA production through MAT2A inhibition could impact metabolic and epigenetic programs that might influence both tumor cell proliferation and, indirectly, immune cell function. However, the observations already describe well‐established mechanisms—such as the role of the mTORC1–c-Myc–SIRT4 axis in methionine metabolism and the contribution of polyamine metabolism in shaping the immunosuppressive microenvironment. Thus, while MAT2A inhibition could plausibly affect these processes, the hypothesis does not introduce a novel causal explanation for the restoration of T cell effector function in the TME; existing literature highlights alternative pathways (e.g., polyamine metabolism or broader metabolic reprogramming) for these immune effects.

This means that while the hypothesis isn’t disproved by the observations, its causal significance for T cell effector function is not uniquely supported.

The hypothesis that inhibition of methionine adenosyltransferase (MAT2A) in HCC cells will reduce SAM/MTA production, thereby restoring T cell effector function and enhancing immunotherapy response, is plausible.
