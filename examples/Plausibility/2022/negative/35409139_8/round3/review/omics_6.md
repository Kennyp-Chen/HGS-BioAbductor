1. Hypothesis Summary  
The hypothesis proposes a dual-metabolic modulation strategy in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), wherein CRISPR/dCas9 is used to suppress MAT2A and microRNA modulation is applied to upregulate MAT1A. These genetic interventions are to be delivered specifically into liver tumor cells by liver-tropic nanoparticles. The intended outcome is to adjust the SAM (S-adenosylmethionine)/MTA (methylthioadenosine) ratio so as to “recalibrate” polyamine biosynthesis and restore proper epigenetic regulation. This metabolic reprogramming is expected to lower the expression of immune checkpoint molecules PD-1 and TIM-3 on tumor-associated T cells, thereby reversing T cell exhaustion. When used in combination with immune checkpoint blockade, the approach is aimed at potentiating anti-tumor immune responses in HCC.

Integrating this metabolic adjustment with changes in epigenetic regulation and subsequent T cell function (via PD-1 and TIM-3 modulation) distinguishes the approach from more conventional immunotherapeutic and metabolic interventions in HCC. Thus, this hypothesis offers an original perspective that innovatively links metabolic pathways to immune checkpoint regulation in the context of cancer.
