1. Hypothesis Summary:
──────────────────────────────
The hypothesis proposes a two-pronged metabolic reprogramming strategy for hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC). Specifically, it suggests:
• Using CRISPR/dCas9 to specifically suppress MAT2A expression while simultaneously upregulating MAT1A via microRNA modulation.
• Delivering these genetic modulators to liver tumor cells using liver-tropic nanoparticles to ensure specific targeting.
• This dual modulation is intended to recalibrate the production of S-adenosylmethionine (SAM) and methylthioadenosine (MTA) as well as polyamine biosynthesis, thereby optimizing the SAM/MTA ratio.
• The optimized metabolic state is expected to restore appropriate epigenetic regulation and lead to the downregulation of immune checkpoint molecules PD-1 and TIM-3 on tumor-associated T cells.
• In conjunction with immune checkpoint blockade therapies, this metabolic reprogramming is anticipated to reverse T cell exhaustion and enhance the anti-tumor immune response in HCC.
