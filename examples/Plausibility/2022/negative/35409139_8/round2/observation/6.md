1. Observation extraction:
 • Observation 1 (Abstract 1): In HCC cells, treatment with 5-Aza-CdR increased MAT1A expression, reversed MAT1A promoter hypermethylation, reduced MAT2A expression, and—contrary to what one might expect from suppressing a SAM-producing enzyme—induced an increase in SAM production. This treatment also inhibited cell growth and induced apoptosis.
 • Observation 2 (Abstract 2): In HCC models, increased levels of certain miRNAs (miR‐664, miR‐485‐3p, and miR‐495) lower MAT1A expression, promoting tumorigenesis. Their knockdown restores MAT1A, increases global CpG methylation, and reduces tumor growth, indicating that altered MAT expression is causally linked with tumor behavior.
 • Observation 3 (Abstract 3): HCC is characterized by decreased MAT1A and increased MAT2A/MAT2B. Transfection with miR-203, which targets MAT2A and MAT2B, lowers their levels. This molecular change is paralleled by an increase in SAM content, with associated inhibition of cell proliferation, migration, invasiveness, and reduction in stemness markers.

2. Causal analysis (individual):
 • For Observation 1:
  a. Its cause is partly established by epigenetic modulation (via demethylating agents) altering MAT gene expression.
  b. Would we see this observation if the hypothesis was true: The hypothesis posits that targeted suppression of MAT2A would reduce SAM (and MTA) production. However, Observation 1 shows that suppression of MAT2A (along with a relative increase in MAT1A) coincides with an increased production of SAM. This is not what would be expected if MAT2A suppression directly led to lower SAM. Thus, this is not a novel explanation but rather contradicts the predicted metabolic outcome.
  c. Explanation: Not a missing piece.
 
 • For Observation 2:
  a. Its cause (altered miRNA regulation impacting MAT1A levels) is well established.
  b. Would we see this observation if the hypothesis was true: The hypothesis specifically focuses on MAT2A suppression leading to metabolic reprogramming. Observation 2 deals with miRNA effects on MAT1A rather than a direct intervention on MAT2A. There is no direct evidence here connecting MAT2A suppression with changes in SAM/MTA or immune checkpoint regulation.
  c. Explanation: Not a missing piece.
 
 • For Observation 3:
  a. Its cause is partly established through miR-203 mediated suppression of MAT2A and MAT2B, which correlates with increased SAM levels.
  b. Would we see this observation if the hypothesis was true: If targeted suppression of MAT2A reduced SAM production (as the hypothesis predicts), then Observation 3 would have shown a decrease in SAM rather than the observed increase. Thus, the observed metabolic profile contradicts the hypothesized outcome.
  c. Explanation: Not a missing piece.

3. Causal analysis (summary):
 Would we see some of the observations if the hypothesis was true: The hypothesis suggests that suppressing MAT2A would lead to lower SAM and MTA production, reduced polyamine biosynthesis, and subsequently affect immune checkpoint marker expression on tumor-associated T cells. However, both Observation 1 and Observation 3 indicate that suppression of MAT2A (or combined with altered MAT1A/MAT2B levels) is associated with an increase in SAM content rather than a reduction. Moreover, none of the abstracts address changes in immune checkpoint markers or T cell exhaustion. Therefore, the hypothesis does not offer a novel explanation for the metabolic changes observed and it omits key established aspects of the MAT enzyme regulation in HCC.

4. Disproof analysis:
 Does some observations disprove the hypothesis: Yes. Observations 1 and 3 conflict with the hypothesis by demonstrating that reduction of MAT2A (and in one case concomitant with increased MAT1A) results in significantly increased SAM production. This directly contradicts the hypothesis’s prediction that MAT2A suppression would reduce the levels of SAM and MTA, and subsequently influence immune checkpoint markers.

5. Conclusion:
 hypothesis: disproved
