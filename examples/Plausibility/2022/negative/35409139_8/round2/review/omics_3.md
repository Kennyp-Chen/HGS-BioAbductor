1. Hypothesis Summary  
──────────────────────────────
The hypothesis proposes a dual therapeutic strategy in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC). It posits that by precisely inhibiting methionine adenosyltransferase (MAT), the production of S-adenosylmethionine (SAM) and methylthioadenosine (MTA) is reduced. This reduction is believed to interfere with the epigenetic upregulation of immune checkpoint molecules, which play a role in driving T cell exhaustion. The hypothesis further suggests that concurrently blocking the CXCL11/CXCR3 chemokine axis using either targeted biologics or small molecules will inhibit the recruitment of T cell subsets that support cancer stem cell (CSC) renewal. The combination of these two interventions is expected to restore robust anti-tumor immunity, reduce CSC maintenance, and thereby slow tumor progression. To validate these connections, the approach relies on state-of-the-art techniques including CRISPR-Cas9 gene editing, single-cell transcriptomics, and computational pathway modeling.

In summary, the hypothesis shows originality by bridging metabolism, epigenetics, and immune modulation in a combined therapeutic context for HCC.
