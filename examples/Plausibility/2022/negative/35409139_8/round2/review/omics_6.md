 • The overall goal is to overcome T cell exhaustion, thereby restoring effective anti-tumor immunity; when combined with conventional immune checkpoint blockade therapies, this strategy is anticipated to enhance clinical outcomes in HCC patients.

2. Originality Assessment  
 • The integration of metabolic reprogramming, epigenetic modulation, and immune checkpoint regulation in a single therapeutic strategy is innovative.  
 • Employing CRISPR/dCas9 for transcriptional suppression (instead of permanent gene disruption) is a distinct approach that aligns well with current trends in precision epigenetic regulation.  
 • The use of liver-tropic nanoparticles for targeted delivery further differentiates the hypothesis from traditional systemic approaches, although nanodelivery systems are an evolving area with emerging examples in literature.  
 • Overall, the hypothesis offers a fresh, interdisciplinary perspective by linking metabolic enzyme suppression with immune modulation, a combination that is not widely explored in existing studies.

 • The strength of the hypothesis hinges on several critical assumptions:

  – That these metabolic changes will lead to a significant decrease in the epigenetic upregulation of PD-1 and TIM-3 on T cells, an area where the causal relationship remains to be firmly established.  
  – That the targeted suppression in tumor cells (versus systemic effects) will be sufficiently selective to avoid off-target consequences on normal liver metabolism.
