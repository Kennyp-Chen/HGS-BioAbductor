1. Hypothesis Summary  
─────────────────────────────  
The hypothesis posits a dual-targeted therapeutic strategy for hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC). It is built on two principal components:  
• First, it claims that the specific inhibition of methionine adenosyltransferase will reduce the production of S-adenosylmethionine (SAM) and methylthioadenosine (MTA). This reduction is expected to interfere with epigenetic mechanisms that upregulate immune checkpoint molecules, which in turn drive T cell exhaustion.  
• Second, the hypothesis suggests that blocking the CXCL11/CXCR3 chemokine-receptor axis—using either targeted biologics or small molecules—will prevent the recruitment of T cell subsets that are implicated in cancer stem cell (CSC) renewal.  
The integration of advanced methodologies (CRISPR-Cas9 gene editing, single-cell transcriptomics, and computational pathway modeling) is proposed as a means to validate the mechanistic interplay of metabolic and immune pathways in restoring anti-tumor immunity, disrupting CSC maintenance, and ultimately reducing tumor progression in HCC.
