1. Hypothesis Summary  
─────────────────────────────  
The hypothesis posits that a dual-modality intervention in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) can restore T cell functionality by:  
• Simultaneously inhibiting metabolic pathways that generate key immunosuppressive metabolites (kynurenine, SAM, and MTA)  
• Disrupting exosomal noncoding RNA (ncRNA) signaling that contributes to the immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment (TME)  
This integrative strategy is expected to reprogram the TME—intervening along the cascade from chronic liver inflammation and stromal activation (with cytokine release) to T cell exhaustion (via PD-1/PD-L1 and CTLA-4 upregulation). Cutting-edge techniques, such as CRISPR-Cas9 screens, single-cell RNA sequencing, and metabolomic profiling, are proposed to interrogate and guide this intervention. In combination with PD-1/PD-L1 checkpoint blockade, the hypothesis anticipates a synergistic effect that potentiates antitumor immunity.

• Integrative targeting of both immunometabolism and exosomal ncRNA signaling in HCC is a comparatively novel approach, as the majority of studies tend to focus on one axis (either metabolic reprogramming or intercellular signaling).

While related studies have individually explored immunosuppressive metabolism or ncRNA regulation in the TME, the simultaneous targeting of these pathways alongside immune checkpoint inhibition offers a fresh perspective that could potentially overcome multifactorial immunosuppression in HCC.
