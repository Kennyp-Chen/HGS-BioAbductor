1. Hypothesis Summary

The hypothesis posits that alcohol consumption triggers epigenetic alterations—specifically the deregulation of gut epithelial microRNAs—which subsequently disrupt the integrity of tight junctions. This disruption increases gut permeability and facilitates the translocation of endotoxins into the circulation. The resulting endotoxemia drives liver inflammation and activates hepatic stellate cells. Furthermore, when this process is compounded by metabolic stress and underlying genetic predispositions, it ultimately leads to fibrosis and the development of hepatocellular carcinoma (hepatocarcinogenesis) in the context of alcohol-associated liver disease.
