1. Observation extraction:
 • Multiple studies confirm that genetic factors, in particular the PNPLA3 risk allele, strongly influence susceptibility to alcohol‐associated liver disease (ALD), alcoholic cirrhosis, and hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC).
 • Alcohol consumption leads to altered ethanol metabolism, resulting in hepatic lipid turnover disturbances, reactive oxygen species (ROS) production, and inflammatory signaling.
 • Alcohol intake changes the intestinal microbiota, including reducing butyrate‐producing bacteria; this gut dysbiosis is linked to increased intestinal permeability, LPS translocation, and consequent liver inflammation.
 • Only a minority of heavy drinkers develop clinical ALD, suggesting that host factors (genetic and environmental, such as microbiota composition) modulate disease risk.
 • Inflammatory responses (involving macrophage polarization and immune mediators) are central to ALD progression, and therapeutic strategies are exploring modulation of these immune mechanisms.
