1. Observation extraction:
• Alcohol consumption leads to gut dysbiosis, intestinal barrier disruption, and release of microbial toxins (such as LPS) that contribute to liver inflammation, fibrosis, and progression to advanced liver disease (cirrhosis and HCC).  
• Alcohol-associated liver disease (ALD) and nonalcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) are linked to abnormal gut microbiota, with changes in microbial metabolites (e.g., bile acids, short-chain fatty acids) and increased endotoxin translocation.  
• Activation of resident liver immune cells (including Kupffer cells) by gut-derived endotoxins via pattern-recognition receptors (e.g., TLR4) triggers inflammatory cascades (involving cytokines such as IL-6) and signaling pathways (including STAT3) that drive liver inflammation and carcinogenesis.  
• Therapeutic interventions targeting the gut microbiota and barrier (fecal transplantation, probiotics, etc.) have been proposed and are under investigation to mitigate liver injury.
