## "Metabolic endotoxemia and cardiovascular disease: A systematic review about potential roles of prebiotics and probiotics." (PMID: 31894861)
"It has been revealed by some studies that alterations in the intestinal microbiota (dysbiosis) lead to increased intestinal permeability and translocation of LPS to the blood circulation."

## "The Herbal Medicine Scutellaria-Coptis Alleviates Intestinal Mucosal Barrier Damage in Diabetic Rats by Inhibiting Inflammation and Modulating the Gut Microbiota" (PMID: 33224253)
"SC had a strong inhibitory effect on some potential enteropathogenic bacteria and LPS-producing bacteria... and could also promote the proliferation of butyrate-producing bacteria, such as Lachnospiraceae and Prevotellaceae...the hypoglycaemic effects of SC were related to the protection of the intestinal mucosal barrier, and the mechanisms might be related to the inhibition of intestinal inflammation and the regulation of the gut microbiota."

## "Parkinson's Disease and Gut Microbiota." (PMID: 34500451)
"Both dysbiosis should increase the intestinal permeability, which subsequently facilitates exposure of the intestinal neural plexus to toxins like lipopolysaccharide and pesticide, which should lead to abnormal aggregation of alpha-synuclein fibrils."

## "Niacin and Butyrate: Nutraceuticals Targeting Dysbiosis and Intestinal Permeability in Parkinson's Disease" (PMID: 33374784)
"Changes in the microbiome with reductions in short-chain fatty acid (SCFA)-producing bacteria and increases in endotoxin-producing bacteria likely contribute to the pathogenesis of PD. ...In inflammatory states, butyrate acts via GPR109A to increase concentrations of tight junction proteins and improve intestinal permeability."

## "Testing Epithelial Permeability in Fetal Tissue-Derived Enteroids." (PMID: 35781541)
"As gram-negative dominant bacterial dysbiosis is a known risk factor for intestinal injury, we used lipopolysaccharide (LPS), an endotoxin produced by gram-negative bacteria, to induce permeability in the enteroids. ...The experiment showed that apical exposure to LPS induces epithelial permeability in a concentration-dependent manner. These findings support the hypothesis that gram-negative dominant dysbiosis contributes to the mechanism of intestinal injury in preterm infants."
