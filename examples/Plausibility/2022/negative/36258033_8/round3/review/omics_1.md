1. Hypothesis Summary  
The hypothesis posits that amongst heavy alcohol consumers who carry the PNPLA3 risk allele, a specific gut microbial alteration occurs—marked by a decline in butyrate-producing bacteria coupled with a rise in lipopolysaccharide (LPS)-producing taxa. This dysbiosis weakens the integrity of the intestinal barrier, leading to excessive endotoxin (LPS) translocation into the portal circulation. The increased endotoxin load is then proposed to activate liver-resident Kupffer cells, triggering an IL-6/STAT3-mediated inflammatory cascade that drives the transition from cirrhosis to hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC). Additionally, the hypothesis suggests that state-of-the-art, multi-dimensional multi-omics technologies can validate these microbial/metabolic alterations and help tailor personalized microbiome-based interventions (e.g., precision probiotics, prebiotics, or fecal microbiota transplantation) to intercept the pathogenic progression toward HCC.
