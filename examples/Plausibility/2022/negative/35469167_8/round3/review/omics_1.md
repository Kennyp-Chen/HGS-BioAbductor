1. Hypothesis Summary  
──────────────────────────────
The hypothesis posits that in patients with NAFLD and obesity, enhanced de novo lipogenesis leads to an overproduction of saturated fatty acids that then modulate key ceramide biosynthetic enzymes (such as SphK2, CERT, and CERS2). This enzymatic modulation purportedly drives ceramide metabolism to favor an accumulation of C16-ceramide while reducing polyunsaturated ceramide species. The resultant altered ceramide profile is hypothesized to interfere with the p53-mediated apoptosis pathway, activate NF-κB signaling, and disturb bile acid receptor functions (notably FXR and TGR5). Together, these changes are believed to create a chronically inflamed and immune-suppressive hepatic microenvironment that predisposes patients toward the initiation and progression of hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC). The strategy for testing this hypothesis involves leveraging advanced lipidomics and transcriptomics to characterize these molecular shifts and employing CRISPR-based gene editing to selectively manipulate the enzymes involved in the ceramide pathway, aiming to restore a balanced ceramide profile that reinvigorates apoptotic regulation and tumor immune surveillance as a means toward precision therapy against HCC.
