The altered ceramide balance is proposed to disrupt the cellular apoptotic machinery by impairing p53-mediated apoptosis, while also activating NF-κB signaling and perturbing the function of bile acid receptors (notably FXR and TGR5). Collectively, these events are believed to create a chronic inflammatory and immunosuppressive hepatic microenvironment that predisposes patients to hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) initiation and progression. The authors suggest employing advanced lipidomics and transcriptomics analyses, combined with CRISPR-based gene editing to selectively modulate the implicated enzymes, as a means to test whether rebalancing ceramide profiles can restore proper apoptotic regulation and improve tumor immune surveillance—proposing a novel precision therapeutic strategy against HCC.
