1. Hypothesis Summary:
• The hypothesis proposes that in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), targeted upregulation of DGAT2 activity—using either contemporary CRISPR activation systems or pharmacological inducers—can lead to enhanced triglyceride synthesis.  
• This increased triglyceride production fosters the formation of lipid droplets, which are postulated to sequester lipotoxic free fatty acids (FFAs), thereby mitigating direct lipotoxicity.  
• The reduction in lipotoxicity is further hypothesized to improve mitochondrial function and modulate the ESRRA–PROX1 transcriptional network.  
• These alterations are suggested to attenuate oncogenic signaling pathways, such as those associated with endoplasmic reticulum (ER) stress and inflammation.  
• The hypothesis also advocates employing advanced technologies (e.g., single-cell transcriptomics and lipidomic profiling) to track and validate the resultant metabolic shifts and, ultimately, the therapeutic potential in reducing tumor aggressiveness and improving patient outcomes.
