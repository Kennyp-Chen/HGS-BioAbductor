1. Hypothesis Summary

Your hypothesis proposes that in individuals with NAFLD and obesity, increased de novo lipogenesis drives the accumulation of saturated fatty acids. This results in a specific shift in ceramide metabolism—namely, an elevation in C16-ceramide levels alongside a reduction in polyunsaturated ceramide species. The altered lipid profile is proposed to disrupt p53-mediated apoptosis and simultaneously activate NF-κB signaling. In parallel, abnormal bile acid signaling further boosts inflammatory cytokine production. The combined effect of these events is suggested to cultivate an immune-suppressive and pro-tumorigenic hepatic microenvironment that is critical for the initiation and progression of hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC). To address this cascade, your proposal emphasizes the use of advanced lipidomics, transcriptomics, and CRISPR-based gene editing to (a) quantify and characterize ceramide species, (b) map downstream signaling pathways, and (c) modulate key enzymes of ceramide biosynthesis. Ultimately, you hypothesize that restoring a balanced ceramide profile could reinstate proper apoptotic responses and enhance tumor immune surveillance, representing a promising therapeutic strategy against HCC.
