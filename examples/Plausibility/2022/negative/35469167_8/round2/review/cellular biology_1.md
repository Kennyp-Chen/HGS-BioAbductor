1. Hypothesis Summary  
─────────────────────────────  
The hypothesis posits that in the context of non‐alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) and obesity, increased de novo lipogenesis results in an overaccumulation of saturated fatty acids. This biochemical shift alters ceramide metabolism, promoting an increase in C16-ceramide and a decrease in polyunsaturated ceramide species. The resulting imbalance is proposed to interfere with p53-mediated apoptosis and simultaneously activate NF-κB signaling. In parallel, aberrant bile acid signaling is suggested to boost inflammatory cytokine production. The combined effect of these molecular dysfunctions is hypothesized to establish an immune-suppressive and pro-tumorigenic hepatic microenvironment, thereby facilitating the initiation and progression of hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC). The hypothesis also suggests that interventions—using advanced lipidomics for precise ceramide profiling, transcriptomics for pathway elucidation, and CRISPR-based gene editing to adjust key enzymes in ceramide biosynthesis—could restore a balanced ceramide profile, re-establish apoptotic mechanisms, and improve tumor immune surveillance as a potential therapeutic strategy against HCC.
