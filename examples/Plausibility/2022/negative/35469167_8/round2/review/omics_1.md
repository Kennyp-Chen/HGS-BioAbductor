1. Hypothesis Summary

The hypothesis posits that in NAFLD and obesity, an increase in de novo lipogenesis causes a buildup of saturated fatty acids. This buildup skews ceramide metabolism to favor higher levels of C16-ceramide over polyunsaturated ceramide species. The resulting lipid imbalance is proposed to interfere with p53-mediated apoptosis and simultaneously activate NF-κB signaling while altered bile acid signaling further boosts inflammatory cytokine production. Together, these events create an immune-suppressive and pro-tumorigenic hepatic microenvironment, thereby driving the initiation and progression of hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC). The hypothesis additionally suggests that correcting the aberrant ceramide profile—using advanced lipidomics, transcriptomics, and CRISPR-based gene editing to modulate ceramide biosynthetic enzymes—might restore apoptotic mechanisms and enhance tumor immune surveillance, thus offering a novel therapeutic strategy against HCC.
