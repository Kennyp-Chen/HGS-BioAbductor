1. Hypothesis Summary:
The hypothesis posits that in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), a dual therapeutic approach combining FASN (fatty acid synthase) inhibitors with PPARα (peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor alpha) agonists can synergistically disrupt tumor lipid metabolism. The strategy relies on a temporally and quantitatively optimized regimen, guided by dynamic metabolic profiling methods (e.g., single-cell metabolomics, metabolic flux analysis, CRISPR-based screening). The intended outcome is to deplete lipid reserves, force tumors to rely excessively on fatty acid oxidation, and elevate reactive oxygen species (ROS) levels to an extent that causes metabolic overload and triggers apoptosis. Additionally, the approach aims to target cancer stem cell populations that depend on altered lipid metabolism, thereby reducing tumor growth and recurrence.

2. Originality Assessment:
The hypothesis integrates two distinct modulation strategies: inhibiting lipid synthesis (via FASN inhibition) and promoting lipid utilization (via PPARα activation). While both FASN inhibitors and PPARα agonists have been individually studied in cancer contexts, combining them for a synergistic effect is a less common approach. The emphasis on dynamic metabolic profiling to optimize timing and dosage further distinguishes this hypothesis from more static treatment regimens reported in the literature. Moreover, the proposal to use cutting-edge tools like single-cell metabolomics and CRISPR-based screening to elucidate compensatory metabolic pathways lends an innovative, multi-dimensional perspective to the therapeutic strategy. Thus, the hypothesis exhibits originality by proposing a combinatorial intervention with a sophisticated monitoring framework.

However, the counteracting nature of these interventions (one reducing lipid synthesis, the other increasing oxidation) requires precise calibration, which the hypothesis acknowledges. The predicted synergy is contingent upon achieving a delicate balance in dosing and timing—a factor that introduces considerable complexity.
