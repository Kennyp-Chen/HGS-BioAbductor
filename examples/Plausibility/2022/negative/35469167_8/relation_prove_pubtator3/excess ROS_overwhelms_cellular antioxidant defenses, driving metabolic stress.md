# Search Strategy and Reasoning
The minimal keywords representing the entities in the relationship are "ROS" (reactive oxygen species) and "cellular antioxidant defenses" or "metabolic stress." I performed two primary searches:
1. "ROS cellular antioxidant defenses" for abstracts discussing how ROS interacts with or impacts cellular antioxidant defenses.
2. "reactive oxygen species metabolic stress" for abstracts connecting ROS and metabolic stress, which may relate to their effects on antioxidant defenses.

The goal was to identify explicit literature linking excess ROS overwhelming (surpassing the capability of) cellular antioxidant defenses, thus causing or driving metabolic stress.

# literature evidence

## "Disorders of Endogenous and Exogenous Antioxidants in Neurological Diseases" (PMID: 37891890)
"oxidative stress load commonly surpasses endogenous antioxidative capacity. While oxidative processes have been robustly implicated in the pathogenesis of these diseases..."

## "Regulation of Reactive Oxygen Species and Antioxidant Defense in Plants under Salinity" (PMID: 34502233)
"At higher concentrations, those ROS cause oxidative stress. Salinity-induced osmotic stress and ionic stress trigger the overproduction of ROS and, ultimately, result in oxidative damage to cell organelles and membrane components, and at severe levels, they cause cell and plant death. The antioxidant defense system protects the plant from salt-induced oxidative damage by detoxifying the ROS and also by maintaining the balance of ROS generation under salt stress."

## "Drought-Induced Oxidative Stress in Pearl Millet (Cenchrus americanus L.) at Seedling Stage: Survival Mechanisms through Alteration of Morphophysiological and Antioxidants Activity" (PMID: 36013352)
"After 7, 14 and 21 days of drought stress, activities of all the antioxidant enzymes viz., catalase (CAT), guaiacol peroxidase (GPX), superoxide dismutase (SOD) and glutathione reductase (GR) were inhibited, clearly indicating a loss of antioxidant defense machinery. Likewise, the levels of ascorbate (AsA) and reduced glutathione (GSH) levels declined significantly (p <= 0.01)."

These abstracts repeatedly cite the phenomenon of oxidative stress (excess ROS) overwhelming endogenous (cellular) antioxidant defenses, which is closely linked to the onset of metabolic dysfunction or stress in both plants and animals.

Answer: True
