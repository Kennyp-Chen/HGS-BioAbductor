1. Hypothesis Summary  
─────────────────────────────  
The hypothesis posits that increasing the activity of DGAT2 (diacylglycerol acyltransferase 2) in hepatocellular carcinoma cells will lead to enhanced formation of lipid droplets. These lipid droplets are proposed to act as protective buffers that sequester lipotoxic free fatty acids. By doing so, the accumulation of harmful free fatty acids would be reduced, leading to diminished oncogenic signaling and decreased tumor aggressiveness, with the eventual goal of improving patient outcomes.

However, focusing specifically on DGAT2’s role in hepatocellular carcinoma and framing it as a therapeutic target that both sequesters toxic fatty acids and directly inhibits oncogenic signaling adds a novel twist. While parts of the mechanism echo existing understanding (i.e., DGAT2’s role in catalyzing triglyceride synthesis), applying this concept to directly counteract oncogenic progression in liver cancer is innovative.
