1. Hypothesis Summary:
 The hypothesis proposes a sequential and multi-modal therapeutic approach designed to transform “cold” tumors into “hot” tumors. It suggests that a carefully timed regimen starting with low-dose, fractionated radiotherapy—enhanced by nanoparticles to boost immunogenic cell death—and supported by real-time immune profiling, can set the stage for PD-1 inhibitor therapy. This combination is hypothesized to preferentially expand precursor exhausted CD8⁺ T cells within the systemic immune environment (STIE), facilitate their recruitment into the tumor microenvironment (TIME), and, upon administration of PD-1 blockade during a precise therapeutic window, enable these cells to differentiate into highly active cytotoxic T cells, thereby enhancing antitumor responses.

2. Originality Assessment:
 The concept integrates several existing elements—radiotherapy, nanotechnology, immune profiling, and PD-1 inhibition—which are well-studied individually in cancer immunotherapy. However, the novelty here lies in the precise timing and sequencing to achieve a synergistic effect: specifically, the integration of nanoparticle-enhanced immunogenic cell death and real-time immune monitoring to optimize the induction of precursor exhausted T cells and their subsequent functional conversion. This multi-pronged, temporally optimized strategy offers an innovative perspective on combination immunotherapy and represents a distinctive approach compared to many conventional combination regimens where timing and immune profiling are less emphasized.
