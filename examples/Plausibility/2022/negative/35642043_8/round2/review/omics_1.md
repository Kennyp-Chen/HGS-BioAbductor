The first step involves the transient and localized disruption of tight junctions within the tumor microenvironment (TME) by using nanocarriers loaded with agents that temporarily disassemble these junctions. This process is guided by real-time imaging, ensuring precision and safety. The purpose of disrupting tight junction integrity is to increase the surface exposure of Claudin18.2 epitopes on tumor cells. In the second step, after exposing these epitopes, therapies that specifically target Claudin18.2—such as ADCs (antibody-drug conjugates), bispecific antibodies, and CAR-T cells—are administered. The expected outcome is improved binding and internalization of these therapeutic agents, thereby increasing their antitumor efficacy.
