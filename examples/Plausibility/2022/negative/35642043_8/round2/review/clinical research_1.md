1. Hypothesis Summary:
The hypothesis proposes that a targeted, controlled approach using nanocarriers loaded with agents that transiently and reversibly disrupt tight junctions—specifically in the tumor microenvironment (TME)—can enhance the surface exposure of Claudin18.2 epitopes on tumor cells. The strategy is aided by real-time imaging to precisely localize and time the administration. This enhanced exposure is hypothesized to improve the binding and internalization of Claudin18.2-targeted therapies (such as ADCs, bispecific antibodies, and CAR-T cells), ultimately leading to improved antitumor responses. The approach leverages and refines the natural tendency of TME stresses to alter tight junction organization, adding a technological layer of control to optimize treatment efficacy.
