1. Hypothesis Summary

The hypothesis proposes that in solid tumors with an inflammatory microenvironment, increased levels of cytokines (specifically IL‑6 and TNF‑α) activate post-translational modification (PTM) enzymes—including glycosyltransferases and kinases. These enzymes then specifically modify the tight junction protein CLDN18.2 through glycosylation and phosphorylation. The modified CLDN18.2 experiences altered surface topology which unveils new binding epitopes. The hypothesis further suggests that by utilizing advanced technologies (e.g., machine learning-assisted proteomic profiling, CRISPR-based functional screens, and high-resolution imaging), it is possible to identify and characterize these modifications. Finally, by modulating the PTM status of CLDN18.2 pharmacologically, one could enhance its surface presentation, thereby improving the efficacy of CLDN18.2-directed immunotherapies (like monoclonal antibodies and CAR-T cells) to achieve more effective anti-tumor responses.
