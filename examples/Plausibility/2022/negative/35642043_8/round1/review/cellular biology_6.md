1. Hypothesis Summary:
The hypothesis posits that the cancer-specific expression and therapeutic response of CLDN18.2—a tight junction protein highly expressed in some solid tumors—is governed by both epigenetic regulatory mechanisms and factors within the tumor microenvironment. It further suggests that simultaneously targeting CLDN18.2 along with these upstream regulatory mechanisms (epigenetic regulators and microenvironmental factors) could overcome resistance mechanisms and produce synergistic anti-tumor effects.

While epigenetic regulation and TME dynamics have been individually implicated in drug resistance and cancer progression, combining these elements with CLDN18.2 targeting represents a more integrated, multi-dimensional therapeutic strategy. The idea of a dual (or multi-target) approach is not entirely novel; however, its specific application to modulating CLDN18.2 expression and therapy response appears to be a creative and innovative perspective that distinguishes it from more conventional single-target treatments.
