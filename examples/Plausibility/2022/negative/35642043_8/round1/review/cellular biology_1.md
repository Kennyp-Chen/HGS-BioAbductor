1. Hypothesis Summary  
The hypothesis posits that a transient, controlled pharmacologic modulation of the tumor microenvironment can disrupt tight junction integrity. This disruption is expected to increase the surface exposure of the cell adhesion protein Claudin18.2. The aumento in exposure is theorized to enhance the binding and efficacy of Claudin18.2‐targeted therapeutics—including antibody–drug conjugates (ADCs), bispecific antibodies, and CAR‐T cells—which in turn should lead to improved antitumor responses and better clinical outcomes in cancers that express Claudin18.2.

The concept of targeting Claudin18.2 is not entirely new, as it has been recognized as a promising target in various cancers. However, the proposed method of pharmacologically disrupting tight junctions in the tumor microenvironment to transiently boost surface antigen exposure represents an innovative twist. Traditionally, research has focused on directly targeting overexpressed antigens rather than manipulating the tumor’s barrier properties to improve target accessibility. This approach may distinguish itself from current strategies by offering a dual mechanism: both improving antigen accessibility and potentially sensitizing tumor cells to the subsequent immunologic or cytotoxic intervention. Thus, while both the target (Claudin18.2) and the subsequent immune therapies are established in research, the preconditioning strategy to modify the tumor microenvironment constitutes a novel perspective that could enrich the existing body of work.

  - The specificity of the pharmacologic modulation: ensuring that the tight junction disruption is localized, transient, and does not adversely affect normal tissues.

   - Assess the risk of systemic leakage or adverse effects stemming from tight junction disruption in clinical settings.
