The hypothesis posits that RMDN1 functions as a context-dependent epigenetic scaffold in liver neoplasms. Specifically, when RMDN1 is upregulated, it recruits histone deacetylases (HDACs) along with chromatin modifiers NSD1 and KDM3A—thereby establishing a repressive chromatin state that limits oncogenic transcription and suppresses tumor progression. Conversely, when RMDN1 is downregulated, its failure to recruit these repressive complexes permits chromatin activators to bind, activating pro-metastatic and angiogenic pathways. The proposal includes employing advanced technologies (CRISPR-mediated epigenetic editing, single-cell ATAC-seq with spatial transcriptomics, and quantitative proteomics) to dissect these mechanisms and validate the dualistic function of RMDN1 in liver cancer.
