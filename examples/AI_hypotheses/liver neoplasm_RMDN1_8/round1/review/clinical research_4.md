1. Hypothesis Summary

The hypothesis posits that in liver neoplasm, RMDN1 plays a crucial regulatory role by modulating chromatin states. Specifically: 

• When RMDN1 expression is increased, it interacts with and recruits chromatin repressors such as HDAC1.  
• This recruitment counterbalances chromatin activators (e.g., EP300), leading to reduced chromatin accessibility and lower transcription of genes involved in metastasis and angiogenesis.  
• Conversely, when RMDN1 expression is reduced, there is less efficient recruitment of chromatin repressors, allowing EP300 to enhance chromatin binding and thereby potentially promoting an aggressive cancer phenotype.

The central idea is that RMDN1’s regulation of chromatin state via interactions with HDAC1 (and possibly related factors) is a critical mechanism in modulating liver tumor progression.
