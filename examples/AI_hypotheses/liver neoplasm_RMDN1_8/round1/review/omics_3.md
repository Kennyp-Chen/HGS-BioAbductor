1. Hypothesis Summary

The hypothesis proposes that high levels of RMDN1 suppress a functional module responsible for positively regulating chromatin binding. This suppression is thought to occur through the modulation of chromatin-modifying enzymes—specifically, by decreasing the acetylation activity of histone acetyltransferases, which then reduces the binding of chromatin activators. Furthermore, the hypothesis posits that RMDN1’s protective role is strengthened via its interaction with tumor-suppressive genes, such as TP53 and histone deacetylases, ultimately maintaining a chromatin landscape less conducive to oncogenic transcription. The overall effect of these interactions is hypothesized to lead to reduced metastatic and angiogenic processes, thereby improving the prognosis in liver neoplasm.

• Novel Element: The hypothesis centers on the role of RMDN1—a gene not traditionally associated with chromatin regulation—in modulating histone acetyltransferase activity and influencing chromatin states. This perspective, linking RMDN1 to a coordinated network of histone modifiers and tumor-suppressive elements (like TP53), introduces a novel mechanism of tumor suppression in liver neoplasm.

• Comparative Novelty: While the roles of histone acetyltransferases, deacetylases, and TP53 in cancer have been extensively studied, the specific interplay where RMDN1 elevates and indirectly suppresses chromatin activator binding by modulating these enzymes creates an innovative angle.
