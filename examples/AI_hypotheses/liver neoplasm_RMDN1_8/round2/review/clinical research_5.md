1. Hypothesis Summary

• Main Idea:  
The hypothesis posits that RMDN1 functions as a context-dependent tumor suppressor in liver cancer by altering chromatin architecture. When upregulated, RMDN1 is proposed to interact with chromatin repressors (e.g., histone deacetylases) to promote chromatin compaction at oncogene loci, thereby reducing the transcriptional activation of those oncogenes that are key drivers of metastasis and angiogenesis.

• Key Components:  
– RMDN1 upregulation leads to its direct interaction with chromatin repressors resulting in deacetylation and chromatin compaction.  
– This compaction specifically targets oncogene loci, suppressing their activation by reducing chromatin accessibility.  
– There is a proposed “epigenetic switch” governed by the balance between RMDN1 and other epigenetic regulators (NSD1 and JMJD2D) that critically modulates liver cancer progression.  
– The hypothesis envisions employing advanced techniques (CRISPR perturbation, ChIP-seq, quantitative proteomics) to elucidate the direct interactome of RMDN1, changes in chromatin state, and the consequences on oncogenic transcription programs.

– Utilizing state-of-the-art techniques to map RMDN1’s interactome and correlate it with functional chromatin changes further underscores the innovative thrust of the proposed research.

– While roles for chromatin modifiers in oncogenesis aren’t novel, attributing a context-dependent, tumor-suppressive function to RMDN1 specifically in liver neoplasms is a divergent and potentially distinctive perspective within the field.  
– The hypothesis stands out by not only focusing on a singular epigenetic factor but by proposing a dynamic network (or “switch”) among multiple regulators, which, if verified, would add significant depth to current models of epigenetic tumor suppression.
