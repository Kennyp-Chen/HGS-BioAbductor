1. Hypothesis Summary

The hypothesis centers on the idea that CIAO2A, along with a partner gene (NER1), jointly regulates the nuclear export of mRNA. It posits that when both genes are upregulated, the efficiency of mRNA export increases, thereby enhancing synthesis of proteins that fortify cell adhesion and inhibit metastasis. In contrast, downregulation of one or both genes is thought to impede proper mRNA export, causing an accumulation of improperly processed transcripts that then activate pro‐tumorigenic pathways, such as angiogenesis and metastatic behavior. Ultimately, the hypothesis suggests that this cooperative interaction could serve as a novel regulatory mechanism for liver neoplasm progression, with potential implications for targeted therapeutic interventions.
