1. Observation extraction:
 • Abstract 1: Liver cancer progression is linked to alterations in dynamic RNA modifications that facilitate the selective translation of oncogenic transcripts, driving tumor initiation, metastasis, metabolism changes, microenvironment alterations, and resistance to therapy.
 • Abstract 2: In hepatocellular carcinoma, upregulation of eIF5A2 (a translation initiation factor) is associated with enhanced tumor aggressiveness, metastasis, and adverse prognosis through its role in promoting epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT).
 • Abstract 3: Dysregulated long non‐coding RNAs (lncRNAs) in liver cancer influence cell cycle progression, EMT, invasion, metastasis, and treatment resistance, acting through diverse downstream signaling pathways.

2. Causal analysis (individual):
 • On RNA modifications (Abstract 1):
  – Cause already established? Yes. Altered RNA modifications are an established mechanism that promotes the translation of oncogenic mRNAs.
  – Would we see this observation if the hypothesis was true: Even if CIAO2A upregulation improves mRNA export fidelity, the aberrant RNA modification patterns leading to selective oncogenic mRNA translation would still occur through known epitranscriptomic mechanisms.
  – Explanation novelty: This does not offer a new explanation for the observed RNA modification-mediated oncogenesis; it remains “not a missing piece.”
 
 • On eIF5A2 upregulation (Abstract 2):
  – Cause already established? Yes. The increased expression of eIF5A2 directly promotes tumor aggressiveness and induces EMT, contributing to metastasis.
  – Would we see this observation if the hypothesis was true: If CIAO2A’s function were protective by ensuring high-fidelity mRNA export, we might expect less aberrant expression of oncogenic factors. However, the established role of eIF5A2 in translation initiation and EMT remains independent of nuclear export regulation.
  – Explanation novelty: The hypothesis does not provide a novel causal mechanism for the eIF5A2-associated tumor progression. Thus, it is “not a missing piece.”
 
 • On lncRNA-mediated regulation (Abstract 3):
  – Cause already established? Yes. It is well documented that lncRNAs modulate cell cycle, apoptosis, EMT, and metastasis in HCC.
  – Would we see this observation if the hypothesis was true: While proper mRNA export is important in gene regulation, the dysregulation of lncRNAs and their downstream effects on signaling pathways and EMT are already explained by other mechanisms independent of nuclear mRNA export fidelity.
  – Explanation novelty: This observation, too, is adequately explained by existing lncRNA mechanisms; therefore, the hypothesis does not add a new causal piece—“not a missing piece.”

3. Causal analysis (summary):
 “Would we see some of the observations if the hypothesis was true:” If CIAO2A upregulation improved the regulation and fidelity of mRNA export, one might expect a decrease in the accumulation of aberrantly processed transcripts. However, the principal observations—such as altered RNA modifications, eIF5A2 upregulation driving EMT, and lncRNA dysregulation—are already explained by well-established mechanisms in liver cancer. Thus, the hypothesis does not provide a novel causal pathway for a subset of these observations.

4. Disproof analysis:
 “Does some observations disprove the hypothesis:” None of the observations directly contradict the possibility that proper mRNA export is beneficial. However, they strongly implicate other well-characterized molecular mechanisms (RNA modifications, translation initiation factors, lncRNAs) in tumor progression. This suggests that even if the CIAO2A axis plays a role, it is not the missing or foremost causal explanation for the oncogenic events observed in these abstracts.

5. Conclusion:
Since the mechanisms associated with RNA modifications, eIF5A2, and lncRNAs are already well-established in driving liver tumor progression—and they account much more directly for the experimental and observational data—the proposed role of CIAO2A in enhancing mRNA export does not appear to fill a novel causal gap.
