1. Hypothesis Summary

The hypothesis posits that in liver neoplasm, higher expression of the PAFAH1B3 gene increases the activity of 1-alkyl-2-acetylglycerophosphocholine esterase. This enzymatic boost, together with the coordinated expression of lipid metabolism genes (e.g., LPCAT2), is proposed to disturb the normal balance of lipid mediators. The resulting dysregulated lipid profile is suggested to activate signaling pathways that drive angiogenesis and metastasis, thus worsening the cancer prognosis. Conversely, reduced expression of PAFAH1B3 (or its co-regulators) may lower enzyme activity, leading to a less aggressive tumor phenotype.
