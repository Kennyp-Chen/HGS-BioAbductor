The hypothesis proposes that in liver neoplasm, PAFAH1B3—an enzyme or regulatory protein—physically or functionally interacts with other regulators of lipid metabolism, specifically including a putative gene referred to as GeneX. It suggests that when PAFAH1B3 is upregulated and GeneX (a positive modulator of a particular esterase) is downregulated, there is a net decrease in the activity of 1-alkyl-2-acetylglycerophosphocholine esterase. The hypothesis further infers that therapeutic interventions, aimed either at restoring the function of these positive lipid regulators or inhibiting PAFAH1B3, might reactivate the esterase pathway and thereby slow or reverse the aggressive progression of liver neoplasm.
