1. Hypothesis Summary

The hypothesis posits that in liver neoplasms the enzyme PAFAH1B3, when upregulated, initiates a cascade by functionally coupling with LPCAT1. This partnership is suggested to enhance the activity of the enzyme 1-alkyl-2-acetylglycerophosphocholine esterase, leading to a remodeling of the lipid landscape. As a result, a unique profile of bioactive lipid species is generated, which modulate classical fatty acid metabolic pathways driven by PPARα/SCD1 and concurrently engage specific lipid receptors. This dual action is proposed to potentiate angiogenic and metastatic signaling. The hypothesis further outlines an experimental strategy incorporating CRISPR interference, high-resolution lipidomic profiling, and single-cell RNA sequencing in combination with machine learning to dissect this molecular interplay and its temporal dynamics, ultimately aiming to determine whether disrupting this signaling axis can yield improved therapeutic outcomes in liver cancer.
