1. Hypothesis Summary

The hypothesis posits that in liver neoplasms, the overexpression of PAFAH1B3 leads to direct physical interactions with lipid-regulatory proteins—particularly PLA2G4A. This interaction is proposed to interfere with the normal activity of the enzyme 1-Alkyl-2-Acetylglycerophosphocholine esterase, which is critical for phospholipid turnover. As a result, disrupted phospholipid dynamics cause alterations in membrane microdomain organization and cell signaling pathways, thereby promoting metastatic behavior and a more aggressive cancer phenotype. The hypothesis also outlines the use of modern molecular techniques such as CRISPR-based gene editing, co-immunoprecipitation, proteomics, and in silico analyses to validate this sequence of events.
