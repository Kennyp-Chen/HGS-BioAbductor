1. Observation extraction:
 • Abstract 1: In colorectal cancer liver metastases, several genes (e.g., FJX1, CSTL1, IER5L, KLHL35) are upregulated in patients with poorer prognoses. In particular, FJX1 is highlighted as being strongly linked to metastasis, potentially via effects on chromatin modification, Notch signaling, and cell senescence.
 • Abstract 2: In a rat model of colorectal liver colonization, there is deregulation of many genes during different colonization stages, notably in claudins, IGFBPs, and cadherin changes associated with epithelial–mesenchymal transition.
 • Abstract 3: In hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), the premalignant ECM environment is crucial. Elevated expression of the nuclear receptor RORγ drives the matrisome program, enhancing ECM remodeling (e.g., increased fibronectin-1, collagen production) and promoting metastasis.

2. Causal analysis (individual):

Observation (Abstract 1 – FJX1 & related gene expression changes):
 • Cause status: The expression changes (e.g., FJX1 upregulation) are already established as correlating with metastasis through pathways like chromatin modification and Notch signaling.
 • Would we see this observation if the hypothesis was true: If elevated COL1A2 triggered an integrin-mediated cascade leading to nuclear retention of mRNAs, while it could contribute to a malignant phenotype, it does not directly account for the specific upregulation of FJX1 or its established links. 
 • Conclusion: This is not a missing piece, as better established explanations exist for these gene expression changes.

Observation (Abstract 2 – Gene deregulation in CRC metastasis models):
 • Cause status: The deregulation of numerous genes (e.g., claudins, IGFBPs, cadherin downregulation) is already observed in the process of liver colonization and EMT.
 • Would we see this observation if the hypothesis was true: Although an integrin-triggered signaling cascade might influence downstream gene expression, the dynamic modulation observed (early deregulation followed by normalization) is more conventionally explained by the known steps of metastasis and EMT rather than by alterations in nuclear export regulators.
 • Conclusion: Again, this observation is already explained by established mechanisms and is not uniquely or more plausibly explained by the proposed COL1A2/integrin-nuclear export pathway—so not a missing piece.

Observation (Abstract 3 – ECM remodeling in HCC):
 • Cause status: The contribution of ECM remodeling to metastasis is well established, with RORγ directly driving ECM gene expression (including collagens) and modifying the tumor microenvironment.
 • Would we see this observation if the hypothesis was true: Given that COL1A2 encodes a component of collagen, elevated COL1A2 could theoretically contribute to ECM remodeling via integrin signaling. However, the observation emphasizes RORγ’s role and well‐defined matrisome regulation, which already explains the ECM changes.
 • Conclusion: While there is an overlap in the ECM theme, the established role of RORγ and the detailed mechanisms already provided in HCC make the new pathway (involving nuclear export regulators) less compelling. This is not a missing piece but rather an alternative mechanism that does not supplant the current explanation.

3. Causal analysis (summary):
 • Would we see some of the observations if the hypothesis was true: It is plausible that elevated COL1A2 could affect integrin-mediated signaling and perhaps indirectly influence malignancy features. However, the observations from the abstracts are already convincingly explained by other well-established mechanisms (for instance, FJX1 and Notch in CRC metastasis, EMT signaling, and RORγ-driven ECM remodeling in HCC). Therefore, the hypothesis does not fill a novel causal gap for these observations.

4. Disproof analysis:
 • Does some observations disprove the hypothesis: None of the observations directly contradict the possibility that increased COL1A2 could trigger an integrin-mediated cascade. However, the established roles of other regulators (e.g., FJX1, RORγ) in colorectal cancer liver metastasis and HCC suggest that the new mechanism is unnecessary to account for the observed phenomena. This makes the hypothesis less likely relative to existing explanations.
