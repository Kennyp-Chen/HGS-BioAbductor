## "Exploring RAB11A Pathway to Hinder Chronic Myeloid Leukemia-Induced Angiogenesis In Vivo" (PMID: 36986603)
"From a molecular point of view, the small GTP-binding protein Rab11a, involved in the endosomal slow recycling pathway, has been shown to play a crucial role for the neoangiogenic process at the bone marrow of CML patients, by controlling the secretion of exosomes by CML cells, and by regulating the recycling of vascular endothelial factor receptors. ... These results demonstrate the relevance of Rab11 for the neoangiogenesis mediated by tumor exosomes, whose deleterious effect may be counteracted via targeted silencing of these crucial genes; thus, decreasing the number of pro-tumoral exosomes at the tumor microenvironment."

This paper provides direct evidence that tumor-derived exosomal mRNAs (under the control of RAB11A and related export machinery) drive pro-angiogenic processes that are intimately connected to tumor progression. The export of mRNA via exosomes directly modulates the tumor microenvironment to promote angiogenesis and tumor growth.
