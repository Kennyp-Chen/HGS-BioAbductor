1. Hypothesis Summary  
────────────────────────────  
The hypothesis proposes that in liver neoplasm, the overexpression of COL1A2 increases collagen synthesis and remodels the extracellular matrix (ECM), thereby altering the tumor microenvironment. This modified environment is hypothesized to affect the regulation of key mRNA export factors (notably NUP62 and NXF1). When these export regulators are downregulated or impaired, the export of critical transcripts—especially those that control cell cycle progression and apoptosis—is disrupted. The cumulative impact of ECM remodeling (driven by COL1A2) combined with defective mRNA export is suggested to sustain signaling cascades that enhance metastasis and angiogenesis, leading to a poorer prognosis for patients.
