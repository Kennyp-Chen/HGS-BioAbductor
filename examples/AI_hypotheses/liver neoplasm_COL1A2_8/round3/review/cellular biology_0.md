1. Hypothesis Summary  
─────────────────────────────  
The hypothesis posits that in liver neoplasms, overexpression of COL1A2 has a dual function. First, it remodels the extracellular matrix (ECM) akin to the established roles of COL4A1 and RORγ-mediated ECM gene expression, thereby altering the tumor microenvironment. Second, this remodeling promotes enhanced integrin engagement, which in turn triggers the activation of key intracellular signaling cascades—specifically the FAK/PI3K-AKT and MAPK pathways. The activation of these pathways is proposed to lead to the phosphorylation of mRNA export regulators, namely XPO1 and selected RNA-binding proteins. This post-translational modification is conjectured to selectively increase the nuclear export and translation of oncogenic mRNAs, thereby driving metastatic behavior and angiogenesis in liver cancer. The experimental design suggests employing CRISPR-based gene editing to manipulate COL1A2 expression, complemented by quantitative phosphoproteomics for kinetic mapping of kinase activities and RNA immunoprecipitation assays to assess mRNA export selectivity.
