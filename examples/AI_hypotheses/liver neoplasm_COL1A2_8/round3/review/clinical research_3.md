1. Hypothesis Summary

The hypothesis posits that in liver tumors, COL1A2 overexpression instigates significant extracellular matrix (ECM) remodeling. This remodeling then activates integrin-mediated FAK (focal adhesion kinase) signaling, creating a cascade that reaches the nucleus. Specifically, FAK signaling is proposed to phosphorylate key components of the mRNA export machinery (such as NXF1 and THO complex members) and to promote the activity of transcriptional co-activators like YAP/TAZ. These nuclear events are believed to selectively enhance the export of mRNAs that encode proteins driving angiogenesis and metastasis, thereby accelerating tumor progression. Finally, the hypothesis suggests that targeted disruption of this COL1A2-integrin/FAK-nuclear export axis using advanced methodologies (CRISPR interference, spatial transcriptomics, and phosphoproteomics) will yield mechanistic insights and potentially new therapeutic avenues for mitigating liver tumor aggressiveness.
