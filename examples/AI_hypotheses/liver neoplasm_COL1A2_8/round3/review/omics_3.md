1. Hypothesis Summary  
─────────────────────────────  
The hypothesis posits that in liver neoplasms, an overabundance of COL1A2 results in significant extracellular matrix (ECM) remodeling. This remodeling activates integrin-mediated focal adhesion kinase (FAK) signaling, which then exerts nuclear effects by phosphorylating key mRNA export machinery components (specifically NXF1 and members of the THO complex). Concurrently, the hypothesis suggests that transcriptional co-activators, notably YAP/TAZ, are upregulated or activated, further enhancing the expression of mRNA export factors. The combined consequence of these processes is a selective increase in the export of mRNAs encoding proteins that promote angiogenesis and metastasis, thereby accelerating liver tumor progression. Furthermore, it is proposed that advanced techniques such as CRISPR interference, spatial transcriptomics, and phosphoproteomics can be used to dissect this signaling axis and potentially identify therapeutic intervention points.

─────────────────────────────  
2. Originality Assessment  
─────────────────────────────  
• The integration of ECM remodeling (via strong COL1A2 expression) with integrin/FAK signaling is well grounded, given the established role of ECM dynamics in modulating cellular behavior in tumors.  
• The novel aspect lies in linking FAK signaling to the regulation of mRNA export machinery—specifically post-translational modifications (phosphorylation) of NXF1 and THO complex components.  
• Additionally, the inclusion of YAP/TAZ as mediators that may upregulate mRNA export factors adds another layer of complexity by connecting mechanical signaling to nuclear transcription/export programs.  
• Taken together, while individual components (ECM remodeling, integrin/FAK, YAP/TAZ, mRNA export) have been studied in cancer, the cascade as a unified “COL1A2-integrin/FAK-nuclear export” axis offers an innovative perspective that distinguishes it from more traditional paradigms.

3. Validity and Strength Evaluation  
─────────────────────────────

  - The active role of FAK in relaying signals to downstream nuclear events, although plausible from a mechanotransduction standpoint, is less well established concerning direct effects on mRNA export components.
  - The proposed phosphorylation of NXF1 and THO complex members by FAK or a downstream effector is a key, yet currently speculative, connection that will require direct biochemical validation.  
  - The involvement of YAP/TAZ in upregulating mRNA export factors adds an element of transcriptional control; while YAP/TAZ are known to be responsive to mechanical cues, direct regulation of mRNA export machinery is not extensively documented.

  - The hypothesis is theoretically coherent as it integrates multiple known signaling pathways; however, the direct link between FAK signaling and nuclear export of mRNAs is not robustly supported by existing experimental data.
  - The specificity of “selective export” of pro-metastatic and angiogenic mRNAs needs to be defined: What makes these transcripts more susceptible to the proposed export mechanisms?  
  - The use of advanced techniques (CRISPR interference, spatial transcriptomics, phosphoproteomics) is well aligned with current capabilities and could, in principle, test the validity of the multi-step signaling cascade.
  
• Overall Strength:  
  - The hypothesis has strong conceptual appeal and is ambitious in linking extracellular changes to post-transcriptional regulation.  
  - It would benefit from more in-depth mechanistic insights or preliminary data that specifically demonstrate the phosphorylation events and the subsequent functional outcomes on mRNA export.
