1. Observation extraction:
• In Abstract 1, HCC tissues show GOLM1 overexpression that is associated with increased angiogenesis (via VEGF signaling), altered oxidative stress biology, and enhanced immune cell infiltration.
• In Abstract 2, HCC specimens display overexpression of GCN5 that promotes cell proliferation and tumor progression through upregulation of AIB1 and associated cell cycle markers (with established roles in oncogenesis).
• In Abstract 3, increased levels of GNG5 in breast cancer (and/or liver cancer cell models) are linked with elevated cell proliferation, migration, invasion, metabolic reprogramming, EMT, and activation of signaling pathways.

2. Causal analysis (individual):
• For the angiogenesis observation (Abstract 1):
 – Established cause? Yes: VEGF signaling activation and interactions with factors like E2F1/IGF2BP3 are already known to drive angiogenesis in HCC.
 – Would we see this observation if the hypothesis was true: If GEMIN5 upregulation caused persistent genomic instability resulting in enhanced angiogenesis, one might expect increased angiogenesis. However, angiogenesis in these studies is already well explained by GOLM1-mediated activation of VEGF signaling.
 – Conclusion: not a missing piece.
  
• For the proliferation and progression observation (Abstract 2):
 – Established cause? Yes: GCN5’s effect through upregulation of AIB1, PCNA, and the decrease of p21 is a clearly defined mechanism in HCC.
 – Would we see this observation if the hypothesis was true: The hypothesis posits that GEMIN5-induced disruption in DNA repair leads to genomic instability which could eventually promote aggressive cancer behavior, including proliferation. Yet, here the proliferation is explained directly by GCN5 action.
 – Conclusion: not a missing piece.
  
• For the migration, invasion, and metabolic reprogramming observation (Abstract 3):
 – Established cause? Yes: Overexpression of GNG5 has been experimentally linked to enhanced EMT, migration/invasion, and metabolic changes, with involvement of Wnt/β-catenin signaling.
 – Would we see this observation if the hypothesis was true: While genomic instability from reduced DNA repair (as the GEMIN5 hypothesis suggests) might contribute to tumor aggressiveness, the direct cellular changes are tied to GNG5 functions.
 – Conclusion: not a missing piece.

3. Causal analysis (summary):
 “Would we see some of the observations if the hypothesis was true:”  
While persistent genomic instability from impaired double-strand break repair could theoretically contribute to metastasis and angiogenesis, the observations in these abstracts are already accounted for by the overexpression and functional effects of genes such as GOLM1, GCN5, and GNG5. These genes exhibit well-established causal links to angiogenesis, proliferation, and invasiveness. Hence, the GEMIN5 hypothesis does not provide a novel layer to explain the observed phenomena.

4. Disproof analysis:
 “Does some observations disprove the hypothesis:”  
None of the observations explicitly contradict a role for genomic instability; however, they do not support the specific mechanism posited by the GEMIN5 hypothesis. The established causal mechanisms in these studies rely on other proteins and pathways without any mention of GEMIN5 interfering with DNA repair genes (ATM, BRCA1) or interacting with SMC1A. Thus, while no observation directly disproves the theory, they strongly favor alternative, better‐supported explanations.

5. Conclusion:
hypothesis: other explanations more likely.
