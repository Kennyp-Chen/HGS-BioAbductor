1. Hypothesis Summary:
The hypothesis proposes that GEMIN5, in conjunction with key DNA repair genes such as BRCA1 and RAD51, modulates the double-strand break (DSB) repair pathway via break-induced replication. It suggests that when GEMIN5 is upregulated, it can form aberrant complexes with these repair proteins. This interaction purportedly inhibits their normal function, leading to diminished repair efficiency, increased genomic instability, and downstream effects that facilitate aggressive cancer behaviors such as metastasis and angiogenesis in liver neoplasms. The hypothesis further speculates that restoring the balance between GEMIN5 and its interacting partners might reinstate proper repair function and potentially mitigate cancer progression.
