1. Hypothesis Summary  
──────────────────────────────
The hypothesis proposes that in liver neoplasm, an elevated expression of GEMIN5 contributes to poor prognosis. It claims that GEMIN5 disrupts the double-strand break (DSB) repair process—specifically through the break-induced replication pathway—by interacting with and dysregulating key repair proteins such as BRCA1 and RAD51. This interference is posited to lead to impaired repair complex formation and a reduction in repair enzyme activity, resulting in genomic instability. Ultimately, this instability is suggested to drive aggressive tumor behavior and metastasis. Additionally, the hypothesis implies that targeting the GEMIN5-driven network could be a potential therapeutic strategy to modify the repair pathway and improve clinical outcomes.
