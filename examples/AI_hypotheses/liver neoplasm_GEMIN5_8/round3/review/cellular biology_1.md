1. Hypothesis Summary  
─────────────────────────────  
• The hypothesis proposes that elevated GEMIN5 in liver neoplasms directly modulates the splicing of two critical DNA repair genes, RAD51 and MRE11.  
• The alteration in splicing produces alternative isoforms, shifting the DNA repair mechanism away from high-fidelity homologous recombination toward error-prone break-induced replication.  
• This switch in repair pathways is predicted to enhance genomic instability, facilitating increased metastatic and angiogenic activity in aggressive liver cancers.  
• The study design includes CRISPR/Cas9 gene editing to tweak GEMIN5 levels, along with a battery of modern techniques (high-throughput RNA and RIP sequencing, quantitative proteomics, splicing reporter assays, and single-cell transcriptomics) to elucidate the mechanism and its functional consequences.
