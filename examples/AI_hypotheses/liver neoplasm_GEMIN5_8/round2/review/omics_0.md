This disruption leads to inefficient break-induced replication and results in genomic instability. Second, GEMIN5 is proposed to reshape the tumor microenvironment via its role in mRNA processing, whereby it enhances the expression of key angiogenic and metastatic regulators such as VEGF. The combination of these processes is posited to give rise to an aggressive tumor phenotype and poorer clinical outcomes in patients. The hypothesis also outlines that modern experimental techniques such as CRISPR-mediated gene perturbation, high-throughput RNA sequencing, and proteomic analysis can be employed to dissect the underlying mechanisms.
