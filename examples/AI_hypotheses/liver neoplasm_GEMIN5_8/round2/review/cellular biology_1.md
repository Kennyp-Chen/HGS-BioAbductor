1. Hypothesis Summary:
The hypothesis proposes that in liver neoplasms the protein GEMIN5 is overexpressed and, through its interaction with key DNA repair regulators (specifically RAD51 and MRE11), alters their splicing and regulatory functions. This altered splicing is suggested to shift the balance from accurate double-strand break (DSB) repair to error-prone repair pathways, such as break-induced replication, thus promoting increased genomic instability. The resultant instability is proposed to drive processes like metastasis and angiogenesis, which contribute to a more aggressive tumor phenotype. The researchers plan on using cutting-edge molecular tools—including CRISPR-based gene editing, RNA immunoprecipitation sequencing (RIP-seq), and advanced proteomic profiling—to dissect this mechanism and identify a novel targetable pathway in liver cancer.
