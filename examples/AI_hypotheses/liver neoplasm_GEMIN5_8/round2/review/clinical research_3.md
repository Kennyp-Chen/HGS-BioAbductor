1. Hypothesis Summary

The hypothesis proposes that in liver neoplasms, overexpression of GEMIN5 leads to a disruption in the assembly of DNA repair complexes. This occurs because GEMIN5 alters the stability and translation efficiency of mRNAs coding for vital repair proteins—especially those involved in break-induced replication. Concurrently, the dysregulation of the RNA-binding protein FUS (whether via mislocalization, post-translational modification, or altered expression) further impairs enzymatic activities and signal transduction within the repair cascade. As a result, the efficiency of the double-strand break repair mechanism is markedly reduced, fostering genomic instability, tumor invasion, metastasis, and consequently, a poorer prognosis. The study is planned to use advanced techniques such as single-cell transcriptomics, CRISPR screens, and high-resolution protein-RNA interactome assays for causal inference and validation of this mechanism.
