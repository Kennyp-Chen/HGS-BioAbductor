1. Hypothesis Summary  
The hypothesis posits that an increase in MAP3K2 expression triggers a kinase cascade that enhances the expression or activity of enzymes responsible for protein geranylgeranylation—a lipid modification process. This modification increases membrane association and function of small GTPases, notably the Rho family, which in turn promotes cellular behaviors such as motility, invasion, and angiogenesis. The hypothesis further suggests that other genes interacting with MAP3K2 fine-tune this process, and that the resulting increase in geranylgeranylation contributes to a more aggressive tumor phenotype in liver neoplasm patients. Finally, it proposes that targeting this MAP3K2-centered pathway could serve as a therapeutic intervention against cancer progression.

While the individual roles of kinase cascades in cancer progression and the importance of protein geranylgeranylation (especially in regulating small GTPase activity) have been well studied, directly connecting MAP3K2 upregulation with enhanced geranylgeranylation—and by extension, with increased cancer aggressiveness in liver neoplasms—introduces a novel perspective. The idea that additional genes interacting with MAP3K2 might modulate this cascade further adds an innovative layer to understanding regulatory fine-tuning in cancer cell biology. Thus, the hypothesis offers an original synthesis that, if validated, could help bridge current gaps between kinase signaling and post-translational modifications in oncogenesis.
