1. Hypothesis Summary

The hypothesis proposes that in liver neoplasm, the upregulation of MAP3K2—potentially acting together with the regulator RABGGTB—leads to an increase in the activity of the protein geranylgeranylation machinery. This upregulation enhances the lipid attachment and membrane localization of oncogenic proteins, notably Rho GTPases. Once anchored to membranes, these proteins become activated, triggering downstream signaling pathways that promote metastasis and angiogenesis, thereby contributing to a poorer prognosis. The hypothesis further suggests that this previously uncharacterized regulatory axis could offer a promising target for novel therapeutic interventions to slow or inhibit cancer progression.
