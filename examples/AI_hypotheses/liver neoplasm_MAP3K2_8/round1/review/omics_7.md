1. Hypothesis Summary

The hypothesis posits that in liver neoplasm, the upregulation of MAP3K2 is not merely a risk factor but an active contributor to disease progression. It suggests that MAP3K2 interferes with the protein geranylgeranylation process—a post-translational modification necessary for lipid attachment and proper membrane localization of signaling proteins, including Rho GTPases. This interference could compromise cell adhesion and signaling balance. Additionally, the hypothesis speculates that MAP3K2 might interact with other signaling components (e.g., elements of the MAPK pathway or regulatory subunits of geranylgeranyl transferases) to further inhibit the enzymatic machinery responsible for geranylgeranylation, ultimately fostering processes like metastasis and angiogenesis that are linked to poorer patient prognosis.
