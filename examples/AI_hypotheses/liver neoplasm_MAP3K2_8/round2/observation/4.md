1. Observation extraction:
 • Observation 1 (Abstract 1): In liver metastases of gastric cancer, tumor‐associated macrophages secrete GDNF that binds GFRA1 on tumor cells. This GDNF–GFRA1 axis promotes cell survival under metabolic stress by regulating lysosomal function, autophagy flux, and cytosolic calcium signaling.
 • Observation 2 (Abstract 2): In metastasis, the loss of the tumor suppressor DLC1 leads to deregulated Rho GTPase activity. In particular, RhoA–dependent signaling increases tumor cell migration and invasion, highlighting established control of cytoskeletal dynamics.
 • Observation 3 (Abstract 3): Hepatocellular carcinoma features widespread deregulation of multiple signaling pathways (e.g., RAS/ERK, PI3K/AKT, NF‐κB, WNT, TGF‐β, NOTCH, Hedgehog, Hippo) and altered cellular metabolism. These changes collectively correlate with dedifferentiation and enhanced tumor aggressiveness.

Thus, although parts of the hypothesis could be consistent with the presence of heightened RhoA signaling, the cascade it proposes does not capture the primary known drivers of these phenomena.

These established mechanisms imply that even if MAP3K2 overexpression were present, its role might be redundant or secondary compared to the better‐characterized pathways.
