The resulting alteration in the post-translational modification of small GTPases, specifically RHOA and RAC1, leads to their mislocalization. Such mislocalization disrupts normal cell adhesion and promotes a migratory, metastatic phenotype. The hypothesis recommends the use of advanced techniques like CRISPR gene editing, co-immunoprecipitation combined with mass spectrometry, and high-resolution live-cell imaging to validate each step of the proposed signaling cascade and interactions.

The involvement of MAPK1 in potentiating this effect further adds a layer of complexity that distinguishes the hypothesis from more traditional views on kinase signaling in cancer.
