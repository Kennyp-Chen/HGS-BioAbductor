1. Hypothesis Summary  
─────────────────────────────  
The hypothesis posits that in hepatocellular carcinoma (a liver neoplasm), an aberrant overexpression of the kinase MAP3K2—potentially caused by deregulated non-coding RNA interactions and epigenetic modifications—initiates a signaling cascade. In this cascade, MAP3K2 activates RhoA via specific adaptor proteins such as guanine nucleotide exchange factors (GEFs). Once activated, RhoA is proposed to drive an increase in protein geranylgeranylation by upregulating the expression and activity of geranylgeranyltransferase enzymes. The resultant enhanced geranylgeranylation modifies the function of key proteins involved in cytoskeletal dynamics, leading to altered enzyme activities and signaling pathways that ultimately increase cell motility and facilitate metastasis. The hypothesis further suggests the use of state-of-the-art approaches, including CRISPR-Cas9 screens, live-cell imaging with fluorescent reporters to monitor geranylgeranylation, and advanced proteomics, to quantitatively validate each step of this cascade and potentially identify novel therapeutic targets.
