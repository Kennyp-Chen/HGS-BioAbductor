1. Hypothesis Summary
──────────────────────────────
The hypothesis proposes that in liver neoplasm, increased expression of MAP3K2 drives a dual mechanism that enhances the activity of geranylgeranyltransferases. This occurs via both upregulation of their expression and modulation of their enzymatic activation, which in turn boosts protein geranylgeranylation. The heightened prenylation, particularly of signaling proteins such as RhoA, is posited to promote their membrane localization, thereby escalating metastatic potential and angiogenesis. The study plans to explore this regulatory axis using state-of-the-art molecular tools (e.g., CRISPR for gene modulation, single-cell transcriptomics, and quantitative proteomics) to validate the pathway. Furthermore, the hypothesis distinguishes this mechanistic route from alternative pathways (e.g., those implicating MAP3K14 or REGγ-mediated mTORC1 modulation) and suggests that its findings could reveal novel therapeutic entry points for liver cancer.
