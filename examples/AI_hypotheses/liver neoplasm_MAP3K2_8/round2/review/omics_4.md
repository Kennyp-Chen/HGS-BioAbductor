1. Hypothesis Summary:
──────────────────────────────
The hypothesis posits that in liver neoplasms (specifically hepatocellular carcinoma), MAP3K2 is aberrantly overexpressed. This overexpression, potentially influenced by deregulated non-coding RNA interactions and epigenetic modifications, initiates a signaling cascade. In this cascade, MAP3K2 facilitates the activation of RhoA through specific adaptor proteins such as guanine nucleotide exchange factors (GEFs). Once activated, RhoA purportedly increases the expression and activity of geranylgeranyltransferase enzymes. This heightened geranylgeranylation of key proteins alters cytoskeletal dynamics, enzyme functions, and downstream signaling pathways, thereby enhancing cell motility and promoting metastasis. The study also outlines the use of advanced techniques (CRISPR-Cas9 screens, live-cell imaging with fluorescent reporters, and proteomics) to quantitatively validate each step of this proposed cascade and to identify novel therapeutic targets.
