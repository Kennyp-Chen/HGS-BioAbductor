1. Observation extraction:
 • Observation 1: In metastatic gastric cancer, tumor‐associated macrophages (TAMs) secrete GDNF that activates GFRA1 in tumor cells at the invasive margin. This axis promotes survival under metabolic stress by regulating lysosomal functions, autophagy flux, and cytosolic calcium signaling.
 • Observation 2: In a liver cancer model, small increases in wild‐type Met receptor tyrosine kinase (RTK) levels lead to spontaneous tumor formation. This model identifies established synthetic lethal interactions and vulnerabilities that contribute to tumorigenesis.
 • Observation 3: In liver cancer, the autophagy‐related gene MAPT is overexpressed and functionally promotes tumor cell proliferation, migration, and invasion by inhibiting autophagy and suppressing apoptosis.

 "would we see some of the observations if the hypothesis was true:" The hypothesis might coexist with these established pathways and potentially amplify metastasis. The proposed MAP3K2-mediated dysregulation of the geranylgeranyl transferase complex represents an additional, parallel pathway rather than a novel, primary causal explanation for the observations.

 "does some observations disprove the hypothesis:" None of the observations directly contradict the hypothesis. However, they do establish well‐accepted mechanisms for metastasis and tumor progression that already account for the observed phenomena. This suggests that while the proposed MAP3K2 pathway might exist in parallel, it is not required to explain these findings, and better‐characterized mechanisms already prevail.

 hypothesis: other explanations more likely

The observations support multiple established mechanisms driving tumor progression and metastasis in liver neoplasms. Although the hypothesis introduces a potentially interesting MAP3K2-mediated pathway that might affect geranylgeranylation and indirectly metastasis, the observations are better explained by the well-documented TAM-derived GDNF-GFRA1 axis, the impact of subtle Met RTK changes, and MAPT-related autophagy modulation.
