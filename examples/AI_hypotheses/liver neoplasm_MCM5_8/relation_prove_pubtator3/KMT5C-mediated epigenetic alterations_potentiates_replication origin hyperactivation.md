# literature evidence
## "Human ORC/MCM density is low in active genes and correlates with replication time but does not delimit initiation zones" (PMID: 33683199)
Exact supporting sentence from full text:
“We previously demonstrated that H4K20me3 provides a platform to enhance licensing in late-replicating heterochromatin. We functionally link replication licensing to H4K20me3 in a specific subset of late-replicating domains as we detect both elevated ORC and MCM levels when selecting for H4K20me3-enriched non-genic ASs and NRRs (Figures 5f and 6b). Whether H4K20me3 and/or additional chromatin modifications may also promote the origin-firing step remains to be investigated.”

# Analysis
The evidence shows that KMT5C (which catalyzes H4K20 trimethylation) mediates an epigenetic mark (H4K20me3) that clearly *enhances* (potentiates) replication origin licensing in certain chromatin contexts (specifically, late-replicating, non-genic regions). The relationship is slightly more qualified: it potentiates origin licensing (increasing the number of licensed origins) rather than directly causing origin “hyperactivation” (increased firing beyond licensing). However, the mechanistic step from licensing to hyperactivation is closely linked in the context of origin biology, and the language of the cited work explicitly supports a potentiating effect of KMT5C-mediated H4K20me3 on replication origin activity. Thus, there is semantic and mechanistic support in the literature for the claim.
