1. Hypothesis Summary
──────────────────────────────
The hypothesis posits that in liver neoplasms, an altered balance—specifically, an upregulation of MCM5 relative to MCM7—creates a unique, synergistic interaction with the DNA repair protein RAD51. This interaction is proposed to channel double-strand break repair (DSBR) via an error-prone break-induced replication (BIR) mechanism rather than through high-fidelity pathways. The error-prone nature of this repair is thought to contribute to genomic instability and, consequently, metastasis and poorer clinical outcomes. Further, the hypothesis implicates the epigenetic regulator KMT5C, which is claimed to enhance RAD51 complex formation, and RAD54B, which is proposed to act as a mediator by integrating signals from the wnt/β-catenin pathway. The researchers plan to use modern techniques—including CRISPR-based screens, single-cell analyses, and live-cell imaging—to clarify these interactions and assess their potential as therapeutic targets.
