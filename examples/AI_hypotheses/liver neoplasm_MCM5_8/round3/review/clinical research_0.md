1. Hypothesis Summary  
──────────────────────────────
The hypothesis proposes that in liver neoplasms, overexpression of MCM5 drives excessive replication stress through the hyperactivation of replication origins. This stress perturbs the regulatory balance of RAD51 and ATR—key proteins in homologous recombination repair—forcing tumor cells to increasingly rely on the error‐prone break-induced replication (BIR) repair pathway. Such a shift results in high genomic instability, which underpins aggressive tumor behavior (enhanced metastasis and angiogenesis) and poor clinical outcomes. The study plans to manipulate MCM5 levels using CRISPR-Cas9, monitor the heterogeneity of the DNA repair network via single-cell RNA sequencing, and analyze RAD51/ATR interactions through advanced proteomics.

• The integration of replication stress, detailed repair pathway dysregulation, and the consequences for tumor aggressiveness presents a holistic view that distinguishes it from more conventional single-facet investigations.  
• The planned use of cutting-edge tools such as CRISPR-Cas9, single-cell RNA sequencing, and proteomics adds another layer of novelty by promising dynamic and high-resolution insights into these molecular events.

RAD51 and ATR’s roles in ensuring proper DNA repair are well documented, making their perturbation likely to shift repair outcomes.
