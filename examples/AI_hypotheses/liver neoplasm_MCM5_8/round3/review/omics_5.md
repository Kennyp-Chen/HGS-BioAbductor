Hypothesis Summary:
──────────────────────────────
The hypothesis proposes that in liver neoplasms, an increased expression of MCM5 compared to MCM7 sets up a specific cooperative interaction with RAD51 that steers double-strand break repair (DSBR) via an error-prone break-induced replication pathway. This process is theorized to be modulated by the epigenetic regulator KMT5C—which aids in RAD51 complex formation—and by RAD54B, which potentially integrates signals from the wnt/β-catenin pathway. Together, these molecular interactions are suggested to promote genomic instability and metastasis, ultimately worsening clinical outcomes. The planned experimental approach includes modern techniques such as CRISPR screening, single-cell analysis, and live-cell imaging to dissect these molecular mechanisms and consider their value as therapeutic targets.

The hypothesis is relatively original due to several factors:
• It introduces the novel idea that the balance between MCM5 and MCM7 is critical, rather than looking at the upregulation of one replication factor in isolation.
• It suggests an unconventional role for proteins typically involved in replication and homologous recombination (MCM5, RAD51) contributing to an error-prone repair mechanism.
• The incorporation of KMT5C as an epigenetic modulator of RAD51 complex formation, together with a role for RAD54B linking wnt/β-catenin signaling, provides an integrative perspective that spans multiple regulatory layers—involving replication, repair, epigenetics, and signal transduction.
While individual components (e.g., roles of RAD51 in DSBR or the involvement of wnt/β-catenin in cancer) have been studied extensively, combining them in this specific regulatory network and context of liver neoplasms offers innovative angles that distinguish it from more conventional models.
