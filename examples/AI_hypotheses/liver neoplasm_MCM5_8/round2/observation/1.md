1. Observation extraction:
 • In liver cancer tissues and cells, replication factors (specifically MCM7, though the review discusses the MCM family more broadly) are overexpressed and associated with increased proliferation, cell cycle progression, and reduced patient survival.
 • Manipulation of MCM7 expression (silencing or overexpression) demonstrably alters cell viability, apoptosis, and sensitivity to chemotherapy agents (e.g., cisplatin), with linked effects on downstream signals like the PI3K/AKT pathway.
 • Reviews indicate that overexpression of MCM proteins serves as a proliferation marker in cancers; however, the mechanistic underpinnings are not completely elucidated.
 • In a separate study, the epigenetic regulator KMT5C is shown to promote liver tumor growth by facilitating DNA repair processes via its interaction with RAD51—and thus favoring homologous recombination (HR)—with inhibition of KMT5C enhancing sensitivity to PARP inhibitors.

2. Causal analysis (individual):

Observation: Overexpression of MCM7 in liver cancer is associated with enhanced proliferation and survival.
 • Cause already established: Overexpression of replication factors (here MCM7) is a well‐known driver of cancer cell proliferation.
 • Would we see this observation if the hypothesis was true: If MCM5 were uniquely overexpressed and induced excess replication stress, we might expect increased or altered replication dynamics in liver tumors. However, the observation is about MCM7 (or the MCM family in general), whose role in accelerating cell cycle progression is already known.
 • This mechanistic role is therefore not a novel explanation; it is “not a missing piece.”

Observation: Activation of the PI3K/AKT pathway is linked to MCM7 overexpression.
 • Cause already established: MCM7 overexpression has been shown to activate PI3K/AKT signaling in liver cancer.
 • Would we see this observation if the hypothesis was true: The hypothesis regarding MCM5 does not address PI3K/AKT signaling; thus, the activation of this pathway is explained independently of any unique role for MCM5.
 • This observation remains already explained by existing work, so it is “not a missing piece.”

Observation: KMT5C, a histone methyltransferase, enhances DNA repair through its interaction with RAD51, promoting the formation of the RAD51/RAD54 complex and high-fidelity homologous recombination.
 • Cause already established: The molecular studies robustly show that KMT5C facilitates DNA repair via a RAD51-mediated homologous recombination mechanism.
 • Would we see this observation if the hypothesis was true: The hypothesis posits that epigenetic modulators like KMT5C would bias repair toward error-prone break-induced replication (BIR) via an enhanced role for RAD52. However, the observed data demonstrate a role for KMT5C in promoting accurate homologous repair. This discrepancy means that if the hypothesis were true, we would expect a shift toward BIR rather than HR, which is not supported by the experimental observations.
 • Thus, this is not a novel explanation but contradicts the proposed mechanism; it is “not a missing piece.”

Observation: MCM proteins, in general, are recognized as biomarkers for proliferation in cancers.
 • Cause already established: The prognostic and biological roles of MCM proteins in cancer have been documented, although the specific contributions of individual family members (such as MCM5 versus MCM7) are less clear.
 • Would we see this observation if the hypothesis was true: While the hypothesis targets a unique role of MCM5 in exaggerating replication stress and channeling DNA repair via RAD52-mediated BIR, the general overexpression of MCM proteins is already an acknowledged contributor to tumor progression.
 • Hence, this does not provide a novel causal insight and is “not a missing piece.”

3. Causal analysis (summary):
 Would we see some of the observations if the hypothesis was true: A liver tumor with heightened replication stress might indeed show altered replication protein dynamics. However, the observations from the abstracts point to established roles for general MCM proteins (especially MCM7) in driving cell proliferation via known signaling pathways, and they document KMT5C’s role in promoting high-fidelity homologous recombination via RAD51 rather than an error-prone BIR pathway. Therefore, the hypothesis’s contention that MCM5 uniquely exacerbates replication stress, triggering a RAD52-mediated BIR repair bias and being influenced by KMT5C to shift repair away from HR, is not supported by the evidence presented.

4. Disproof analysis:
 Does some observations disprove the hypothesis: The clear documentation that KMT5C enhances DNA repair by forming the RAD51/RAD54 complex—and thereby promoting homologous recombination—directly contradicts the hypothesis’s proposal that KMT5C biases the repair landscape toward an error-prone BIR mechanism via RAD52. This key conflict between observed mechanisms and the hypothesis provides a strong contradiction.

5. Conclusion:
 Taking into account that (a) the established roles of MCM proteins (especially MCM7) and the documented mechanistic insights into KMT5C-mediated homologous recombination already provide adequate explanations for the observed phenotypes in liver cancer, and (b) the hypothesis relies on replacing these with a novel MCM5-RAD52-related mechanism biased by KMT5C – which is directly at odds with the observed involvement of RAD51 in KMT5C’s function—the hypothesis is effectively contradicted by current evidence.
