1. Hypothesis Summary

The hypothesis posits that within liver neoplasms, overexpression of the protein AHNAK2 plays a central oncogenic role by engaging two complementary mechanisms. First, AHNAK2 overexpression is proposed to enhance the activity of geranylgeranyltransferases, which leads to increased protein geranylgeranylation and activation of small GTPases. Second, AHNAK2 is suggested to interact with additional regulatory pathways—including the epigenetic modifiers NAA40 and HDAC2 and the tumor suppressor lnc-GNAT1-1—to construct a synergistic network that amplifies oncogenic signaling. This combined effect is believed to drive increased metastatic and angiogenic potential in liver cancer, thereby worsening patient outcomes. The hypothesis further advocates the use of advanced experimental techniques, such as CRISPR-based gene editing and high-resolution proteomics, to validate these interactions and elucidate novel therapeutic targets.
