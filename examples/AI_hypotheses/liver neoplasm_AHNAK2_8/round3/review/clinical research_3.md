1. Hypothesis Summary

The hypothesis proposes that in liver cancers, the protein AHNAK2 is overexpressed and works in concert with known oncogenic signals to alter protein geranylgeranylation. The central idea is that AHNAK2 directly binds to geranylgeranyltransferase enzymes, thereby inducing selective changes in the lipid modification of key signaling molecules such as Rho and Ras GTPases. This modification is posited to disrupt their proper membrane localization and downstream signaling. In addition, the hypothesis suggests that through cooperative interactions with RAB family proteins, AHNAK2 enhances vesicular trafficking. Together with its integration into a broader network involving other oncogenic mediators (like NAA40, NEK2, and circRNA-mediated regulation of AHNAK), these events are believed to promote increased cellular motility, angiogenesis, and metastasis, culminating in a more aggressive liver cancer phenotype. The hypothesis will be tested using advanced methods such as CRISPR/Cas9 screening, proximity-labeling mass spectrometry, live-cell imaging, and integrated proteogenomic profiling.

2. Originality Assessment

– The hypothesis is innovative in that it links AHNAK2 not only to established oncogenic pathways but also to the specific modulation of protein geranylgeranylation, a process that is crucial for the proper functioning of various signaling proteins.  
– Introducing the notion that AHNAK2 can directly interact with geranylgeranyltransferase enzymes and thereby affect Rho/Ras dynamics offers a novel mechanistic insight, distinguishing it from more traditional views of oncogenesis.  
– The integration of AHNAK2 into a broader network that includes NAA40, NEK2, and circRNA-mediated regulation further expands its potential impact, suggesting a multifaceted role in liver neoplasia that has not been widely explored.  
– Overall, while individual components (e.g., roles of Rho/Ras and vesicular trafficking in cancer) are established, the specific synergistic relationship with AHNAK2 and protein geranylgeranylation represents an original angle that could provide new insights into liver cancer’s pathobiology.

  • It further extends this mechanistic insight to cellular processes (altered membrane localization and signaling, enhanced vesicular trafficking) and ultimately to phenotypic outcomes (increased motility, angiogenesis, metastasis).  
  • The sequential connection—from molecular interaction to cancer aggressiveness—is plausible and supported by established principles in cancer biology.

– Notable Weaknesses and Gaps:

  • The specificity of the effect on Rho and Ras GTPases—versus potentially other geranylgeranylated proteins—needs further clarification.
  • The proposed involvement of RAB family proteins and the integration with other oncogenic signals (NAA40, NEK2, and circRNA-mediated mechanisms) adds complexity. It might be challenging to delineate the individual contributions of each element within this network.
