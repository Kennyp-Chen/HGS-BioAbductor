1. Observation extraction:
 • Observation 1 (NEK2 study): NEK2 overexpression in HCC is linked to tumor growth, drug resistance, metastasis, and angiogenesis. Its actions are attributed to activation of downstream pathways (pAKT/NF‑κB, MMP activation, IL‑8) that promote these malignant phenotypes.
 • Observation 2 (circRNA and AHNAK study): An upregulated circRNA (circ_0008194) is shown to sponge miR‑190a, thereby increasing AHNAK expression. This upregulation promotes epithelial–mesenchymal transition and enhances metastasis in liver cancer.
 • Observation 3 (NAA40 study): NAA40 is significantly upregulated in LIHC from early stages. Its expression is associated with high proliferation, poor differentiation, and aggressive liver cancer especially in contexts of p53 inactivation, correlating with worse patient survival.

3. Causal analysis (summary):
 Would we see some of the observations if the hypothesis was true: The hypothesis could, in theory, add an extra layer of oncogenic modulation by linking AHNAK2 overexpression to altered protein geranylgeranylation, which might indirectly affect Rho/Ras signaling, vesicular trafficking (via RAB proteins), and thus cellular motility and angiogenesis. However, the major observed features in HCC (driven by NEK2 signaling, the circRNA–AHNAK axis, and NAA40 upregulation) already have well‐characterized causal mechanisms. Therefore, while the proposed mechanism might occur concurrently, it does not offer a novel or necessary explanation for the observations.

4. Disproof analysis:
 Does some observations disprove the hypothesis: None of the observations explicitly contradict the possibility that AHNAK2 might interact with geranylgeranyltransferase enzymes. However, each observation is already strongly accounted for by other established pathways. The current data sets do not provide independent evidence of altered protein geranylgeranylation or direct involvement of AHNAK2, making it hard to attribute a novel causal role to this mechanism.

5. Conclusion:
 The hypothesis suggests that overexpression of AHNAK2 modulates protein geranylgeranylation and works synergistically with known oncogenic mechanisms (NEK2, circRNA-driven AHNAK upregulation, and NAA40 upregulation) to promote metastasis and angiogenesis. However, the observations instead are explained through well‐established pathways with direct causal links. There is no novel element provided by the AHNAK2–geranylgeranylation mechanism that is required to account for these phenomena, and alternative mechanisms (already established in the literature) more directly explain the clinical and molecular findings.
