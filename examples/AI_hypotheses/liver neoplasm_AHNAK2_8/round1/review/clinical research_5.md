1. Hypothesis Summary  
The hypothesis posits that in liver cancers, high expression levels of AHNAK2 interact with other regulatory proteins—specifically components of the geranylgeranyl transferase enzyme complex and small GTPases—to boost the process of protein geranylgeranylation. In this model, increased AHNAK2 expression enhances the enzymatic activity that attaches geranylgeranyl groups to target proteins. This post‐translational modification is proposed to alter the membrane localization and function of small GTPases, thereby activating signaling pathways that promote critical aspects of cancer aggressiveness such as cellular migration, angiogenesis, and metastasis. Cumulatively, these changes are expected to contribute to a more aggressive tumor phenotype and poorer clinical outcomes in liver neoplasms.
