1. Hypothesis Summary

The hypothesis proposes that high levels of the AHNAK2 protein in liver neoplasms downregulate key modulators of the protein geranylgeranylation pathway—particularly regulators of small GTPases. This downregulation is thought to reduce the lipid modification of proteins necessary for proper cell adhesion and signaling, thereby promoting tumor metastasis and angiogenesis, and ultimately leading to a poorer prognosis. In addition, the hypothesis suggests that other interacting genes within this regulatory network may further modulate enzyme activity and signal transduction related to geranylgeranylation. It proposes that targeting these interactions to restore normal pathway activity might yield a novel therapeutic strategy for liver cancer.
