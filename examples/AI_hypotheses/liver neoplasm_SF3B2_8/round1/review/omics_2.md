1. Hypothesis Summary:
The hypothesis posits that in liver neoplasms, upregulated expression of the splicing factor SF3B2 alters splicing patterns of key genes involved in DNA repair and regulatory pathways, such as TP53 and RAD51. This aberrant splicing is suggested to facilitate the integration of viral DNA into the host genome, leading to increased genomic instability. As a subsequent result, oncogenic processes—including metastasis and angiogenesis—are activated, which may contribute to a worsened clinical outcome. In addition, the interplay between SF3B2, TP53, and RAD51 could establish a regulatory network that shifts cellular homeostasis, further driving liver cancer progression.
