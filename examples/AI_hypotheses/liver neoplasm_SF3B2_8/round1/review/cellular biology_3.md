1. Hypothesis Summary

The hypothesis posits that the overexpression of the splicing factor SF3B2, along with another splicing modulator SRSF1, causes aberrant splicing of key regulatory genes that are involved in the integration of viral DNA into the host genome. This mis-splicing is proposed to increase the likelihood of viral genome integration, which then contributes to genomic instability. Such instability is believed to trigger oncogenic pathways that promote tumor progression in liver cancer, specifically by enhancing processes like metastasis and angiogenesis. Ultimately, this cascade of events is linked to a poorer prognosis in liver neoplasm patients.
