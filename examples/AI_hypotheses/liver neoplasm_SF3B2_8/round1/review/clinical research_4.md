1. Hypothesis Summary  
─────────────────────────────  
The hypothesis proposes that in liver neoplasm, increased expression of the splicing factor SF3B2 induces aberrant alternative splicing of key regulatory genes—specifically p53 and APOBEC3B. Such aberrant splicing purportedly disrupts normal DNA repair and alters regulation of viral DNA integration into the host genome. The resulting genomic instability is hypothesized to drive oncogenic processes, including metastasis and angiogenesis, eventually worsening the prognosis of liver neoplasm. Additionally, the hypothesis suggests that interactions between SF3B2 and other genes may modulate enzymatic activities and signal transduction pathways in a synergistic manner that further promotes tumor progression.

2. Originality Assessment

• The idea that splicing factor dysregulation contributes to tumorigenesis is a well‐recognized concept in molecular oncology.  
• However, the specific focus on SF3B2—compared to the more frequently studied SF3B1—and its direct role in mediating the alternative splicing of p53 and APOBEC3B provides a distinctive angle in the context of liver neoplasm.  
• The proposed link between altered splicing and the regulation of viral DNA integration is less conventional and represents an innovative perspective.  
• Overall, the hypothesis does offer some innovation by connecting SF3B2‐mediated splicing alterations to both classical tumor suppressor (p53) dysfunction and viral integration processes, though it builds upon existing themes in splicing regulation instead of entirely novel pathways.
