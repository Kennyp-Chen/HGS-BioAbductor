1. Observation extraction:
 • Abstract 1: The splicing factor SF3B1 is overexpressed in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) and is associated with tumor aggressiveness, oncogenic splicing variants (KLF6-SV1, BCL-XL), and decreased overall survival. Both genetic silencing and pharmacological blockade of SF3B1 reduce cell viability, proliferation, and migration, and alter multiple signaling pathways.
 • Abstract 2: Several splicing factors (including PSF, SRSF3, SRSF6) show dysregulated expression in liver disease and HCC. Although aberrant splicing is observed in HCC, some splicing factor upregulation (e.g., SRSF3) appears to correlate with prolonged overall survival, suggesting a complex role in disease progression.
 • Abstract 3: A suite of spliceosome components, notably EIF4A3, is aberrantly expressed in HCC. EIF4A3 upregulation is associated with increased tumor aggressiveness and decreased survival, and its silencing modulates the splicing of key HCC-related genes such as FGFR4.

Therefore, the hypothesis does not provide a novel explanation for any subset of the observations; the established mechanisms offer more direct explanations for the splicing-related oncogenic processes noted.

The absence of evidence connecting viral integration with altered splicing in these studies indirectly challenges the proposed hypothesis.

 The observations from the abstracts show that various splicing factors (SF3B1, EIF4A3, etc.) are dysregulated in HCC and contribute to oncogenic splicing events leading to tumor aggressiveness.
