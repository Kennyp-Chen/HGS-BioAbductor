1. Observation extraction:
 • Abstract 1: In hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), SF3B1 is overexpressed at the RNA and protein levels; its overexpression is associated with tumor aggressiveness, the expression of oncogenic splicing variants (e.g., KLF6‐SV1, BCL‐XL), and decreased overall survival. Pharmacological inhibition (pladienolide‐B) and genetic silencing of SF3B1 reduce cell proliferation, migration, and tumor formation.
 • Abstract 2: In murine liver disease models and human HCC, several splicing factors (e.g., PSF, SRSF3, SRSF6) have altered expression profiles that correlate with liver damage stages and patient outcomes. While splicing patterns of selected liver disease–related genes are modified, these changes occur early in liver injury and may even contribute to hepatocyte homeostasis.
 • Abstract 3: SF3B4 is identified as an early diagnostic biomarker in HCC, where its overexpression alters splicing (e.g., aberrant splicing of the tumor suppressor KLF4), leading to cell cycle dysregulation and contributing to tumor initiation and progression.
