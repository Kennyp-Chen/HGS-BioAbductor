1. Observation extraction:
 a. In the first abstract, SF3B1 is overexpressed at RNA/protein levels in HCC; its overexpression is linked to tumor aggressiveness, oncogenic splicing variants (e.g., KLF6-SV1, BCL-XL), and decreased overall survival. Its inhibition (via silencing or pharmacological blockade with pladienolide‐B) reduces proliferation, migration, and tumor growth.
 b. In the second abstract, various splicing factors (such as PSF, SRSF3, SRSF6) display disease stage–specific expression alterations in liver damage and HCC. Although changes in splicing factor levels occur early in liver injury, their modulation is not directly linked to classical oncogenic splicing alterations in the selected target genes, and in some cases higher levels correlate with extended overall survival.
 c. In the third abstract, a broad dysregulation of spliceosome components is observed in HCC. Specific factors (e.g., EIF4A3) are upregulated, and such dysregulation is associated with tumor aggressiveness, recurrence, and mortality through mechanisms like altering FGFR4 splicing.

2. Causal analysis (individual):

Observation a:
 • Established cause? Yes – Dysregulation and overexpression of SF3B1 are established events contributing to oncogenic splicing and aggressive tumor behavior in HCC.
 • Would we see this observation if the hypothesis was true: If SF3B2 upregulation were driving cancer progression via altered DNA repair and enhanced viral integration, a similar phenotype of increased aggressiveness might be expected. However, the observation specifically implicates SF3B1 rather than SF3B2.
 • Explanation: This is not a missing piece because SF3B1’s role and its established causal effects are already documented, and a viral integration mechanism is not invoked here.

Observation b:
 • Established cause? Partially – Aberrant splicing factor expression occurs early during liver damage and is correlated with disease state, although the precise mechanistic contribution remains multifactorial.
 • Would we see this observation if the hypothesis was true: If SF3B2 mediated viral integration in liver neoplasm, one might anticipate seeing specific splicing defects in DNA repair genes rather than the general SF dysregulation profile described.
 • Explanation: The observations are well accounted for by the general dysregulation of splicing factors in liver injury, and they do not highlight any specific viral integration mechanism. Thus, this does not offer a novel explanation.

Observation c:
 • Established cause? Yes – The association of dysregulated spliceosome components (like EIF4A3) with tumor aggressiveness via mechanisms such as aberrant FGFR4 splicing is an established explanation.
 • Would we see this observation if the hypothesis was true: If SF3B2 were responsible for promoting viral DNA integration through altered splicing of DNA repair genes, the expected markers would involve altered expression of DNA repair pathway genes or evidence of viral integration, rather than the modulation of FGFR4 splicing as presented.
 • Explanation: Again, the observation is better explained by previously documented spliceosome component dysregulation rather than a novel viral integration pathway.

3. Causal analysis (summary):
 Would we see some of the observations if the hypothesis was true: While aggressive tumor behavior is common to both the established findings and the hypothesis, the specific mechanism of promoting viral DNA integration via SF3B2 acting on DNA repair genes (RAD51, BRCA1) is not addressed in these studies. The current observations point toward different splicing factors (e.g., SF3B1, EIF4A3) that affect oncogenic splicing programs through other mechanisms. Therefore, the hypothesis does not account for these observations in a novel or more compelling way.

4. Disproof analysis:
 Does some observations disprove the hypothesis: None of the abstracts provide evidence of viral DNA integration or implicate alterations in DNA repair gene splicing (RAD51, BRCA1) as a driver in HCC. The absence of any mention of viral integration or associated DNA repair defects suggests that the proposed mechanism is not supported by these findings, though it is not directly disproved.

5. Conclusion:
 The existing observations consistently implicate dysregulation of splicing factors (such as SF3B1 and EIF4A3) in HCC progression through well‐established oncogenic splicing pathways—not through aberrant viral integration mediated by SF3B2. Therefore, although SF3B2 upregulation as a promoter of viral DNA integration is a novel idea, the current data do not support this mechanism over the recognized explanations.
