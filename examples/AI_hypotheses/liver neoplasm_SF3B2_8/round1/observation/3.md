1. Observation extraction:
 • Abstract 1: In various murine models and human HCC samples, liver-specific splicing factors (e.g., SRSF3, PSF, SRSF6) are dysregulated early during liver injury. Their altered expression correlates with changes in alternative splicing events and, indirectly, with patient outcomes such as tumor recurrence and overall survival.
 • Abstract 2: In liver cancer, the splicing factor SF3B1 is overexpressed at both RNA and protein levels. This overexpression is linked to the generation of oncogenic splice variants (e.g., KLF6-SV1, BCL-XL), increased tumor aggressiveness, and a decrease in overall survival. Inhibition of SF3B1 reduces proliferation, migration, and other malignant features.
 • Abstract 3: A broad array of spliceosome components is dysregulated in HCC, with elements such as EIF4A3 being consistently elevated and associated with increased tumor aggressiveness, altered splicing of key regulators (e.g., FGFR4), and poorer prognosis.

Instead, they focus on alternative splicing changes leading to oncogenic variants and altered cell signaling. This discrepancy suggests that the hypothesis introducing viral integration as a driver is not supported by the available data.
