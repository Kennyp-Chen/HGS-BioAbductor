 If the hypothesis were valid, one might expect that upregulation of SF3B2 (along with SF3B1 and EIF4A3) would also lead to aberrant splicing of pivotal DNA repair regulators like RAD51 and BRCA1, contributing to genomic instability and promoting viral DNA integration in the setting of hepatitis B infection. However, the abstracts primarily document spliceosome dysregulation affecting pathways such as FGFR4 signaling and apoptotic regulation. This indicates that while the hypothesis extends the role of splicing factors to DNA repair, it does not account for the key observations that are better explained by other established mechanisms.

However, the specific mis-splicing of DNA repair regulators (RAD51, BRCA1) and the consequential predisposition to viral DNA integration are not observed in the abstracts. Given that the already described causal chains (e.g., EIF4A3 impacting FGFR4 splicing) provide a clearer mechanism for tumor aggressiveness in HCC, the novel pathway proposed in the hypothesis is not supported by the current observational data.
