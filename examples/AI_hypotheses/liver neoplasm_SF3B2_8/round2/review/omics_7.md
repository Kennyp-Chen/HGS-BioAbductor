1. Hypothesis Summary  
─────────────────────────────  
The hypothesis proposes that within liver neoplasms, the upregulation of the splicing factor SF3B2 works in concert with the genomic guardians ATM and TP53. This interaction is believed to lead to two main consequences: (a) a rewiring of RNA splicing, producing oncogenic splice variants, and (b) a reduced capacity for proper DNA repair, resulting in the formation of genomic fragile sites. These fragile sites not only foster oncogenic signaling directly but also might act as hotspots for viral DNA integration, thereby compounding genomic instability. The compounded effects drive the activation of pathways that support metastasis and angiogenesis, ultimately leading to a poorer cancer prognosis. The hypothesis also outlines a multi-pronged approach to testing these claims using advanced sequencing (RNA and DNA), CRISPR-mediated gene modulation, and proteomic interaction studies.

• Addressing the multifaceted downstream consequences such as metastasis and angiogenesis.

This suggests an innovative cross-talk between mRNA processing and genomic stability pathways in a tissue-specific context, distinguishing the hypothesis from more traditional views.
