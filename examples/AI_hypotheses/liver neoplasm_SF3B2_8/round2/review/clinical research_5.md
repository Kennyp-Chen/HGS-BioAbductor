 • The combined genomic disruptions are hypothesized to lead to a higher likelihood of metastatic dissemination and poorer clinical outcomes.  
 • A multi-pronged experimental approach is planned, including advanced transcriptomic analyses (long-read and single-cell RNA sequencing) and CRISPR-based perturbation assays, to directly link SF3B2 expression with splicing alterations, DNA repair deficits, and viral integration events.

 • The hypothesis is innovative in its specific connection of spliceosome dysregulation to both impaired DNA repair and viral integration in liver cancer.

 • Moreover, the inclusion of chronic viral infection (e.g., hepatitis B virus) as a factor that exploits compromised repair mechanisms to enhance genomic instability adds a novel, multifaceted dimension that distinguishes this hypothesis from more conventional oncogenic models.

 • One area requiring further evidence is the direct causal relationship between SF3B2 upregulation and altered splicing outcomes. While correlations may exist, additional mechanistic studies (e.g., demonstration of SF3B2 binding to pre-mRNA sites critical for RAD51 and BRCA1 splicing) would solidify the claim.
