# Search Strategy and Reasoning
I started by searching PubMed using combinations of the minimal MeSH-relevant keywords: "chromatin modifiers" and "open chromatin landscape." These terms map closely to the entities regardless of the relational direction. Initial queries returned several relevant articles discussing chromatin modifiers, chromatin accessibility, and the epigenetic or open chromatin landscape, but none with the direct phrase "dysregulated chromatin modifiers." I then focused on combinations like "chromatin modifiers chromatin accessibility" and explicitly "dysregulated chromatin modifiers chromatin accessibility" to refine the search to perturbations. This led me to articles studying mutated or dysregulated chromatin modifiers (such as EZH2) and their impact on chromatin accessibility or open chromatin states using functional genomics assays (e.g., ATAC-Seq).

## "Integrative Multi-Omics Analysis of Oncogenic EZH2 Mutants: From Epigenetic Reprogramming to Molecular Signatures" (PMID: 37511137)
**Supporting sentence from the full text:**  
"In EZH2 GOF mutants, the mutation of either Y641F or A677G caused an overall increase in H3K27me3 deposition on promoter genomic regions with a consequential decrease in chromatin accessibility and transcriptional levels."  
And further:  
"In this study, the role of EZH2 GOF and LOF mutations in isogenic cells was interrogated under a comprehensive multi-omics approach by integrating global histone PTM analysis via EpiProfile, with mapping of H3K27me3 deposition across the genome using Cleavage Under Targets and Tagmentation (CUT&Tag), chromatin accessibility via Assay for Transposase-Accessible Chromatin with sequencing (ATAC-Seq), RNA-Seq, label-free bottom-up proteomics, and an MS-based untargeted metabolomics assay. An extensive multi-omics overview of how EZH2 mutations alter cellular epigenetics, gene expression and downstream protein and metabolite levels is provided herein."

The data show that **dysregulated chromatin modifiers (EZH2 mutations, both GOF and LOF), can induce changes in the chromatin accessibility landscape (open/closed chromatin), altering the epigenetic status and accessibility.**
