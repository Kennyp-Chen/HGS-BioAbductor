 • Abstract 1: In hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), SF3B1 is overexpressed at the RNA and protein levels. Its increased expression is linked with tumor aggressiveness, the upregulation of oncogenic splice variants (e.g., KLF6‐SV1, BCL‐XL), and decreased overall survival. In vitro and in vivo inhibition (by silencing or pladienolide‐B treatment) of SF3B1 reduces liver cancer cell aggressiveness.
 • Abstract 2: Liver injury and HCC are accompanied by an altered expression profile of several splicing factors (e.g., SRSF3, PSF, SRSF6), with SF dysregulation occurring early in liver damage. In human HCC, certain splicing factor levels (including those considered tumor suppressive) are upregulated, and some alternative splicing events correlate with clinical outcomes.
 • Abstract 3: A wide range of spliceosomal components are dysregulated in HCC. Specific factors (e.g., EIF4A3) are associated with poorer survival and increased tumor aggressiveness by altering the splicing of key genes (e.g., FGFR4). Alterations in spliceosomal machinery appear to be central to tumor behavior.
