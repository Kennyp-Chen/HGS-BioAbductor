1. Hypothesis Summary:
The hypothesis posits that elevated levels of HDAC1 (histone deacetylase 1) directly modify chromatin structure through increased histone deacetylation and, importantly, indirectly promote viral DNA integration into the host genome. This indirect effect is proposed to occur through HDAC1's interactions with genes such as p53 and MYC, which are known to regulate DNA repair enzymes and other factors that facilitate viral integration. The increased viral integration then fosters genomic instability, thereby contributing to cancer-promoting processes like metastasis and angiogenesis in liver neoplasms. Conversely, reduced HDAC1 expression is hypothesized to downregulate these interactions, lowering the activation of the viral integration pathway and potentially decelerating cancer progression.
