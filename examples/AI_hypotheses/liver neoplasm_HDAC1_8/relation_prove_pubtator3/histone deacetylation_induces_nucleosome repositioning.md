# Search Strategy and Reasoning
I searched PubMed using the paired minimal keywords "histone deacetylation" and "nucleosome repositioning" as well as the synonym "nucleosome remodeling." The goal was to identify whether histone deacetylation can be shown to induce (directly or indirectly) nucleosome repositioning/remodeling. In my search, I found a number of studies discussing the interplay between histone deacetylation (or hypoacetylation) and the action or effect of chromatin remodelers on nucleosome positioning. Upon examining the abstracts, the paper with PMID: 32375040 ('Fine Chromatin-Driven Mechanism of Transcription Interference by Antisense Noncoding Transcription') described experimental evidence linking histone deacetylation to the repositioning (sliding) of nucleosomes. I therefore proceeded to the full text to verify whether the evidence meets the "induces" criterion.

# literature evidence
## "Fine Chromatin-Driven Mechanism of Transcription Interference by Antisense Noncoding Transcription" (PMID: 32375040)
Exact sentence from the abstract:
"Reduced acetylation results in the decreased binding of the RSC chromatin remodeler at -1/+1 nucleosomes and subsequent sliding into the nucleosome-depleted region hindering pre-initiation complex association."

This sentence supports a mechanistic link: histone deacetylation (reduced acetylation) induces changes in nucleosome positioning by diminishing chromatin remodeler binding and causing nucleosomes to slide into regions they do not normally occupy.

Answer: True
