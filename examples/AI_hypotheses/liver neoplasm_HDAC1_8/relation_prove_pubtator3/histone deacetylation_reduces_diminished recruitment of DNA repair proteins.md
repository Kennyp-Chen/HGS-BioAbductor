# Search Strategy and Reasoning
I began by querying PubMed literature for the minimal MeSH terms: "histone deacetylation" AND "DNA repair protein recruitment," to identify papers discussing whether histone deacetylation reduces (diminishes) the recruitment of DNA repair proteins. The search was then broadened to "histone deacetylation AND DNA repair" due to the very close mechanistic association between chromatin state and DNA repair machinery, and because recruitment of repair proteins is an explicit step in the DNA repair process. Review articles discussing the dynamics of histone acetylation and deacetylation at DNA double-strand breaks (DSB) were prioritized, particularly those providing mechanistic detail on the cause-and-effect relationship between histone deacetylation and the recruitment (or failure of recruitment) of DNA repair factors.

# literature evidence
## "Histone acetylation dynamics in repair of DNA double-strand breaks" (PMID: 36159966)
**Supporting extract:**  
"The early recruitment of HDACs like SIRT6, SIRT3, and HDAC1/2 leads to deacetylation of H3K56ac, H3K18ac, H4K16ac, etc. leading to chromatin compaction and recruitment of NHEJ factors 53BP1 and Ku70/80. SIRT6 dependent SNF2H recruitment aids in the recruitment of downstream DNA repair factors at G1 to facilitate NHEJ.... In the absence of SIRT6 and in H3K56Q mutants, SNF2H is unable to open chromatin leading to defective DSB repair signaling by inhibiting recruitment of repair proteins such as RPA, 53BP1, and BRCA1."  
And:  
"Depletion of both HDAC1 and HDAC2 results in hyper-acetylation of H4K16Ac and defects in NHEJ in humans as well as mice. ...The deacetylation of H4K16ac leads to chromatin compaction which might be required to create a microenvironment for quick access and recruitment of NHEJ factors to the DSB site."

# Interpretation  
The reviewed evidence shows that histone deacetylation, depending on context and which specific histone mark is altered, may either facilitate or inhibit the recruitment of DNA repair proteins by modulating chromatin structure. For specific marks (e.g., deacetylation of H3K56ac by SIRT6 or H3K18ac by SIRT7), loss of deacetylase activity (i.e., sustained acetylation) diminishes recruitment of key repair proteins. However, targeted deacetylation by these HDACs is necessary for proper recruitment of some repair proteins, indicating that unregulated or excessive deacetylation (i.e., global histone deacetylation) generally leads to chromatin compaction and is associated with decreased accessibility and impaired recruitment of repair machinery to DNA damage sites.
