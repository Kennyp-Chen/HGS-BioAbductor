1. Hypothesis Summary:
The core idea of the hypothesis is that, in liver neoplasms, overexpression of the metabolic enzyme PFKFB2 not only changes metabolic flux but also actively promotes the geranylgeranylation process. This is proposed to occur through either upregulation or direct interaction with geranylgeranyltransferases. As a result, an increase in the geranylgeranylation of key signaling proteins—specifically small GTPases—would intensify metastasis- and angiogenesis-related signaling pathways. The hypothesis further outlines an approach that employs advanced gene-editing (e.g., CRISPR), along with proteomic, metabolomic, and functional assays to determine if PFKFB2 interacts with or regulates these enzymes and if additional metabolic regulators act synergistically to enhance tumor aggressiveness and worsen clinical outcomes.

2. Originality Assessment:
• This hypothesis integrates aspects of metabolic reprogramming in cancer with post-translational modification mechanisms, particularly geranylgeranylation.  
• While cancer metabolism and the role of metabolic enzymes like PFKFB family members have been explored extensively, linking a glycolysis regulator (PFKFB2) to the regulation of protein geranylgeranylation is not a common notion in the literature.  
• The suggestion that PFKFB2 may either physically interact with or transcriptionally regulate geranylgeranyltransferases offers an innovative perspective by bridging metabolic control with direct modulation of protein modifications involved in metastasis and angiogenesis.  
• Overall, the hypothesis distinguishes itself by proposing a dual mechanism (metabolic flux alteration and direct enzymatic regulation) that could reveal novel insights into liver tumor biology.
