1. Hypothesis Summary:
──────────────────────────
The hypothesis posits that in liver neoplasm cells, overexpression of the glycolytic enzyme regulator PFKFB2 works in conjunction with other canonical glycolytic regulators—specifically PGK1 and a ZEB1-induced isoform of PFKM—to amplify glycolytic flux. This enhanced glycolysis is theorized to create a metabolic environment that indirectly alters HMGCR (3-hydroxy-3-methylglutaryl-CoA reductase) activity, leading to increased production of geranylgeranyl pyrophosphate (GGPP). Elevated GGPP then promotes enhanced geranylgeranylation of proteins, which in turn activates small GTPases. The activation of these GTPases drives cytoskeletal remodeling, resulting in increased cell motility and ultimately contributes to heightened invasiveness and metastasis of liver tumors. The hypothesis also outlines an experimental plan using CRISPR/Cas9-mediated genetic modulation of PFKFB2, stable isotope-based metabolic flux analysis, proteomic assessments of protein geranylgeranylation, and live-cell imaging to validate the proposed mechanism.
