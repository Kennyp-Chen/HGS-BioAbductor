The biological entities "elevated NADPH levels" and "HMGCR activity" were reduced to their minimal MeSH/bioconcept search terms: NADPH (as @CHEMICAL_NADP) and HMGCR (as @GENE_HMGCR). I then queried PubMed/PubTator for publications linking these two entities. Abstracts were reviewed for any semantic or direct mechanistic relationship where elevated NADPH levels could be seen to modulate (increase or decrease) the activity of HMGCR. Since the core of the query is whether NADPH level changes modulate (not merely participate in) HMGCR activity, full texts were consulted for top hits that mentioned both entities in a regulatory/feedback relationship. The focus was to find experimental evidence where altering NADPH levels directly impacts or modulates HMGCR's activity (positively or negatively).

# literature evidence

## "A positive feedback between cholesterol synthesis and the pentose phosphate pathway rather than glycolysis promotes hepatocellular carcinoma" (PMID: 37596320)
**Supporting sentence from full text:**  
"NADPH, as a cofactor of HMGCR, is required for cholesterol synthesis. These established findings led us to speculate that the PPP is potentially involved in enhanced cholesterol synthesis in HCC. ... To test this speculation, we treated liver homogenates of pT3 and c-Myc mice with NADP. As expected, NADP treatment significantly increased enzyme activity of G6PD (Supplementary Fig. 4B), which in turn increased incorporation of 14C-acetate into cholesterol in liver homogenates from pT3 and c-Myc mice (Fig. 3E). ... To confirm this speculation, we ablated the PPP via knocking down G6pd in hepatocytes of c-Myc mice ... Consistent with reduced NADPH that is required for cholesterol synthesis, incorporation of 14C-acetate into cholesterol was also significantly reduced in liver homogenates of c-Myc/shG6pd mice (Fig. 3H)."  
**Interpretation:** These findings show that increased NADPH (produced by the PPP) enhances cholesterol synthesis via its central role as HMGCR's cofactor, i.e., modulating HMGCR activity. Decreased NADPH reduces cholesterol biosynthesis, evidencing a functional/causal modulation.

## "A HIF independent oxygen-sensitive pathway for controlling cholesterol synthesis" (PMID: 37558666)
**Supporting sentence from full text:**  
"Each oxygen-dependent step requires reduced nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide phosphate (NADPH) as a cofactor, and therefore oxygen availability and cellular redox impact on cholesterol production. ... Hypoxia promotes a relative accumulation of NADPH, which acts as a ligand for MARCHF6, increasing its activity and promoting SREBP2 degradation. This oxygen and NADPH-sensitive response is co-ordinated with the HIF pathway to shutdown cholesterol synthesis and switch to cholesterol uptake when oxygen is scarce."  
**Interpretation:** This study reveals a regulatory circuit where elevated NADPH triggers downstream signaling (via MARCHF6 and SREBP2 degradation) leading to decreased expression and protein-level stability of HMGCR, thus *negatively* modulating its activity by shutting down cholesterol synthesis.

# Conclusion
The literature provides direct experimental and mechanistic evidence that NADPH levels modulate HMGCR activity—both as a required cofactor enabling its activity (increased NADPH supports activity) and via contributing to feedback loops (excess NADPH can indirectly suppress HMGCR by enhancing degradation signals under certain conditions).

Answer: True
