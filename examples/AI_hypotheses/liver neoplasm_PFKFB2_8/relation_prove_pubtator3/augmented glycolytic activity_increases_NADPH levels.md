An explicit demonstration of flux from glycolysis to the pentose phosphate pathway (PPP), which generates NADPH, was especially relevant.

# literature evidence
## "Tyr-Asp inhibition of glyceraldehyde 3-phosphate dehydrogenase affects plant redox metabolism" PMID: 34156108
From the results section of the full text:  
"Tyr-Asp inhibition of GAPC activity is associated with the shift of the glycolytic flux toward the PPP and increased NADPH/NADP+ ratio... a significant increase in the deduced NADPH/NADP+ ratio (Fig 1G), but an unchanged NADH/NAD+ ratio (Fig 1H)."  

And from the Discussion:  
"Inhibition of the GAPC activity redirects the glycolytic pathway toward PPP, resulting in the production of NADPH (Ralser et al.). While oxidative inactivation of GAPC is a fast event (Ralser et al.; Zaffagnini et al.), we speculate that Tyr-Asp inhibition becomes critical under prolonged stress to maintain high levels of NADPH production."  

Thus, upregulating glycolytic flux (or its inhibition at specific steps) can redirect metabolites into the PPP, leading to increased NADPH production.
