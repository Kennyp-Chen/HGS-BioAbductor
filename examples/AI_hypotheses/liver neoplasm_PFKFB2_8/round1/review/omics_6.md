1. Hypothesis Summary

The hypothesis posits that in liver neoplasm, the overexpression of PFKFB2 (an enzyme involved in regulating glycolytic flux) does more than merely alter metabolic flux. It is suggested that PFKFB2 interacts with the protein geranylgeranylation machinery by upregulating or enhancing the activity of geranylgeranyltransferases and related enzymes. This, in turn, would lead to increased post-translational modification (geranylgeranylation) of key signaling proteins, including small GTPases, which are known to facilitate metastasis and angiogenesis. Additionally, the hypothesis proposes that other metabolic regulators interact with PFKFB2 to modulate this modification process, and that the combined dysregulation of these pathways contributes to a worsened prognosis in liver neoplasm.
