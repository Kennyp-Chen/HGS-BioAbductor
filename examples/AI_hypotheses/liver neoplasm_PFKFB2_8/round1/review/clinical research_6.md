1. Hypothesis Summary  
─────────────────────────────  
The hypothesis proposes that in liver neoplasms the enzyme regulator PFKFB2 is overexpressed, which not only shifts metabolic flux but also interacts with the protein geranylgeranylation machinery. More specifically, it speculates that PFKFB2 enhances the activity or expression of geranylgeranyltransferases and related enzymes, thereby increasing the post‐translational modification of key signaling proteins, such as small GTPases. This modification is hypothesized to promote processes critical for cancer progression, including metastasis and angiogenesis. In addition, the hypothesis suggests that other metabolic regulators may interact with PFKFB2 to fine-tune this process, collectively leading to a worse prognosis in liver neoplasm.

2. Originality Assessment  
─────────────────────────────  
• The idea of metabolic reprogramming in cancer is well established; however, associating PFKFB2—a key regulator of glycolysis—with the modulation of protein geranylgeranylation is not a common or mainstream concept in liver neoplasm research.  
• The proposition that a metabolic regulator directly influences a post‐translational modification pathway (geranylgeranylation) presents an innovative perspective that bridges metabolic dysregulation with altered signal transduction mechanisms.  
• Although some studies have looked at the interaction between metabolism and protein modifications broadly, the specific link between PFKFB2 overexpression and enhanced activity of geranylgeranyltransferases appears to be a novel insight deserving further experimental exploration.

While the proposal is conceptually coherent, the mechanistic pathways underlying this interaction are not well detailed. For example, it is unclear whether PFKFB2 exerts this effect through direct protein–protein interaction, transcriptional regulation of geranylgeranyltransferases, or through an indirect metabolic feedback mechanism.  
   
• Evidentiary Basis:

However, the hypothesis currently lacks direct experimental evidence or literature references to underpin the specific connection between PFKFB2 and geranylgeranylation.
