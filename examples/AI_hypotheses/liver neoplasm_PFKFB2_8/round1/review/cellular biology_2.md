The hypothesis proposes that in liver neoplasms, overexpression of the glycolytic enzyme regulator PFKFB2 boosts cellular metabolism. This metabolic surge is suggested to raise the activity of enzymes involved in protein geranylgeranylation—a lipid modification process essential for the activation of small GTPases. These activated GTPases are known to promote metastasis and angiogenesis. Additionally, the hypothesis posits that PFKFB2 interacts with other metabolic regulators, particularly genes in the mevalonate pathway, creating a regulatory network that modulates the availability of substrates necessary for protein geranylgeranylation. In combination, the coordinated actions of these genes are proposed to modulate the prenylation apparatus ultimately influencing cancer progression and patient prognosis.
