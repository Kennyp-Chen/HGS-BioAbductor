1. Hypothesis Summary  
─────────────────────────────  
The hypothesis proposes that in liver neoplasms, overexpression of the metabolic enzyme PFKFB2 enhances cellular metabolism. This metabolic upregulation, in turn, increases the activity of enzymes involved in protein geranylgeranylation—the process that attaches lipid groups to proteins. By promoting the prenylation (a lipid modification) and subsequent activation of small GTPases, this mechanism drives metastatic behavior and angiogenesis. Additionally, it is proposed that PFKFB2 interacts with other metabolic regulators, including key genes in the mevalonate pathway, thereby forming a regulatory network. This network is thought to modulate the availability of substrates necessary for geranylgeranylation, ultimately influencing whether this protein modification pathway is activated or inhibited, with significant implications for cancer progression and patient prognosis.
