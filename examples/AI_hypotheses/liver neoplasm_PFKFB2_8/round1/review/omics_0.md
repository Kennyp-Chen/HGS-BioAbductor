1. Hypothesis Summary

The hypothesis proposes that in liver neoplasms, overexpression of PFKFB2 triggers a metabolic shift that not only meets the heightened energy demands of rapidly proliferating cancer cells but also enhances protein geranylgeranylation. Specifically, it suggests that PFKFB2 modulates the activity of enzymes involved in the attachment of prenyl groups—key among these are HMGCR and GGTases. This modulation supposedly leads to increased prenylation of critical signaling proteins, such as small GTPases, which then promotes processes like cell migration, angiogenesis, and metastasis. Ultimately, these changes contribute to a more aggressive tumor phenotype and poorer clinical outcomes.

2. Originality Assessment

The proposed connection between a glycolytic enzyme (PFKFB2) and protein post-translational modifications (specifically geranylgeranylation) in liver cancer represents an innovative perspective. Although metabolic reprogramming in cancer, including glycolysis upregulation, is a well‐established concept (the Warburg effect), linking PFKFB2 directly to the modulation of geranylgeranylation through HMGCR and GGTases is relatively novel. By bridging energy metabolism with post-translational modifications, the hypothesis offers a fresh angle that could enrich our understanding of metastatic processes in liver neoplasms if supported by mechanistic evidence.
