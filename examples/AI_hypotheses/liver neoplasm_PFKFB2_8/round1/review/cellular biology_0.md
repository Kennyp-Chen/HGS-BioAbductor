1. Hypothesis Summary:
 The hypothesis proposes that in liver neoplasm, overexpression of the glycolysis-regulating enzyme PFKFB2 not only meets the energetic demands required for rapid cell proliferation but also drives a metabolic shift that enhances protein geranylgeranylation. Specifically, it suggests that increased PFKFB2 levels modulate the activity of key enzymes (such as HMGCR and GGTases) responsible for prenyl group attachment. This, in turn, promotes the prenylation of signaling proteins—especially small GTPases—which is theorized to increase cell migration, angiogenesis, and metastasis, ultimately leading to a more aggressive tumor phenotype and worse clinical outcomes.

2. Originality Assessment:
 This hypothesis offers an innovative perspective by linking metabolic reprogramming (via PFKFB2 upregulation) directly to post-translational modifications (protein geranylgeranylation) that influence tumor aggressiveness. Although metabolic changes in cancer and the importance of prenylation in cancer signaling are well-documented, the specific connection between PFKFB2 and the upregulation of protein geranylgeranylation through interactions with HMGCR and GGTases appears to be novel. There is a creative bridging of glycolytic regulation with lipid-related post-translational modifications, which distinguishes this hypothesis from more traditional views that treat these processes separately.

The idea that increased glycolysis could facilitate other metabolic adaptations (such as the synthesis of prenyl groups necessary for protein modifications) is conceptually sound.  
  – However, the specific mechanistic link—i.e., that PFKFB2 directly or indirectly modulates the activity of enzymes like HMGCR and GGTases—remains a critical gap. The pathway by which PFKFB2 influences prenylation, beyond the general increase in metabolic flux, requires further clarification.

  – While upregulation of glycolytic enzymes (including PFKFB family members) in cancer is well-supported, the literature directly connecting PFKFB2 to the regulation of protein prenylation is sparse or underdeveloped.

  – Overall, the hypothetical connection, while logically plausible, would benefit from direct experimental evidence linking PFKFB2 expression changes to the modulation of prenylation enzymes and the resultant cellular phenotypes.
